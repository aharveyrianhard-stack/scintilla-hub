TSMC, third quarter 2026: beat, raised, and building faster

Call held Thursday 15 Oct 2026 at 14:00 Taipei = 02:00 New York. Transcript arrived 10:33 ET. Numbers checked against TSMC's own Q3 report and slides. Stock at 10:35 ET. Trends section added 21:00 ET.

The read in four lines

  1. Beat and raised. Revenue was $46.7B, above the top of TSMC's own range ($44.6–45.8B). The full year is now about +44% in dollars, up from a little over 40% in July. Q4 is guided to $52.3–54.3B, about +14% on the quarter and +58% on the year.
  2. Margins held through the 2nm ramp. Gross margin was 66.4% (guided 65–67) and operating margin 58.7%, above the top of its 56–58 range. Q4 gross margin is guided to 66% at the midpoint, only 0.4 points lower. The overseas fab drag is now closer to 2 points; they still expect it to widen to 3–4 points later.
  3. Demand still runs ahead of supply, and they're building at a pace they've never done before. About 20 fabs are in progress. 2nm capacity more than doubles in 2027. They are adding 3nm capacity, breaking their habit of freezing a node once it's built. On AI chip growth, previously mid-to-high 50s a year to 2029, C.C. Wei said: "It probably will be higher."
  4. The stock went the other way. It traded −3.6% at $414.52 at 10:35, as the whole AI group sold off on a hot inflation reading. It is under its 50-day (~$434) and about 5% above its 200-day (~$394).

Where the money came from

By end use, AI and data-centre chips (HPC) are 68% of revenue, up 22% on the quarter. Phones are 21% (+10%), IoT 4% (+5%), cars 4% (+23%), consumer electronics 1% (−12%). North American customers are 79% of revenue. Cash and securities are about $126B. Q3 capex was $13.8B. Free cash flow was NT$477B, up NT$189B on the quarter.

What management said, prepared remarks

Demand and AI

  • Leading-edge demand is extremely strong and still exceeds what they can supply.
  • AI use is broadening, which pushes up demand for leading-edge chips. Two drivers: the move from chatbots to AI assistants (agentic AI), and wider use of open-source models.
  • The extra demand comes from AI accelerators, from new CPUs for agentic AI, and from networking chips.
  • New this quarter: TSMC sent its own people to check US data-centre build-outs and power supply. The verdict: fine so far, and the cloud companies are well prepared.

Capacity

  • Close to 20 fabs are in progress worldwide: 13 leading-edge and advanced-packaging fabs in Taiwan over the next several years, plus 5–6 overseas.
  • 2nm: in volume since Q4 2025 with good yield, ramping in Hsinchu and Kaohsiung. The 2nm family's yearly capacity more than doubles in 2027, for both phones and AI.
  • A14 (the next node): risk production in 2027, volume in 2028. More customers are engaged at this stage than were on 2nm, so they expect it to be a bigger node.
  • 3nm, a first: they normally stop adding capacity once a node reaches its target, but now add three fabs.
    • A new Tainan fab starts volume in the first half of 2027, plus one more Tainan fab for the first half of 2028.
    • Arizona's second fab is built and tools are moving in; volume starts Q3 2027, a little ahead of plan.
    • Japan's second fab will run 3nm from 2028.
    • They are also converting 5nm tools to 3nm and shifting capacity between 7, 5 and 3nm as needed.

Margins and money

  • The gross-margin dip comes from the 2nm ramp. Overseas fabs now cost them closer to 2 points of margin (July said 2–3), thanks to scale. That widens to 3–4 points in the later part of the five-year plan as more US fabs ramp. They still say they're the lowest-cost maker in every region.
  • Capex keeps rising. Tools are tight, so orders are placed with suppliers well ahead. The dividend stays sustainable and rising, annually and quarterly.
  • Their stance in one line: capture every piece of business possible and leave nothing for rivals.

The Q&A, every analyst

Laura ChenCiti
Asked
How do they square huge AI demand with bottlenecks like power, funding, memory (HBM), packaging and lasers, and avoid overbuilding? And is there an update to the AI growth rate?
Answer
Every one of those factors is in their planning model, and demand still beats supply. On the growth rate: no sign of a slowdown. Mid-to-high 50s doesn't matter; it will probably be higher.
Asked
Will High-NA EUV in 2030 lift productivity, and does heavier capex mean pricing power and higher long-term margins?
Answer
High-NA is a good tool, planned for 2030. There is no ceiling on profitability, but no sudden jump either. Their value comes from making customers succeed.
Gokul HariharanJPMorgan
Asked
Will wafer capacity finally grow more than 10% a year, after a decade below that? Will next year be even tighter?
Answer
Too early for a percentage. Advanced-node additions are what drive it. The gap is still big, and customers keep raising their numbers.
Asked
US expansion: Texas, and the talks with the customer building its own Terafab (SpaceX/Tesla).
Answer
They are evaluating Texas beyond Arizona, and they are talking with that customer about using its Terafab capacity. Nothing is decided. Any such fab must be run by TSMC.
Krish SankarTD Cowen
Asked
Is there a secondary market where one customer passes its wafer allocation to another?
Answer
No. Customers are not allowed to sublet or auction capacity. A few small mask-licensing cases exist, and that's all.
Asked
Rivals will use High-NA before TSMC. Is that a disadvantage?
Answer
No. They adopt a tool when it's mature and the cost makes sense, and they're working with ASML on larger photomasks by 2031. They still offer the most advanced technology.
Charlie ChanMorgan Stanley
Asked
One chipmaker customer that also runs its own fabs (Intel) is expanding and may make more in-house. Does A14 being a bigger node than 2nm assume it outsources server CPUs?
Answer
All customers are treated alike, and their in-house capacity is factored in. A14 interest is already higher than 2nm's was at this stage. No comment on one customer.
Asked
Very large packages (up to 14× reticle) versus Intel's EMIB-T. Would TSMC back up a customer whose rival packaging falls short?
Answer
Only TSMC is producing at 5× today, with excellent yield. 9× and then 14× are being worked on, and their CoWoS packaging remains customers' first choice. And yes, they would step in as the backup.
Sunny LinUBS
Asked
Capex after 2026 against revenue, and does tool inflation push it up?
Answer
No number yet, but 2026–28 will be well above the previous three years. Tool, material and labour inflation is real. Revenue growth should still outpace capex.
Asked
Does the wider 3–4 point overseas drag mean Arizona's third phase is being pulled in?
Answer
They are accelerating everywhere, the US included. The 3–4 points still applies to the later part of the five-year period; that hasn't changed.
Haas LiuBank of America
Asked
Is demand shifting from AI accelerators toward CPUs and networking?
Answer
He won't split it. CPU demand for agentic AI is rising, everything else is robust, and demand is still far above supply.
Asked
Running phone chips and AI chips on one node with two designs, one of them backside power: does that make yields and margins harder?
Answer
It's an advantage, and they were prepared. The learning curve is on track and customers are happy.
Arthur LaiMacquarie
Asked
Has grid power ever made customers cut forecasts? Does it speed up adoption of backside power?
Answer
Power is one factor they check, and US power looks fine so far. Backside power is chosen per product, not because of power shortages.
Asked
Does the AI growth figure include CPUs, and when will it be updated?
Answer
No. It's a narrow, accelerator-only definition, because they can't tell which CPUs go into AI servers. CPUs are business, just not in that number.
Sahej SinghStifel
Asked
Do the reported price increases apply to existing contracts or only to new capacity?
Answer
No direct answer. Pricing is strategic by market, technology and location, and US-made chips are priced differently from Taiwan-made ones. The aim is to have their value recognized.
Asked
The GlobalFoundries interposer deal: when do they buy from specialty foundries rather than make in-house?
Answer
Packaging (back-end) supply is short. The partnership supplies interposers so TSMC's customers get enough capacity.

  • Growth is accelerating, not slowing. Year-on-year revenue growth went 22% → 35% → 36% → 51% over the last four quarters (in NT$; 41% in dollars this quarter, because the NT$ is 7% weaker than a year ago). The Q4 guide implies about +58% in dollars.
  • Margins gained 7–9 points in a year and now sit on a plateau. Gross margin 59.5% → 66.4%, operating 50.6% → 58.7%. The small dips from Q2's peak are the 2nm ramp, exactly as guided; Q4 is guided flat at 66%. Q2's 55.6% net margin carried a one-off gain from selling Vanguard shares; 51% is the clean number.
  • Capex is 30% of revenue and free cash flow still hit a record NT$477B (about $15B) in the quarter. Q4 capex has to be $19–23B to reach the $60–64B budget [ESTIMATE from their figures], so Q4 free cash flow will be thinner. Depreciation is NT$220B a quarter and rises with every fab.
  • The cash pile is NT$4.0 trillion ($126B), about $94B net of debt. Dividends run NT$156B a quarter, a third of this quarter's free cash flow. R&D is 6% of revenue. The tax rate stepped up to 16% from 14% a year ago.

  • No answer on whether price increases reach existing contracts.

  • Nothing specific about Intel or the Terafab terms.
