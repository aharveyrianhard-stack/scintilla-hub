2 · The waste, with numbers

1

Left-overs from the August backfill that nothing touches

4 tables (414,462 rows, 110 MB), 4 database functions and 2 edge functions. Last writes between 10 and 18 Aug 2026. Two readings of the database's own counters ten minutes apart (21:26 and 21:36 UTC) show 0 reads and 0 writes on all four tables. One of the functions has been broken since 28 Sep and nobody noticed, because nobody calls it.

What to do. DELETE DATA - Alan. Proposal with counts: staged/x2/90_DELETE_PROPOSAL__WAITS_FOR_ALAN.sql. One caution: crypto_volume is mostly stock volume, and one function reads it - check nothing on Fly calls that function by hand before dropping.

2

The quote job downloads a year of daily candles every minute for a number that changes once a day

Job 61 downloads 3,150 daily candles a run - 4,536,000 a day in 12,960 Coinbase calls - only to read yesterday's close for 9 coins. Until 28 Sep it also tried to save them and failed every time; that save is gone (0 errors in 299 runs), the download stayed. The same daily candles are fetched again every hour by job 54. Separately, 104 of its 10,080 runs in 7 days (1.03 %) are marked "timeout" although they finished: the job waits 5 seconds for an answer that takes 3 to 4.

What to do. KEEP the job (the Hub and the ribbons read its prices every minute). Timeout: staged 01, safe now. The download: a small code change to the function (read yesterday's close from the daily bars we already store) - not staged here because it is a function deploy, not a schedule.

3

The candle job asks every minute for sizes that are only read every 5 minutes or slower

27 of job 53's 36 Coinbase calls a minute fetch 5-minute, 15-minute and hourly candles. It downloads 20,218 candles to write 286 rows (1.4 %). Of those 286, about 100 are bars that can really still change; the rest are the two previous bars written again as a safety margin [D].

What to do. SAFE TO SLOW, optional: staged 02 moves those three sizes to every 5 minutes (Coinbase calls 51,840 -> 20,736 a day, row writes about 286 -> 104 a minute). Recommendation: do not apply now - it costs no money, the load is already small, and it would make eight crypto bar sizes up to 5 minutes old.

Checked and found clean: repeating errors (none besides the false timeouts above) · duplicate bars (0 across 4,462,506 rows) · stale tables still being refreshed (none - the stale ones are simply idle) · storage buckets (0 crypto files).

4 · Found on the way - not waste, but the crypto Geiger is built from uneven inputs

  • The crypto 3-day bars stop on 8 Aug 2026, the 2-week bars on 29 Jun and the monthly bars on 1 Jun [M]. Nothing has rebuilt them since. The 3-day ribbon job still runs every night (02:34 UTC) and stamps a reading made from those 58-day-old bars with today's date; the 3-day size carries the fourth-heaviest weight in the Geiger (2.58).
  • The Geiger job counts a bar size only during the 3 hours after that size's ribbon job ran. So for the coins the 6-hour reading (weight 3.18) is in for 3 hours of every 6, the 12-hour reading (3.17) for 3 of every 12, the weekly (0.99) for 3 hours a day, and the stale 3-day reading enters from 02:34 to 05:34 UTC. At 21:29 UTC today each coin's Geiger was built from 11 sizes, 8 of which have weight zero: in effect the 3-hour, 4-hour and daily readings only [M].
  • The anon and signed-in roles hold full write rights (insert, update, delete, truncate) on the four left-over crypto tables [M]. That belongs to the access audit (Q4), not to this lane.

None of this is changed here. It is the same family as the H12 finding (old readings filed under today's date) and belongs with the Geiger work.
