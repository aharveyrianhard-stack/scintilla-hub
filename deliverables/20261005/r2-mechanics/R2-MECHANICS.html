1 · The “peers” date in the READ text

What it is. In the Hub company view, the READ text ends with a sentence comparing the name with its cohort. 391 names carry it tonight.

On your screen now

Against its AI_HARDWARE peers it is diverging from the group (compared on the legacy daily composite of 2026-08-24, 42d old). — MU, read from the live table at 20:20 ET

That date is honest today, because the comparison really is built on 24 Aug readings (364 of the 391 sentences say so). But the date was taken from a different table than the one the comparison is made from. Once H12’s fix moves the comparison to the live Geiger, the sentence would still have said “24 Aug” — a fresh comparison wearing an old date.

After the two go in together

Against its AI_HARDWARE peers it is diverging from the group (compared on daily Geiger readings of 2026-10-06, 40m old).

And when one name in the group still has an old reading, the sentence shows the whole spread, because a comparison is only as fresh as its oldest input:

… roughly in line (compared on daily Geiger readings of 2026-08-24 to 2026-10-06, oldest 42d old).

Where the date comes from

From the comparison’s own row: the time of this name’s reading and the time of the oldest reading inside its cohort’s average. H12 added those two fields on 5 Oct; they are empty until H12’s file 04 is applied (checked tonight: 391 rows, 0 filled).

What could be wrong

  • Deployed alone, this changes no sentence: a row with no date keeps today’s wording, word for word. The new wording appears only after file 04 has run.
  • The READ function running live differs from the repository copy by one line — a reference number it reports and never acts on (live carries the older 486-name value, the repository the current 590-name one). The deploy brings it up to date.
  • Tested on the function’s own code with made-up rows (7 tests), not by running the deployed function.

2 · The night watchman and the renamed companies

What it is. Every night a checker compares our stored prices with two outside sources and gives one colour. One of its seven checks is “no name shows an earlier company’s bars”: each ticker has a recorded start date, and nothing older should be served.

What was wrong. Fifteen companies changed ticker. Bank of New York Mellon traded as BK until 20 May 2026 and as BNY since. We deliberately show BK’s history under BNY — one company, one chart. The watchman only knew BNY’s start date (21 May 2026), saw bars from 2003, and called it a violation. Tonight’s real verdict, written at 20:37 ET, is RED for this reason.

The rule now. A bar older than the start date is allowed only when the join list — the same list the chart service uses — says that company traded under the earlier ticker on that day, and the chart service confirms it applied that join. Anything else is still red, and says why by name.

Why you only just heard. The alarm worked and sent 35 messages to the phone channel (the five whose delivery record is still kept were accepted by the notification service). So either the phone is not subscribed to that channel, or the messages were not seen. That cannot be checked from here — see the question at the bottom. Separately, the jobs board had a row for the collector that could never go red, because nothing reported to it.

What is added (staged)

  • The door reports. Every reading passes through one door (ibkr-ingest). After each landing it now ticks the collector’s row on the jobs board. No tick for 15 minutes on a market day between 09:30 and 16:00 → the row reads LATE and the standard message “Scintilla: a job is late or failing” goes out once; three failed landings in a row → FAILING.
  • Working hours on the jobs board. The board judged every job around the clock. IB logs the Gateway out every night, so without hours this row would have paged you nightly. A job can now have hours; every other job is judged exactly as before (156 live jobs × 11 moments compared on a copy: 0 differences).
  • The nightly report says it. The night watchman adds one sentence, for example: Put/call counter: NO minutes for 2026-10-05 - IB Gateway was not answering during the session; newest reading Sun, 04 Oct, 04:39 ET. A missed session cannot be re-collected. It never changes the night’s colour.

Replaying 4–5 Oct on a throw-away database: weekend silence → fine · Monday 09:44 → fine · Monday 09:45 → LATE · after the close → still LATE (no false “back to normal”) · 20:37 → back · overnight logout → fine.
