Proposed exchange — Dispatch & Return

  1. Set up once, by a person. Alan opens the Test Bot conversation (a deep link can do the opening: grokbot://app/v1/agent?id=<its id>) and pastes DISPATCH-0 below. The bot creates a routine with a webhook trigger. Alan copies its Webhook URL and Webhook key to the coordinator once; the key is stored as a Fly secret (keys-on-Fly rule) and is never written to a file or a page. Alan confirms in the bot's Settings that the bot's GitHub connection reaches one repository we name for this purpose only.
  2. Dispatch (what we send). HTTPS POST to the routine's Webhook URL with Authorization: Bearer <key>, sent from Fly. Until the webhook exists: the same text pasted by Alan. Job 1, the X feed: X list 1405188850188759047 (the Trading list the dead collector read), newest first, every 2 hours 06:30 to 23:30 New York time (the collector's own schedule). Job 2, the FinBERT bake-off: the same 300 posts we scored here, so its FinBERT can be marked on the same sheet.
  3. Return (what it sends back). one JSON file per pass committed to branch `grokbot/inbox` of the named repository, path xfeed/<UTC timestamp>.json or bakeoff/<job>.json. A Git commit is dated, signed by its account, replayable and reviewable; nothing of ours is exposed to the bot. Its last line: STATUS · file path + commit · posts in file · newest post time · what was not captured and why
  4. Into our tables. A job of ours (not the bot) reads new files on `grokbot/inbox`. It refuses a file that fails the schema, has non-numeric ids, dates outside the last 10 days, or a newest id older than the last accepted one. Accepted posts go through the existing scripts/xfeed-ingest.mjs path into the published feed (/api/xfeed), tagged source=grokbot, deduplicated on post id against what the collector already holds. The existing sentiment-x-2h job then scores them into x_post_sentiment exactly as before. The bot never writes to a table and never holds a key of ours.

What could be wrong, and what was not done

  • Small answer sheets, one marker. 100 X posts, 100 news rows and 20 videos, labelled by the reviewing model, not by Alan. A gap under about 10 points (20 for videos) is a tie. The order of the top four on X posts could change with Alan's own labels.
  • ADOPT means "best of what was measured, stage it beside what we have". Nothing here was switched on. The word list still scores the Hub.
  • Bearish is the weak spot everywhere. Only 10 of the 100 X labels and 3 of the 20 videos are bearish, and the best transcript method missed all three.
  • Full-article and transcript labels were made from short text (the title + snippet; the passages naming the ticker), so those two tables under-rate methods that read more than the marker did.
  • No Fly machine was started. Fly speeds and costs are estimates from Mac CPU timings; Fly has no GPU machines to price.
  • Grok Bot was not started, messaged or changed. What its webhook accepts, whether it is connected to GitHub, and what its capture holds were not verifiable from this Mac.
  • The X collector was not restored. The restore was rehearsed in a scratch folder only (its 118 tests pass); whether its X sign-in still works is untested.
  • Not run: nomic-embed (needs remote code), Ollama / llama.cpp / whisper.cpp (system installs), Prophet, Kats, tsfresh (judged from their documentation).
PAGE SPECS
  • What this page shows. A review of open-source models for seven Scintilla jobs, each measured on our own data where possible, with a verdict per candidate; how the Grok Bot desktop app can and cannot exchange work with us; and what to do about the dead X-feed collector.
  • Machine. MacBook Pro, Apple M5 Max, 36 GB; encoders timed on CPU with 4 threads, instruct models on Metal through MLX (4-bit). Classifiers timed on CPU with 4 threads; instruct models as 4-bit MLX builds on Metal.
  • X posts. https://scintillahub.ai/api/xfeed (19,479 posts, newest 25 Sep 2026); 7,213 distinct posts carry a cashtag; 300 drawn with random.seed(20261005); the first 100 hand-labelled before any model ran. Rubric: One label per post: the lean a reader would take from the words alone toward the price of the cashtagged name(s) or the market. B = bullish (author says buy/long/breakout/strength, or reports plainly good price-relevant news: upgrade, beat, jump, new high). S = bearish (sell/short/breakdown/weakness, or plainly bad news: downgrade, miss, insider selling, warning). N = neutral: chart or link with no words of direction, promotion, watch-lists, questions, conditional level maps, and posts that say one good and one bad thing about different names. Pictures were not seen. One labeller (the reviewing model), labelled before any model was run. "Opposite calls" = bullish called bearish or the reverse. Score = F1.
  • News. 592,264 rows; title + a snippet of at most 400 characters (average 302). No article body is stored, so "long-form news" in our tables is short text today. fetched from the stored url with trafilatura for this test only: 58 of 100 gave more than 800 characters (median 4,233); the rest are paywalled, video pages or quote lists. Rubric: One label per news row, for the ticker the row is filed under, from the stored title + snippet only: B = plainly good for that name's price (approval, upgrade, buy call, beat, shares up), S = plainly bad (lawsuit, downgrade, closures, shares down, 'will never recover'), N = routine, comparison pieces with no winner stated, 13F fund-holding notices, quote-list pages, mixed, or the story is not about that name. A second flag marks rows where the story is not about the filed ticker at all. One labeller (the reviewing model), labelled before any model was run on news.
  • YouTube. 0 rows: the table exists and is empty. Captions for this test were fetched fresh with youtube-transcript-api (26 of 26 videos answered, about 0.9 s each, from this MacBook). Rubric: One label per video for the ticker the video is filed under: B = the speaker is plainly positive on that name (bought more, bullish deep-dive, 'undervalued', 'remain bullish'), S = plainly negative (sceptical teardown, 'downgraded', 'strong sell'), N = level maps, debates with both sides, day-trade recaps, news round-ups, 'not bearish but sold'. Labelled by one labeller (the reviewing model) from the title plus the passages that mention the name (not from watching the video), before any model ran. One labeller. News: 100 labels (about plus or minus 9 points). Articles: 58, labelled from the title + snippet, not the full text. YouTube: 20 labels made from the passages naming the ticker (about plus or minus 20 points) — the passages-only arm reads the same passages the labeller read, which favours it.
  • Speech-to-text. video _4_lvPcaT_g, 655 s, 16 kHz mono. "word error" compares against the channel's own human captions, which are edited, so about 0.22 is a ceiling on error, not the true error; all sizes land within 2 points of each other. Fly estimate: not measured (no machine was started). Fly lists performance-4x (4 dedicated cores, 8 GB) at about 132 dollars a month = about 0.18 dollars an hour. If a Fly core is 2.5 to 4 times slower than an M5 Max core, faster-whisper small needs 10 to 15 minutes per hour of video = about 3 to 5 cents per hour of video.
  • Embeddings. 500 real reposts from the X feed: the cut-off "RT @x: …" text (first 110 characters) must find its full original among 5,500 posts. Speed on 3,000 real news headlines (CPU, 4 threads, re-timed in a clean process; Metal). pgvector 0.8.0 is offered by our Supabase project and not switched on (pg_available_extensions). Switching it on is an additive change. gte-base and gte-modernbert were unexpectedly slow on CPU here (about 30 headlines a second, twice) although they are the same size as e5-base; on Metal they are fast. nomic-embed-text-v1.5 was not run: it needs trust_remote_code (it executes code from the model repository).
  • Ticker linking. the 100 hand-flagged news rows: 12 are filed under a ticker the story is not about (examples: TGT on "Target Generation", STX on a TJX story, HD on a Devon Energy story, KO on Coca-Cola Europacific Partners, DOW on the Dow Jones index). Several "good rows wrongly rejected" are rows whose stored snippet never names the company (quote-list pages, "2 AI stocks" teasers, oil stories filed under USO), so the flag should mark a row for a lower weight, not delete it.
  • Anomaly. 89 names, the last 250 daily moves each, daily closes from our chart service (read-only). "next_day_move_vs_normal" = the average size of the day after a flag, divided by the average day: above 1 means the flag marked a name that kept moving. data/scintilla-rules.json: 2x the usual day is "about one day in twenty" = 5% of days.

Not on offer. `fly platform vm-sizes` lists only shared-cpu-1x…8x and performance-1x…16x; fly.io/docs/gpus now redirects to the blog post "We Were Wrong About GPUs" (https://fly.io/blog/wrong-about-gpu/); the pricing page lists no GPU price. No Fly machine was started in this run: the only existing app that could host one copies the provider keys into the machine, and installing Python packages next to those keys is a risk not worth a timing number. A copy of /Applications/Grok Bot.app/Contents/Resources/app.asar was unpacked into the scratchpad with `npx @electron/asar` and searched; the app was not started, changed or messaged. The logs show it broke a day earlier and in two steps: first the installed dependency disappeared (27 Sep, between 16:30Z and 18:30Z), then the folder. Putting the folder back is therefore not enough — the dependency has to be installed too. Pattern followed: A scheduled program lives in a fixed install path owned by its service definition, with its dependencies installed from a lock file at deploy time — the launchd.plist(5) ProgramArguments / WorkingDirectory contract and `npm ci` (https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/commands/npm-ci).
