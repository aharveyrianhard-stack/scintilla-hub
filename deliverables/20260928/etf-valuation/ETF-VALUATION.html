Fund valuation

1 · What the funds cost, and what they earn

Fund P/E = the fund's money ÷ the earnings that money owns = Σ weight ÷ Σ (weight × earnings ÷ price).  Two holdings at P/E 10 and 40, half each → 16, not 25 (the plain average of P/Es overstates it).

2 · Arbitrage view — price against NAV, fund against its basket

FMP's NAV carries no date. On 28 Sep it sat on Thursday's close for SPY, IWM and IJR (and most funds FMP refreshed on Friday evening) — one session old — so a "premium" against Friday's close is mostly Friday's market move, not a real premium. Both closes are shown; ◂ nearest marks the one the NAV matches. The working arbitrage reading is the right-hand side: did the fund move more or less than the stocks inside it today? Rows are ordered by that gap, largest first, among funds whose basket is at least 80% priced; foreign-stock funds (basket 0–10% priced while their home markets are shut) sort to the bottom.

3 · In plain words

What this page shows. For IWM, IJR, VB, SPY, QQQ and every fund the Hub carries: what you pay for a dollar of the companies' profits (P/E), how much profit the companies inside earn in total, whether the fund trades above or below the value of what it holds (premium / discount to NAV), and whether the fund moved today by more or less than the stocks inside it.

Does FMP give an ETF P/E directly? No. Checked on 28 Sep: FMP's ratios and key-metrics answers for IWM are empty, and its quote and fund-information answers carry no P/E field. The only funds with an FMP P/E are two commodity trusts that file their own accounts (DBC, SLV) — that is the trust's bookkeeping result, not a valuation of what it holds; every equity fund's "FMP direct" box is empty. FMP does give each fund's holdings with weights and dollar values, its NAV (net asset value per share, with its date), assets under management and expense ratio. So the P/E and total earnings here are computed by us from the holdings and FMP's per-company trailing-twelve-month earnings yield.

Where each number comes from. P/E (all) counts loss-makers' losses — the honest total. P/E (profitable) leaves loss-makers out — what most fund websites print; the gap between the two is the loss-makers' drag. Without 5 worst = the same P/E with the five holdings whose losses weigh most taken out, and biggest drags names them — so one broken company, or one bad FMP number, is visible instead of hidden. Coverage = share of the fund's weight for which FMP has an earnings number. n/m (not meaningful) = the loss-makers' losses are as large as, or larger than, everyone else's profits, so there is no positive earnings figure to divide by — true of IWM today. Earnings yield (all) is then the honest number: the fund's weighted profit per dollar, which can be negative. Total earnings = the sum of every holding company's whole trailing-twelve-month profit (market value × earnings yield), converted to US$ with FMP's exchange rates; a percentage in brackets means some holdings' currency could not be converted. Fund's share = the part of those profits the fund's own money owns (dollars invested in each holding × its earnings yield). Close vs NAV = that day's close ÷ FMP's NAV − 1, for each of the last two closes, because FMP does not say which day its NAV is from. Fund today / basket today = the fund's move against the weighted move of its holdings from Massive's whole-market snapshot at the time in the header.

What could be wrong. Bond, gold and commodity funds have no earnings (shown blank). Funds holding foreign shares (EWJ, FXI, ASHR…) have low coverage because FMP's holdings list those names under local tickers that do not match its company file. FMP's NAV can be a day old; a "premium" computed against a stale NAV is noise. Before 09:30 ET and for foreign stocks, the basket's "today" is yesterday's or a closed market's move, so a gap there is not an arbitrage. Holdings can lag the fund by days.

What was not done. No history of premiums (one run, 28 Sep). No intraday NAV (iNAV) — FMP does not give it; the basket move is our stand-in. Nothing was written to the database.
