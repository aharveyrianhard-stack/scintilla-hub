What the bow tie is telling you

  1. It is the widest bow tie in the record. Both wings are long at once: the shorter wing reads +0.76 (live Hub +0.71), at the 100th percentile of every day since 2007-07-06. The whole width is 1.63 (99th). The other families show the same shape: iShares at the 99th and Vanguard at the 100th percentile. Equal weight has the same shape, with a live wing of +0.75 (too little history for a percentile). "Scarily insane" is a fair description: the measure has only been this wide on 42 earlier days, in 10 separate episodes.
  2. Two sectors are carrying the tape, and the rest are red. Green wing: XLK +0.86, XLV +0.70, XLC +0.21, XLE +0.19. Red wing: XLF -0.32, XLI -0.32, XLP -0.38, XLB -0.39, XLRE -0.59, XLY -0.59, XLU -0.76. Only 36% of sectors are above zero. Equal weight agrees: over the last 16 sessions RSP trailed SPY by 4.3 points, the 1st percentile since 2003. Over 646 sessions it trails by 15.7 points (6th percentile). In plain words, the index is being held up by a narrow set.
  3. The shift you saw is real. In mid-2025, health care (XLV) sat on the red wing and utilities (XLU) on the green. Today they have swapped (chart 3). Compare today's order with 72 sessions ago: the rank correlation is -0.02, meaning the order has been fully reshuffled over a quarter. Compared with 16 sessions ago it is +0.55, so the latest three weeks mostly extended the moves already under way. The fast (1d) and slow (1w) Geiger orders agree at +0.92 (91st percentile). This tie is not a one-day flicker.
  4. After the 10 earlier ties this wide, one thing happened every time: the tie got narrower. 16 sessions later the wing was smaller on 42 of 42 days (average change -0.59). Part of that is simply what extremes do. What SPY did next was not consistent. Some episodes preceded big falls (January 2008, and the late-January 2020 tie before the COVID crash). Others preceded strong rises (February 2016, August 2019, November 2022). Over 16 sessions SPY averaged -0.99% (median +1.13%), against +0.54% on all days. The 90% block-bootstrap interval for that difference runs from -4.42 to +1.81 points, so it includes zero. Ten episodes are too few to call a direction.
  5. What history says about "rotation" in general. At the short (16-session) and medium (72-session) horizons, a sector's relative-strength rank tells you almost nothing about its rank over the next equal period. The rank correlation is -0.004 and -0.008, and a top-third sector lands in the top third next time 36% of the time, near the 33% of a coin (and in the bottom third 35%). The long horizon (646 sessions ≈ 2.6 years) is different: leaders tend to stay leaders (top third stays top third 40% of the time; rank correlation +0.18, interval +0.05 to +0.36). On the rotation map, moves go clockwise (leading → weakening → lagging → improving) 50% of the time and counter-clockwise 41% of the time. The textbook wheel exists, but it is a loose tendency, not a clock.
  6. For allocation, descriptively (not a recommendation): the spread between sectors is wide (short-horizon dispersion 90th percentile), so which sectors a portfolio holds matters more than usual right now. The index's result rests on two sectors (tech and health care), and equal weight is near its weakest against cap weight. Over three weeks and over a quarter, today's sector ranking has had no forecasting value for the next equal period. Over multi-year spans, leaders have tended to persist. On the earlier days as wide as today, the green-wing sectors went on to trail SPY by 2.3 points on average over the next 72 sessions, against 0.7 on all days (90% interval of the difference -3.68 to -0.10). That is weak evidence that a very wide tie gives back a little, not that it flips.

2 · The bow tie, measured

What the Hub shows. COHORT COMPARE → SECTORS draws one centre-zero bar per sector, sorted from most bullish (left) to most bearish (right). The scale stretches so the largest bar touches the edge (index.html cohortCompareStripHTML, cohortCompareSpan). Under the MEMBERS tab each bar is the average Geiger of the sector's member companies. Under SPDR, iSHARES, VANGUARD and EQUAL-WT each bar is the fund's own Geiger (SECT_FAMILY_FUNDS). The live Geiger is a weighted blend of seven timeframes (3h, 4h, 6h, 12h, 1d, 3d, 1w, with Alan's saved Equalizer weights). Each timeframe is half trend (how orderly the 5 → 200 moving-average fan is) and half momentum (RSI 14 and Williams %R).

How it was rebuilt for history. Daily bars can rebuild the 1d, 3d and 1w timeframes exactly. The Hub's own replay maths were used (geiger-replay.mjs), with 3-day and weekly bars grouped exactly as the chart API groups them. The three are weighted as Alan's Equalizer weights them (1d 3.178477, 3d 2.576738, 1w 0.987499). The four intraday timeframes cannot be rebuilt for past years. Checked against today's live values across 46 funds, the three rebuilt timeframes match exactly, and the rebuilt blend tracks the live seven-timeframe blend at correlation 0.986 (rank 0.965, average gap 0.113). A day counts only once every timeframe has its full nine-line fan, which starts the history at 2007-07-06.

The bow tie as a number. The shorter wing is the smaller of (strongest sector's Geiger) and (minus the weakest sector's Geiger). It is large only when both a long green wing and a long red wing exist at the same time. At zero or below, the strip is one-sided. Alongside it: the whole width (best − worst), the knot (how flat the middle bar is on the autoscaled strip), and the share of sectors above zero. Its history, in quantiles: 10th -0.27 · 25th -0.04 · 50th +0.20 · 75th +0.42 · 90th +0.58 · 95th +0.65 · 99th +0.76.

3 · How sectors rotate: three horizons

Horizons, taken from SPY's own swings (the S9 pivot rule: a pivot high is above the N bars before it and at or above the N after, and the reverse for lows; swings alternate):

  • short = 16 sessions: one SPY swing leg under the S9 pivot rule (pivot 10): median 16 sessions.
  • medium = 72 sessions: one SPY swing cycle, low to low, at pivot 20: median 72 sessions (about a quarter).
  • long = 646 sessions: one SPY swing cycle, low to low, at pivot 120 (the multi-year swings): median 646 sessions (about 2.6 years).

The persistence curves fall to about zero at exactly the horizon's own length. That is the key fact. Within a window, ranks are sticky only because consecutive days share most of their history. Once the window has fully turned over, the old ranking carries no information, except at the multi-year horizon. Spell lengths are short at the median because a daily ranking flickers near the tier edges; the p90 and longest columns show the real runs. Neighbouring horizons (sensitivity) give the same answer: 18 sessions -0.002, 38 sessions -0.030; 54 sessions -0.022, 172 sessions +0.045; 261 sessions +0.005, 378 sessions -0.005.

What could be wrong

  • The rebuilt Geiger is three of the seven timeframes. It matches the live 1d, 3d and 1w readings exactly, but it leaves out 3h, 4h, 6h and 12h. Today it tracks the live composite at 0.986, with an average gap of 0.113. Past readings would have carried the same kind of gap.
  • Only 10 earlier episodes. Every "what followed" number rests on them, and two (January 2008, January 2020) sat just before large falls. The intervals are wide on purpose.
  • Price only. Dividends are not included. Over multi-year horizons this understates high-yield sectors (utilities, real estate, staples, energy) against SPY by roughly their yield gap, about 1 to 3 points a year.
  • Sectors were redrawn. Real estate left financials in 2016 (XLRE), and communications was carved out of tech and discretionary in 2018 (XLC). Long-horizon ranks straddle those changes. Dispersion uses the nine original funds for a constant roster.
  • The horizons are a choice. They are the medians of SPY's own swing lengths. Neighbouring choices are shown in the sensitivity line and tell the same story.
  • Overlapping windows. Daily forward returns overlap, which is why the intervals use a block bootstrap with blocks as long as the horizon, not a plain count.
  • "Bow tie" is my definition of what the eye reads on the strip (the shorter wing). Other definitions (the width, the knot) are shown next to it.

What I did not do

  • No change to the Hub, no deploy, no database write, no paid service.
  • The equal-weight funds' history before June 2023 is not included, because the chart API does not serve the old tickers (RYT, RYF, …); they return 404.
  • No MEMBERS-tab history: that would need every member company's history as it stood on each past date.
  • No intraday Geiger timeframes in the history, and no dividend-adjusted returns.
  • No buy or sell calls. The allocation paragraph describes base rates only.
