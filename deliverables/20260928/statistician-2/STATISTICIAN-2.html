How to read it. "RSI at its 1st pct" is the level below which a type spends 1% of its days: that is the level at which it is as unusual as SPY at 28. Bitcoin and the commodities go five points deeper than the index funds before they are equally rare; the S&P since 1928 goes deeper still because 1929–1932 and 1987 are in it. Single stocks of every size sit at or below 28 about twice as often as the index funds, and the spread across single stocks is wide (small caps 0.4 to 4.2% of days), so a stock's RSI should be read against its own ladder, which the RSI ladder study already builds per security.

8 · One consolidated method: a market-state readout, and what earns a place on the statistics tab

"All of this needs to feed ONE consolidated method, like the new comps method — consider everything on the Hub and the reports inventory."

The proposal. The comps method works because every peer is read on the same scale (its own percentile against the field) and the chart labels the marks, not a side column. The market state should be built the same way: six blocks, each one number on its own-history percentile scale, each with a state word, laid out as one row, never summed into a score. Entry rules for a card (the statistics tab's rules, plus the standard): the horizon is a pivot or a swing, the condition is read over its full distribution, the number carries its three companions (how many, how sure, how big) and its status word after a false-discovery check over everything tried in its study, and a named review that is not part of how the finding was chosen. Under those rules, from this page: the RSI-by-type ladder (a description of full histories), the three luck-proof conditions of section 2, the 5-day put/call peak level of section 3, the eight-state 200-day table of section 4, and the leaders' fall-ratio and lag of section 5 earn cards. The yields-and-dollar quadrant, the fear-gauge deciles and the equal-vs-cap extremes are readings for the strip, not cards: their status is leaning or a list.

What the readout is for. Alan's rule from the notes ("after taking profits, move to another good name with lower risk") needs to know the state, not a forecast: section 5 says the leaders fall with the index and give back most of a run at a top, so the readout's TREND and FEAR blocks tell him when the give-back risk is high; section 4 says where being below the 200-day stops being ordinary; section 2 says that at a low, breadth and a spiking VIX are the companions of a quick recovery, and the put/call peak is a companion of the low itself (section 3), not of the wait after it.

The reports inventory. Everything on the Hub that measures the market (the regime page's six questions, the bottoms page, the RSI ladder, the leaders page, the sector rotation page, the market map's Geiger) can publish its one number into the same six blocks; the strip proposal on the regime page already draws seven gauges in this shape and needs only the state words and the percentile scale to become this readout. A one-line "today" from each block, with a link to the study that measured it, is the whole tab. Build size after the regime strip: half a day.

What could be wrong

  • Survivorship in the stock types (section 1). The 355 single stocks are the Hub's names today, typed by today's market cap; a mega cap measured over 2003–2026 was smaller for most of it, and names that failed are absent. The funds, indexes, commodities, bonds and Bitcoin do not have this problem. The shared window from 2013 removes the different-years problem but not this one.
  • Breadth is back-filled from today's names (sections 2 and 5): 325 served names for the fear gauge; the bottoms study's 486 for the conditions at the low. Past breadth reads a little healthier than it was.
  • Swings are known 10 bars late. Every low and every put/call peak here is a confirmed pivot; the base rates measure what was true, not what was visible on the day. The put/call hit window (5 before to 15 after) is a choice; the table shows the base rate for the same window so the comparison is fair, but a different window gives different absolute rates.
  • The pooled 200-day table treats instruments that move together as separate (SPY, DIA, the S&P index, the sectors). The block bootstrap resamples runs within the pooled table, so the ranges are somewhat too narrow; the instrument-years column is the honest size, and the per-state shares of days are dominated by the long index histories.
  • Regimes last for months (section 6): the 63-session blocks widen the ranges but the four quadrants still share a few long macro eras (1971–1982, 2022–2026), which is why none of them clears the luck check. The leaders basket only starts in 2007 and its quarters are fewer still.
  • The leaders are top-20 from 2007, chosen from 41 large names another lane pulled; a name that was top-20 in a year but never on that list is missed. Before 2007 there is no leaders series here.
  • Equal-vs-cap entries are a list (section 7): 10 entries, eight of them since 2024, sharing one market. The per-pair "lows" in the seven-pairs table come from the regime study's own rule (a swing of the ratio's 63-session stretch), not from this page's percentile rule.
  • The fear gauge is one of many possible gauges. Four equal-weighted percentiles were fixed before looking at the outcome (no weights were tuned), but a different set of inputs would give different deciles; the deciles' own false-discovery check is over the 50 cells of this gauge only.
  • Bootstrap draws: 600 per range. Ranges move by a few tenths between runs with a different seed; the status words do not.

What was not done

  • No Hub wiring, no deploy, no database write, no change to any other page. Section 8 is a proposal; the statistics tab prototype and the regime strip are unchanged.
  • No intraday data; every reading is a daily close. Put/call is one printed number a day.
  • Section 2 measures the wait to a new high only; the wait to the next swing high (a shorter, pivot-based horizon) and the depth of the bounce were not modelled.
  • Section 5 does not test an entry or exit rule; it describes when the leaders turned against the index and what they gave back. The "move to a calmer name" comparison is on the leaders page.
  • The Python checks the method standard names (arch SPA over the 16 conditions of section 2; statsmodels quantile regression for section 6) were not run; the Node bootstrap and Benjamini–Hochberg are the tests here.
