Put/call audit — is it accurate, and why do some names have it and others not?

Monday 28 September 2026, measured between 09:53 and 10:10 ET during the session · read-only: nothing was restarted, written, deployed or changed · lane PUT/CALL AUDIT

The short answer. The numbers that are produced are added up correctly, and at the close our equity line lands within 1–2% of Cboe's own published equity number. But three things are wrong today:

  1. 21 names stopped being read at 00:12 ET last night (TTWO through WBD, alphabetically in one block: TXN, UBER, UNH, V, VZ, VST, VRT…). The IB Gateway blinked at that moment; the reader kept running but never picked those 21 back up. Every other name is fresh to the second.
  2. 122 of the 486 names on the Hub are not on the reader's list at all — the list was frozen at 364 names on 24 September. 33 of them are companies with busy options (RIOT, GLW, COHR, KTOS, TECK, LUNR, ALB…); 89 are funds.
  3. The line called "index" is not index options. It is SPY + QQQ + IWM (funds). Cboe's index number it is compared with is mostly SPX and VIX options, which the reader never reads.
  4. The cohort lines switch between two member lists. In 2 of the 51 stored minutes since 09:31 (10:04 and 10:06 ET), 53 of the 119 cohorts were built from a shorter list — AI_HARDWARE 35 names instead of 43, BLUE_CHIP 106 instead of 128 — so the ratio jumps down and back. On 24 and 25 Sep, 70 of 119 cohorts did the same.

And to the question "some tickers have put/call, others not": no screen on the Station or the Hub shows a single ticker's put/call today. The readings exist per name in the database, but only the group lines (equity, index, sectors, cohorts) and Cboe's daily numbers are served and drawn. The gaps you would notice are in those group lines: the 21 silent names, and cohorts that jump when they are built from the shorter member list.

The sector list comes from company_profile, which has 440 rows, fits in one page, and never switches. That fits, but I have not reproduced a bad read.

3 · Is it accurate?

3.1 Are the sums right? Yes.

I rebuilt the equity line from the raw IBKR readings for the first 33 stored minutes since 09:30 (each company's newest reading, not older than 15 minutes; add the puts, add the calls, divide once). With one fixed cut-off a few seconds into each minute, 22 of 33 minutes checked match to the unit and the median gap is 0.0%; the rest differ only because the minute job starts at a slightly different second each time. The running totals never fell inside the session, on any name or any line. [VERIFIED]

Two small wrinkles: the 09:30 point was computed in the first seconds of the open, when only 83 of 316 companies had been read, and it is never recomputed — the first point of the day's chart is built on a quarter of the names. And Realty Income (O) is flagged as a fund in the company table, so it is left out of the equity and Real Estate lines.

3.2 Does it agree with Cboe at the close? Equity: yes, within 1–2%.

24 Sep: equity 0.544 vs Cboe 0.55; 25 Sep: 0.509 vs 0.52. The "all 364" estimate runs 5–6% under Cboe's total (0.762 vs 0.80; 0.704 vs 0.75). The index pair is not the same product.

Cboe's daily prints (PCC, PCCE, PCCI) count only trades on Cboe's own exchanges, but every stock; we count every exchange but only our names. Agreement within 1–2% on the equity line is as good as that comparison can be. The database's own comparison table has only 24 Sep filled: 25 Sep still says "no Cboe print" because the 08:45 ET catch-up job builds a broken address (see fixes). Cboe's 25 Sep print is on the chart API.

How the two feeds are lined up. Cboe's delayed chain carries every exchange's volume, not just Cboe's: IBKR's running call total reaches Cboe’s call number 12.1–14.2 minutes before Cboe’s stamp for 13 of the 16 checks, the same delay for names fetched seconds apart. (If the chain were Cboe-only, its totals would be a fraction of IBKR's and would match readings from minutes after the open.) So I take IBKR's running totals at the moment its calls equal Cboe's calls, and compare the puts. The shared delay of a check is the median of the eight names' own delays (13.2 min at 09:55, 12.7 min at 10:07). A name whose own delay is more than 1 minute — two IBKR readings — from that is marked "not aligned": at the shared delay its call totals already disagree with Cboe's, so a put gap would mix timing with counting. Every cell above is generated from putcall-audit.json by align-names.mjs, and a test pins them.

What it shows. With the calls matched, IBKR counts 7–15% fewer puts than Cboe on AAPL, NVDA and MSFT at both checks, and on AMZN at the first. Their single-name ratios therefore look more bullish than Cboe's chain says. The index funds do not show one direction: SPY +1% then −8%, QQQ +7% then −3%. IWM is not aligned at either check (own delay 11.0 and 9.2 min), and AMZN (10.9 min) and TSLA (13.7 min, 1.06 min from the shared delay — on the edge) are not aligned at 10:07. For those, IBKR's and Cboe's call totals disagree, so the comparison cannot say anything about their puts. [MEASURED differences] [THEORY cause] — I cannot tell yet whether IBKR's per-underlying put total leaves some trades out, or Cboe's chain counts some (for example spread legs) differently. This matters only if single-ticker put/call is ever put on screen; the equity line's close-of-day match says the sum over 316 names is sound.

5 · What to fix, smallest first

  1. Restart the put/call reader (one command on this Mac: launchctl kickstart -k gui/501/com.scintilla.ibkr-putcall). It re-subscribes everything, so the 21 silent names come back within a minute. coordinator · 🟡 reversible
  2. Pin the cohort membership to one read: find why the minute job sometimes builds cohorts from a shorter member list (53 of 119 cohorts at 10:04 and 10:06 ET today). First thing to try: read ticker_cohorts in a fixed order (order by ticker, cohort) when paging, and refuse a run whose total member count differs from the last good run.
  3. Make the reader exit with a failure code when it loses the Gateway. launchd only restarts it after a failed exit (KeepAlive: SuccessfulExit false, at most once every 120 s). Today, when the link to the Gateway drops, the sweep loop ends and the reader exits with code 0 — "success" — so launchd leaves it dead, and the health file only says "reader stopped". [THEORY from code + config] Change: return 1 when the loop ended because the connection dropped. Then add: when the same names come back empty for, say, 5 sweeps in a row — or right after a "connectivity restored" message — exit with code 1 too. About ten lines in option-volume.py.
  4. Fix the 08:45 ET catch-up job (putcall-vs-cboe-morning). Its address glues a date onto the word "previous" (session=previous2026-09-25), so today it failed with "Invalid time value" and 25 Sep's comparison with Cboe never filled in. Use either session=previous or session=<date>, not both.
  5. Refresh the reader's list to the 486 live names (and keep it tied to the chart API's /universe). About 6 batches instead of 4: a pass takes ~50 s instead of ~40 s.
  6. Make the "index" line honest: either add SPX and VIX to the reader's list (the reader already knows how to ask for them) so SCPCI is real index options, or rename it "index funds (SPY, QQQ, IWM)" and stop comparing it with Cboe's index number.
  7. Count the real membership (only after fix 2): add the newly admitted names to ticker_cohorts, and let each line's "of" be the group's full membership, not only the names the reader reached, so a cohort with unread members says so.
  8. Add one alarm: during the session, fewer than 95% of the list read in the last 10 minutes → alert.
  9. Small: compute each minute once it has finished (so 09:30 is not built on 83 names); unflag Realty Income (O) as a fund.

6 · What could be wrong in this audit

  • Cboe's free delayed chain has no exact "as of" for its volumes. I line the feeds up on the calls, name by name, and report each name's own delay; the 1-minute tolerance for "aligned" is a judgement (two IBKR readings), and TSLA at 10:07 sits just outside it.
  • The cause of the 21-name silence is inferred from the log and the timing (they were mid-subscription when the link dropped). The silence itself is measured.
  • Single-name comparison covers 8 names at two moments in the first 40 minutes of one session. Fitting a delay per name leaves each comparison with little power: a real put gap and a timing error can look alike.
  • The cause of the cohort switching (unordered paging) is inferred from the code and the table sizes; the switching itself is measured in the minute table.
  • Cboe's daily prints (PCC, PCCE, PCCI) are Cboe-exchanges only; ours are all exchanges, so for the daily prints only the ratios compare, not the amounts. The delayed option chain is different: its amounts match IBKR's all-exchange calls (NVDA 507,625 vs 507,339 at the best match), so it is compared amount for amount.

7 · What I did not do

  • Did not restart the reader, touch the Gateway, edit any cron job, or write to any table — this lane is read-only.
  • Did not check FMP or Massive for per-name option volume: the FMP connector has no option volume, and the Massive key is not entitled to options (answered "not entitled" from the Fly machine).
  • Did not open the Station or Hub in a browser to photograph the panes; the map in section 1 is from the live code (Station dedbc31, Hub d31504a, chart API fcal-2b7e953) and the chart API's live answers.
  • Did not check open-interest numbers (also read), only volumes.
