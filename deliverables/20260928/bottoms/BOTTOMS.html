Bottoms: what happens around the lows, the 200-day as a fact, and the rising tide

Statistics lane · 28 Sep 2026 · daily bars to the 25 Sep close · research questions, not buy rules · every chart is a saved file in charts/ next to this page

STATUS · done for SPY, QQQ and IWM, the 200-day test on 36 instruments, and the tide on all 486 served names. Every swing uses the S9 pivot rule (a low lower than the 10 bars on each side). Every "how extreme" is an own-history percentile — today against every earlier day of the same series, never a trailing window. No fixed look-ahead windows: outcomes run to the next swing or to the close back above the 200-day. Up = green, down = red on every line.

What we found, in plain words

  1. The VIX does not lag the bottom — it turns with it or just before it. At SPY's 126 swing lows since 2003, the VIX's own swing high (same 10-bar pivot rule) came on the same session in 59, before in 42 and after in only 13 (no VIX swing in that stretch: 12). For lows that ended a fall of 10% or more: before or same day in 26 of 28. QQQ and IWM look the same (charts below).
  2. RSI and put/call usually turn FIRST. SPY's RSI(14) made its swing low before the price low in 63 of 126 lows (18 of 28 deep ones) — the momentum low comes first, then price makes a slightly lower low. The put/call ratio (PCC) peaked before the price low in 21 of 28 deep lows. Credit is the slowest and the least reliable: over all 126 SPY lows the HYG÷LQD trough came after the price low in 30 and there was no credit swing at all in 39 (most small dips never reach credit); at the deep lows it turned before or with price in 23 of 28.
  3. Every signal cries wolf — measured. A random SPY day has a 24% chance that a swing low follows within 10 bars. With the VIX at or above its 90th own percentile the chance is 37%; 35% of those VIX episodes were false alarms (no low followed); and 72% of all lows happened WITHOUT it. It is a deep-low detector, not an any-low detector: it was on before 82% of the 10%+ lows.
  4. The best single companions to the VIX are RSI and put/call. RSI at or beyond its 90th stress percentile (a low reading for its own history) was followed by a low on 65% of days (vs 24% any day) and caught every 10%+ low at the 80th–90th level. Equity put/call at its 90th percentile: 52% of days followed by a low. All four together (VIX, put/call, RSI, breadth, each at or beyond its 80th percentile) : 62% of days followed by a low, 32% of episodes false alarms, 32% of lows caught (79% of the 10%+ lows).
  5. VIX above VIX3M (inverted curve) is a yes/no condition, no threshold to choose: 553 SPY days since 2006, 46% followed by a low within 10 bars; 48% of inversion episodes had no low follow; it was on before 42% of all lows but 75% of the 10%+ lows. At the 10%+ lows the inversion was present somewhere in the fall 85.1% of the time.
  6. Deep below the 200-day IS a measured problem — for the fall and the wait, not for the eventual bounce. Pooling 36 instruments (4 index funds, 11 sectors + SMH, the 20 largest US stocks), days in the deepest 5% of their own history below the 200-day went on to fall a further 10.2% (median) before a close back above it (any below-200 day: 3.7%), and took a median 80 sessions to get back (any below-200 day: 32). The link holds in every group (rank link ρ 0.37 indexes, 0.37 sectors, 0.24 stocks; shuffles that keep each series' clustering never produced it). But those same deep days also had the biggest median rise to the next swing high: 8.7% vs 4.6% for any day. Deep = wider range both ways.
  7. The rising tide is real and scales with the day. On a median SPY up day 70% of the 486 names rise; on the biggest 5% of SPY days it is 92% (up) and 93% (down). Over whole SPY swings the tide is stronger: a median 86% of names rose over an up-swing, 100% of the sectors. Dispersion peaks AT lows (median 73rd percentile on the low day; 95th at 10%+ lows) and is ordinary at tops (51st).
  8. Today's tide (25 Sep close) is narrow. SPY closed 0.8% below its highest close, yet only 36% of names are above their 50-day (19th percentile of their own history), only 49% of names are above where they were at SPY's last swing low (2026-07-29), and equal-weight (RSP) has lagged SPY by 7.5 points since that low. Research reading, not a call.

2 · "Deep below the 200-day is a problem" — tested as a fact

Pool: SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA · the 11 SPDR sector funds + SMH · the 20 largest US companies by market cap today (FMP, 28 Sep: NVDA, AAPL, GOOGL, MSFT, AMZN, META, AVGO, TSLA, MU, LLY, BRK-B, AMD, JPM, WMT, V, XOM, JNJ, INTC, MA, ABBV). Daily split-adjusted bars from the chart API. For every day, the distance to the 200-day average is turned into that instrument's own-history percentile (1 = the deepest below it had ever been up to then). Outcomes need no fixed window: the move to the next confirmed swing high, the worst fall before it, and — for days below the 200-day — how much further price fell and how many sessions it took before a close back above the 200-day.

Bands: 90% bootstrap, resampling whole stretches above/below the 200-day (the independent units), 150 draws. Test: within each instrument the percentile series is rotated by a random amount against its outcomes (keeps each series' clustering, breaks the link), 400 times; the table shows the observed rank link, the 95% range from the rotations, and how often a rotation matched it.

What could be wrong

  • Survivorship. The tide and breadth use TODAY's 486 names back-filled to 2003. Names that failed or left are missing, so past breadth reads a little healthier than it was, and the early years carry about 232 names, not 486.
  • QQQ starts in 2011. The cached QQQ history has a gap of more than a year; the S9 rule analyses only what follows it, so QQQ misses 2003–2010 (and 2008).
  • Short histories for the other gauges. Put/call from Nov 2006, VIX3M from Jul 2006, HYG from Apr 2007: lows before those dates count as "no reading", which lowers their "lows caught" share a little.
  • Swings are known 10 bars late. A low is only confirmed 10 sessions after it; the base rates measure what was TRUE, not what was visible on the day.
  • Few deep lows. 28 SPY lows ended a fall of 10% or more; percentages on those carry wide error.
  • Put/call is one printed number a day and noisy; its "turn" uses the same pivot rule on that single number.
  • Overlap in the 200-day pool. SPY, DIA, the sectors and the megacaps move together; the pooled bands resample stretches within each instrument, not across them, so the pooled band is somewhat too narrow. The per-group rows are the safer read.

What was not done

  • No Hub page wiring, no deploy, no database write. The gauges above are proposals with today's readings, not live widgets.
  • No longer history from ^GSPC (1927+) for the swing-low study — the served SPY from 2003 was used, as S9 did.
  • The breadth branch (candidate/breadth-20260927) carries an ingest job but no history yet, so breadth here is computed from the cached bars of the served names.
  • No intraday data; every reading is a daily close.
