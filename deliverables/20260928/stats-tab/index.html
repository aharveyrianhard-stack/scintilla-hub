Rising tide lifts all boats. Every stock study is read in the market's state; the market's own numbers come first.

Does a low SPY RSI mean more when SPY is above its 200-day than below it?

hover a bar for its count and range

Yes, on the record so far. SPY's RSI in its bottom 10% while SPY was above its 200-day was up 20 sessions later 74.8% of 103 times (any uptrend day 67.2%). The same reading below the 200-day: 54.3% of 94, a coin flip against 61.0% for any downtrend day.

How sure: The bottom-10% share over all days is 66.3% with a 95% range of 57.2–74.9% (months resampled), and 74.8% is right at the level random uptrend days reach 1 time in 20 (S8), so read it as suggestive, not settled. The RSI full-history lane (28 Sep, in flight) is re-measuring this zone with a 90% range; if it disagrees, this card changes.

Pullbacks and bottoms

Alan buys well and sells too early. What separates a dip that makes a new high from one that does not, and where 'deep below the 200-day' becomes a problem.

When SPY pulls back, does the depth of the decline tell whether the next swing makes a new high?

hover a bar for its count and range

It does, sharply. Declines of 3–5% (36 since 2003): 80.6% of the rebounds made a new high. 5–10% (50): 70.0%. 10–20% (24): 25.0%. Across all 125 declines: 64.8%. That is the “selling too early” question in numbers: on a 3–5% pullback the next swing usually makes a new high; on a 10%+ decline it usually does not.

How sure: Counts are small in the deep buckets: 10–20% has 24 declines (95% range 12.0–44.9%) and 20%+ has 4, which is a list, not a rate. Swings are 10/10 pivots, known only 10 bars later.

How far below its 200-day does SPY have to be before “deep below” becomes a problem?

hover a bar for its count and range

More than 10% below (4.9% of SPY's days) is where the wide outcomes live: up 60 sessions later 61.5% of the time (any day 72.1%) with a middle result of +4.0%, but 42.5% of those days saw a close 10% lower inside the 60 sessions. Days 2–5% above the line: up 76.6%, and only 7.6% saw a 10% drop.

How sure: Days cluster: the 275 days more than 10% below come from a handful of bear markets (2008–09, 2020, 2022), so the shares are those episodes, not 275 independent draws. Slope of the 200-day and the 50/200 order are not yet a second dimension (S9 C3).

RSI percentiles

Own-history percentiles, 1 to 100, never a fixed number: an RSI of 35 means one thing for SPY and another for Bitcoin.

Does a stock's own low RSI beat any day, and how far down its own ladder does the edge last?

hover a bar for its count and range

A stock's RSI in the bottom 5% of its own 3-year history was up 20 sessions later 59.4% of 22,864 times, against 56.4% for any day. The edge shrinks step by step and is gone by the bottom 30% (56.4%). S8 adds that at the bottom 5% the stock beat SPY over the same days only 51.6% of the time: it mostly rode the tide.

How sure: Episodes fire together in sell-offs (many names on the same days), so the effective sample is far smaller than the count; S8's month-clustered ranges for the fund rows are 8–20 points wide. Pooled over 313 names, unweighted by size or era.

Fear, greed and macro

Trade the fear/greed cycle and manage risk between the extremes: the VIX, put/call, the dollar and yields, read at the market's own turning points.

How high is the VIX at the low, given how far SPY has fallen so far?

hover a bar for its count and range

The VIX at SPY's swing low rises with the depth: middle 13.8 on 0–3% dips, 16.9 on 3–5%, 23.5 on 5–10%, 30.6 on 10–20% and 65.8 on the 4 declines of 20% or more. A VIX in the low 20s has marked the low of a 5–10% decline far more often than of a 10%+ one.

How sure: These are the VIX on the day of the low, not a level that calls the low in advance; the quarter-to-three-quarter ranges (in the hover) overlap between neighbouring buckets. VIX 3-month, put/call and Fear & Greed are not yet on this card (S9 D1–D3; the market-regime lane, 28 Sep, in flight).

Execution

The setups that held in both halves of history, how rare they are, and what an entry into a report is worth.

Did the best entry combination hold in both halves of history, or was it found in one and fitted to the other?

hover a bar for its count and range

It held. stock RSI in its bottom 20% + close extended (top 10%) vs its 200-day SMA, while SPY RSI in its bottom 30%: up 20 sessions later 67.0% in 2004–2016 (found there) and 66.4% in 2017–2026 (checked there), against 56.5% and 56.4% for any day. But only 231 trades in 85 entry months: a rare setup, not a daily one.

How sure: 231 trades that mostly fired in the same sell-off months; a 95% range on 116 trades is 57.4–74.3%. Middle result +2.6% and +2.8%. Price only, no costs.

What may enter, and what never does

  • A card needs all of: a stated question · a measured answer with its uncertainty · a saved chart (both sizes) · the sample and date range · a link to the study and its numbers · a review by a second lane or a second method. Missing one, the build refuses it.
  • Never: a raw table dump, a study nobody has reviewed, a number without a chart, a chart without a date, a grey line, a buy rule.
  • At most twelve cards on the tab. A new card that answers an old question replaces it; the old chart stays in the study's folder.
