What this tab is. One card per question. Each card carries one chart that is also saved as a file next to this page, one plain sentence with the numbers, how sure the number is, the sample and the dates, the study it came from and who reviewed it. A study that has not been reviewed shows up only as a name under “measuring now”. A card measured over a fixed window (“up after 20 sessions”) or a forced cut-off (“the bottom 10%”) is held back below the tab until it is re-measured on pivots and full percentiles. Green is up or better than any day; red is down or worse. A dot is hollow when its 95% range crosses its any-day line. A fear measure is drawn in red.

Pullbacks and bottoms

Alan buys well and sells too early. What separates a dip that makes a new high from one that does not, and where 'deep below the 200-day' becomes a problem.

Once SPY has fallen X% from its last swing high, how often did the next swing go on to make a new high?

hover or tap a mark for its count and range

The further it has already fallen, the less often the rebound made a new high, and this uses only what is known during the fall. Of all 125 SPY declines since 2003, 64.8% were followed by a new high. Of the 78 that got at least 5% deep, 53.8%; of the 28 that got at least 10% deep, 25.0%. QQQ and IWM fall the same way but less steeply: at 10% down, QQQ 46.4% of 28 and IWM 46.3% of 54, so SPY's 25.0% is not repeated in its siblings. All three drop under their own all-declines line from about 3% down.

How sure: Counts shrink along the curve: SPY has 28 declines that got 10% deep (95% range 12.7–43.4%, not clustered) and 4 that got 20% deep, which is a list, not a rate; the chart fades points as their count falls instead of cutting them off. Swings are 10/10 pivots: the swing high is confirmed 10 bars after it, and one long fall can be split into several pivot declines (SPY's deepest here is 36.5%, not 2008's full fall). With a 20-bar pivot the count of SPY declines that reached 10% goes from 28 to 23 (S9 sensitivity), so the deep end depends on the pivot width.

Fear, greed and macro

Trade the fear/greed cycle and manage risk between the extremes: the VIX, put/call, the dollar and yields, read at the market's own turning points.

How high was the VIX at SPY's swing lows, against how far SPY had fallen?

hover or tap a mark for its count and range

Deeper declines ended with a higher VIX: the rank correlation between how far SPY fell and the VIX on the day of the low is 0.81 over 125 declines since 2003 (QQQ 0.82, IWM 0.75). The middle VIX at a SPY low was 21.3. But the spread is wide: the 25 declines whose VIX at the low sat in the middle fifth of SPY's own lows (19.3–24.7) ranged from 3.1% to 10.4% deep.

How sure: The VIX on the day of the low is known only once the low is confirmed (10 bars later), so this is a description of past lows, not a level that calls one. The four deepest declines carry the top-right of the chart on their own. VIX 3-month, put/call and Fear & Greed are not on this card yet (S9 D1–D3; the market-regime lane, 28 Sep, in flight).

Does a low SPY RSI mean more when SPY is above its 200-day than below it?

hover or tap a mark for its count and range

Not in a way that can be told apart from chance, at any step. At SPY's RSI bottom 10%: above its 200-day, up 20 sessions later 74.8% of 103 times, 95% range 62.9–86.2%, which contains any uptrend day's 67.2%; below it, 54.3% of 94, range 40.8–66.3%, which contains any downtrend day's 61.0%. The two ranges overlap from 62.9% to 66.3%. The steps do not even agree with each other: at the bottom 5% the below-200 reading sits further above its line (68.0% vs 61.0%) than the above-200 one (72.5% vs 67.2%), and at the bottom 20% the above-200 reading is under its line (64.2% vs 67.2%). 12 of the 12 ranges on the chart cross their any-day line.

How sure: Ranges are S8's month-clustered 95% ranges. The above-200 reading at the bottom 10% (74.8%) is exactly the level random uptrend days reach 1 time in 20 (S8 shuffle, 95th percentile 74.8%). The RSI full-history lane (28 Sep, in flight) reports no reliable gain over any day in this zone.

How far below its 200-day does SPY have to be before “deep below” becomes a problem?

hover or tap a mark for its count and range

More than 10% below (4.9% of SPY's days) is where the wide outcomes live: up 60 sessions later 61.5% of the time (any day 72.1%) with a middle result of +4.0%, but 42.5% of those days saw a close 10% lower inside the 60 sessions. Days 2–5% above the line: up 76.6%, and only 7.6% saw a 10% drop.

How sure: Days cluster: the 275 days more than 10% below come from a handful of bear markets (2008–09, 2020, 2022), so the shares are those episodes, not 275 independent draws. No range is published per band. Slope of the 200-day and the 50/200 order are not yet a second dimension (S9 C3).

Does a stock's own low RSI beat any day, and how far down its own ladder does the edge last?

hover or tap a mark for its count and range

A stock's RSI in the bottom 5% of its own 3-year history was up 20 sessions later 59.4% of 22,864 times, against 56.4% for any day: a 3.0-point gap whose 95% range (56.3–62.5%) reaches just below the any-day line. It shrinks step by step and is gone by the bottom 30% (56.4%). 11 of the 11 steps have a range that crosses the line. At the bottom 5% the stock beat SPY over the same days only 51.6% of the time: it mostly rode the tide.

How sure: Episodes fire together in sell-offs (many names on the same days), so the month-clustered range is the one that counts: 56.3–62.5% at the bottom 5%, touching the any-day 56.4%. Random same-size draws of days reach 57.0% 1 time in 20 (S8 shuffle), which 59.4% beats; the two checks disagree, so this is not settled. Pooled over 313 names, unweighted by size or era.

What did the best-scoring entry combination do, and has it been tested on data it was not chosen on?

hover or tap a mark for its count and range

It has not. This is the best of 1,159 combinations, chosen on the weaker of its two halves: stock RSI in its bottom 20% + close extended (top 10%) vs its 200-day SMA, while SPY RSI in its bottom 30%. Up 20 sessions later 67.0% of 115 trades in 2004–2016 and 66.4% of 116 in 2017–2026, against 56.5% and 56.4% for any day. Both halves were used to pick it, so neither half is a check, and no untouched period was held back. 231 trades in 85 entry months: a rare setup.

How sure: Picking the best of 1,159 on both halves lifts both halves' numbers by an unknown amount (the winner's curse). The 95% range on all 231 trades (59.6–73.1%) and the shuffle (random days in the same market state reach 64.9% 1 time in 20) do not correct for that choice. A fair test: choose on 2004–2016 only and report 2017–2026 untouched. Price only, no costs.

What may enter, and what never does

  • A card needs all of: a stated question · a measured answer with its uncertainty · a saved chart (both sizes) · the sample and date range · its method (horizon and cut) · a link to the study and its numbers · a named review. Missing one, the build refuses it.
  • To be on the tab (not held back): the horizon is a pivot or swing, never a fixed count of sessions · the condition is read over its full distribution (percentiles 1..100, every whole percent, every record), never a forced cut-off · a named review that is not part of how the finding was chosen.
  • Never: a raw table dump, a study nobody has reviewed, a number without a chart, a chart without a date, a grey line, a bar that starts above zero, a buy rule, a fixed window, a forced cut-off.
  • At most twelve cards on the tab. A new card that answers an old question replaces it; the old chart stays in the study's folder.
