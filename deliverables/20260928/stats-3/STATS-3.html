What we found, in plain words

  1. The Geiger's own scale does not run narrower for index funds. The composite's 5th–95th band is about 1.51 wide for index funds and 1.51 for large caps, because the trend family saturates at ±1 for everything. What runs narrower is RSI (36.6 points for index funds against 39.3 for large caps), which sits inside the momentum family. Scored against its own history, 12 of 19 index funds would read redder today by more than 0.2, and SPY would read less green (0.76 printed, 0.50 on its own scale, the 75th percentile). Alan's pullback cell (strong trend + weak momentum) is rare (1.1% of stock-days) and carried no edge against SPY pooled (-0.07 points over 63 sessions, range -0.49 to 0.29); in a falling market it was worse (-1.76, luck-proof). The cell that did carry an edge was the opposite corner, weak trend + weak momentum (+0.73 points, leaning), and the composite ladder slopes the same way: the lower the rung, the better the next quarter (section 1).
  2. RSI's own percentile is the strongest single fear gauge, and confluence adds size, not certainty. Alone, SPY's RSI in its bottom 10% gave +3.01% over the next 21 sessions (any day +1.47%; luck-proof) and a confirmed swing low within reach 84.0% of the time (any day 30.9%). The VIX level alone gave a similar median (+3.23%) with a range three times wider, because it fires inside crashes (83.3% of its firing days sat more than 10% under the high). Of 63 combinations at the top-10% line, 26 are luck-proof, all of them containing RSI or breadth or the fear proxy; the best pair adds about 0.37 points to its better single. At the top-5% line VIX level + RSI gave +4.67% (31 visits, luck-proof). At tops nothing works: no greed combination clears the check over 21 sessions. Alan's framing holds on the tape: VIX extremes fire in the deep declines (median -18.0% under the high) and put/call extremes in the shallow-to-middle ones (median -10.2%), and in bar-hours put/call's edge is significant from about 20 bar-hours on while the VIX level's is only clear at 13–20 bar-hours and then too wide to call (section 2).
  3. Below a falling 200-day with the 50 under it, one entry in six fell another 33% within a year, and the one-in-ten line was -43%. 6,714 entries, 390 single companies, 2004–2025; within 126 sessions the 33% line was crossed by 9.7%, which is Alan's one in ten. What marked the bad decile at entry, luck-proof: a wide usual day (the strongest, 2.6% against 1.8%), a deep fall from the 52-week high already (-26% against -18%), a steeper 200-day slope, a worse prior year, and a market that was itself falling with the VIX high. RSI, volume and days already below did not mark it. Seven flags chosen on 2004–2015 and read on 2016 →: with no flag 7.3% fell 33%+, with four or five flags about 21.3%. Being in the state did not disqualify a name from leading that year: 11.9% of names in the state on 1 January were top-decile winners that year (any name 10.2%); what did predict winning was having won the year before (20.1%). Alan's line holds on the second half: bet on winners; the state does not forbid a name, it widens its tail (section 3).
  4. Leaders trail SPY after a low because the first bounce belongs to the worst fallers, not because leaders fell more. From the confirmation of 73 SPY swing lows (2007–2026), the leaders basket trailed SPY by 0.42 points over 63 sessions (range -0.72 to -0.03, p 0.06) and was level by 252 (+0.02). They fell as much as SPY into the low (ratio 0.96), so it is not beta. Equal weight and small caps did not beat SPY after lows either (+0.13, -0.01); the worst-decile fallers into the low did (+3.43 at 63, +7.95 at 126), which is where the leaders' lag comes from, and which is inflated by survivorship (today's names survived their falls). Rules to test, not advice: shifting part of a leaders book to equal weight after a confirmed low gained nothing (+0.59 points for 100% for 63 sessions, not shown); shifting a quarter to the worst fallers for a quarter gained +0.98 points per low (67.1% of lows, luck-proof), a number to re-test on a point-in-time universe before believing (section 4).
  5. A one-sided sigma day like 23 September (0 up, 25 down of 486) says little about the next month and a little about the next ten days: more of the same. Days of that class have come 350 times in 315 separate visits since 2003; SPY's next 21 sessions after them were +1.82% against +1.40% on any day (not shown), and of the 11 rungs of the ladder and 13 one-sided definitions, 1 clear the false-discovery check over 21 sessions and 0 over 63, none of them the row today sits in. What does differ is what comes right after: 13.7% of the next ten sessions were themselves top-decile down-sigma days, against 10.5% on any day, and after a five-day cluster in its top 1% it was 22.2%. Per name, the 126,589 sigma events since 2003 carry no edge as triggers: a 2×+ down day in a name above its 200-day was followed by +0.12 points against SPY over 21 sessions (leaning), the cap leaders' down days by -0.19 (not shown), and every cell of direction × size × trend sits within a fraction of a point of any day. A sigma scan is a list of names to look at, not a signal; the page says which rows to print and with what base rate (section 5).
  6. Gold's parabolic run already broke: it peaked on 2026-03-01 at 5366 (31% above its 200-day, 72% up on the year) and has fallen 25.6% since, with no new high 117 sessions later; today it sits 5.6% under its 200-day, the 16th percentile of its own extension. In 52 years gold had 13 such runs (extension in its top 5% of own history). What followed the peak: a median fall of 21.2%, and the wait for a new high ran from a month (1978, 2024) to 28 years after 1980 and 9 years after 2011. Read as a ladder, though, extension was not a sell signal: the top rungs' next year was +20% to +22% (any day +5%), because runs ran on; the bad rungs are the deep-below ones, where gold is now. The dollar is not extended: +1.6% from its 200-day, the 66th percentile; its 19 past extensions were followed by a median year of -2.9%. Gold's best years came with the dollar deep under its 200-day (+14.4% when the dollar was in its bottom decile, luck-proof); at the dollar's rung today the gold year was +1.8% (section 6).

How to read it. The composite's width is the same for every type within a few hundredths: the trend family is a count of fan pairs in order, which reaches ±1 for an index as easily as for a stock. How to read it. Strong trend with weak momentum is the corner Alan described, and it is a small corner: the two families move together, so only about one stock-day in a hundred sits there. Pooled, it did not beat the stocks' own any-day (-0.07 against +0.07); it leaned up in the first half of the record and down in the second, and when SPY was below its 200-day it was clearly worse (-1.76 points, luck-proof): a strong-trend name whose momentum breaks in a falling market is a name whose trend is about to break. The edge sits in the opposite corner, weak trend + weak momentum (+0.73, leaning), which is mean reversion in the beaten names, and it is the same thing the composite ladder shows: the lowest rungs of the composite had the best next quarter, the highest the worst. That ladder holds for stocks and, more weakly, for index funds (section 1e). None of this is a buy rule: a 0.73-point median edge over a quarter is small next to the -5.7% worst fall those same names carried inside the quarter. On the fifths grid (the bar's own units) the brightest corner, trend 0.6 to 1 with momentum −1 to −0.6, is 13 dates: a list, not a rate; the family maths almost never let a fully ordered fan sit with a floored momentum.

Why. Three candidates, two ruled out. Not beta: the leaders fell 0.96 times SPY's fall into the low (range 0.92 to 0.99), so they had no more ground to make up. About this page

What it shows. Six questions from Alan's 28 Sep afternoon notes, each measured on the full record with base rates and ranges, ten follow-ups, and nothing that changes the Hub.

Where the numbers come from. Daily bars for every served name and the macro series (VIX, put/call, dollar, gold, yields) from the chart API https://scintilla-massive-chart-api.fly.dev/candles?tf=1d at the 25 Sep close, fetched once on 28 Sep into a local cache; VIX3M and the dividend-adjusted HYG/LQD from the regime lane's Fly pull of FMP (cached); the two sigma tables and the company profiles (market cap, fund flag, sector) from Supabase through the Hub's public read key, read only; the year-by-year top-20 leader lists from the statistician-2 data file. The Geiger replay imports the Hub's own per-rung maths (deliverables/20260927/geiger-review/geiger-replay.mjs) through the sector-rotation lane's daily-history function. Code: research/statistics/stats-3/ (lib.py the arithmetic, pooled.py the date-block bootstrap, one file per question, build_page.py this page); numbers in data/q1.json … q6.json; every chart is a PNG in charts/. Tests: tests/stats-3.test.mjs (the Python RSI against the Hub's rsiLast to nine decimals, the percentile, the false-discovery rule against the Node method library, the pivot swings, the page's images and data files, the grey-only palette).

How sure. Every conditional number carries the standard's three: how many (independent episodes: visits, lows, entries, dates in blocks of 63, or years), how sure (a 90% range from resampling those units, never single days), how big (the shift against any day), and a word after the false-discovery check over everything tried in that table. Pooled stock tables resample dates, not name-days, so a crash counts once.

What could be wrong. (1) Survivorship: the stock universe is today's served names; sections 1d, 3, 4 and 5c measure survivors, and the today's-cap tranches are biased in both directions (said in each place). (2) Daily Geiger only: the intraday rungs carry about three-fifths of the Equalizer's weight and are not in the replay. (3) Fault cuts: 77 histories are cut at a price fault (the bottoms lane's rule); META before June 2022 is not spliced here. (4) The fear proxy is a reconstruction, not CNN's number; its parts are the public ones but the weights and windows are this lane's. (5) Put/call starts in November 2006, so every confluence number covers 20 years, not 23. (6) Gold and the dollar are FMP continuous series; the 1970s prints are daily closes with no volume, and the extension percentile in the first years compares against a short history. (7) The laggards basket ignores costs and liquidity. (8) The 28 Sep sigma reading is not in the stored table yet; the page compares it with 23 Sep.

What was not done. Nothing was deployed; no key, database write or price table was touched; the Hub's own pages were not edited; the Geiger was not changed. The point-in-time universe (F1) was not built. The intraday rungs were not replayed. No proposal here is a buy or sell rule.
