RSI · FULL HISTORYWhere does RSI sit across the whole record (the S&P back to 1928, the Nasdaq, VIX, oil, gold and more), and does a low RSI pay?
SPY's bottom 10% is an RSI of 39.5 or lower. It gets there about 4.6 times a year and stays about 6 sessions. What followed was not reliably better than an ordinary day, and it usually got a little worse first (a typical further dip of 1.9%). The Nasdaq 100 bottoms at about the same RSI as the S&P; the difference is that it runs hotter at the top.

RSI · THE LADDER, 1 TO 100For every RSI percentile from 1 to 100 (no forced 10%), what came next? And does Bitcoin's RSI go deeper?
Yes, Bitcoin goes deeper, and more often. Its bottom 1% is an RSI of 23.3, against 28.7 for SPY over the same years. Single rungs are mostly noise. "The lower the rung, the better the next 20 sessions" holds in US stocks since 2004 but did not hold in 1928–2003, and for Bitcoin it runs the other way.

BOTTOMS · 200-DAY · RISING TIDEWhat happens around market lows? Is "deep below the 200-day" really a problem? Is the rising tide real?
The VIX turns with the low or just before it, not after. When a name was among the deepest 5% of its own history below its 200-day, it went on to fall a median 12% more before getting back above. That is about twice what chance alone would give, so yes, it is a problem. The tide is real: on SPY's biggest days, 92–93% of names move with it. Today's tide is narrow: SPY is near its high, but only 36% of names are above their 50-day.

LEADERSHow much of the market's gains came from the leaders? What did they look like beforehand? After taking profits, does moving into a calmer name help?
Since 2020 the ten biggest contributors gave about two-thirds of each up year's gain. Before 2020 it was at least a third, and that figure is a floor. On the chart, leaders did not look special the day before their year. Moving into one calmer name trimmed the worst drop a little (−17.5% vs −20.5%) and gave up a little return. Spreading the money across a basket is what roughly halved the pain.

ABOUT THIS PAGE

What it shows. Each study from today gets one line, the headline is taken from the study's own page, and a link opens the full study. Each full page gives the sample, the dates and how sure each number is.

Where the numbers come from. Every figure here is copied from the linked page, and those pages were built today from the chart API's daily bars, FMP fundamentals and holdings, Massive's bulk day files, Cboe's published put/call, and Kalshi / Polymarket for the Fed. Each page names its sources.

What could be wrong. A one-line headline drops the caveats; read the page before acting on a line. The put/call and scout figures are a snapshot from Monday morning. The leaders figures before 2020 are floors, because they see only 113 companies that still exist.

What was not done. None of this is on the Hub's front page. The Hub's own index.html was not touched, and nothing was deployed. The regime strip, the scout tier and the Statistics tab are proposals or prototypes waiting on your call.
