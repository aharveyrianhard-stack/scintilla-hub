Market leaders: how much of the market they were, what they looked like, and the rotation question

Leaders · 28 Sep 2026 · built 2026-09-28 14:21 UTC · research, not rules · price only · nothing here predicts
STATUS · all three studies ran on real data. 2020 → 2026 uses SPY's own quarterly holdings (the actual index weights, 28 filings, 2019-09-30 → 2026-06-30). 2004 → 2019 is an estimate from FMP market caps of 113 large companies that still exist — shown in a dimmer colour everywhere. Bars to 2026-09-25. Green = up / ahead, red = down / behind; every line is coloured by its own direction.

What it says, in plain words

  1. The market's gains have become far more concentrated in a handful of names. In the estimated up years 2004–2019, the ten biggest contributors delivered 33.9% of the year's gain in the middle case (all those years' top-10 points added together ÷ all their index points: 34.9%). In the measured up years 2020–2025 it was 67.4% (pooled 61.1%). The single biggest name: 8.5% then, 15.9% now. In 2023 the top 10 gave 17.05 of the index's 24.23 points (70.4%); in 2024 15.70 of 23.31 (67.4%), and one name — NVDA — gave 5.22 points on its own. 2026 so far: 8.68 of 13.12 points (66.2%), led by AAPL, NVDA, MU.
  2. In down years leaders do not carry the index. In 2008 (-38.5%) the best twenty together added 0.02 points; in 2022 (-19.4%) 2.31. Leadership is an up-market phenomenon. The names that were a top-5 contributor most often: AAPL 16×, MSFT 14×, GOOGL 11×, XOM 8×, AMZN 8×, NVDA 6×, CVX 4×, META 4×.
  3. On the last close before their year, leaders did not look special on the chart. Their relative strength against SPY was a little better (middle +3.7% vs -1.4% for everyone else), their distance from the 52-week high about the same (-6.8% vs -8.2%), their RSI own-history percentile slightly lower (45.9 vs 50.1). Cloud order barely moved the odds: bull order 15.2%, mixed 13.6%, bear order 16.0% (base rate 14.7%).
  4. What did recur: size, and growth. A leader's starting index weight was 0.80% in the middle case against 0.20% — contribution is weight × return, so the giants lead by arithmetic. Revenue growth was higher (trailing year +8.7% vs +5.9%); among companies of comparable size, a growing top line lifted the chance from 21.8% to 26.6%. The strongest relative-strength names did lead more often: in the top tenth of the pool the chance was 21.8% against 14.7% for all.
  5. Their runs started from washed-out lows — but so did everyone's. Looking back, a leader's run began at a low about 8.8 months before its year, typically 23.8% below its 52-week high, with RSI in the bottom 6.2% of its own history and price below all four cloud lines 71.4% of the time. Companies of the same size that did NOT lead looked the same at their lows (20.8% below the high, RSI percentile 5.8, below all lines 73.9%). The difference is what came after: leaders rose 81.0% to their peak in the middle case, the others 44.2%; revenue growth at the low was 7.8% vs 5.3%.
  6. The rotation question: moving to calmer strong-trend names gave about the same middle result with roughly half the pain. Across 13,107 up-swings in 103 leader names, one year after the swing was confirmed the leader returned +10.8% in the middle case and the calmer basket +11.6% (SPY +12.3%). The basket came out ahead 48.9% of the time — a coin toss. But the worst drop inside that year was -20.5% for the leader and -10.4% for the basket in the middle case, and -42.4% vs -26.6% in the bad case (1 in 10). The basket behaved almost exactly like SPY (SPY's worst drop: -10.3% middle, -25.4% bad case) — so in practice "switch to calm strong-trend names" looked like "go back to the index". The leader kept the fatter right tail: its best tenth returned +52.1% or more, the basket's +27.1%.
  7. After the very biggest swings (the top tenth of a name's own history) holding did slightly better: switch minus hold -3.05 pts at one year, basket ahead only 44.3% of the time — momentum persisted a little. Everywhere else the middle difference stayed within ±1.3 points. The drawdown gap was there at every swing size (leader ≈ -21.9% vs basket ≈ -11.2% in the top tenth).

How far to trust the two regimes

  • Measured years check out against the index. Quarter by quarter, the weight × return of every company adds up to within 0.62 points of the S&P 500's own quarterly return (the rest is companies that joined or left mid-quarter). 99.1%+ of the weight is priced in every quarter.
  • The estimate was tested on the measured years. Using the estimate method on 2020–2025 gives a top-10 sum +0.61, +1.06, +0.10, +1.19, +1.47, +0.71 points away from the measured answer, and the same top names. It runs slightly high, because FMP caps are full caps while the index uses float. The scale factor (index value ÷ level) moved only between 8.28 and 8.73 billion across 2019–2026.
  • The estimate only sees survivors. Before 2020 only the 113 companies with cap histories here are counted (today's large names, while they were members). A company that led and later disappeared (AIG, HP, Yahoo, Lehman…) is inside "everyone else", so pre-2020 top-N sums are, if anything, too LOW — which would make the rise in concentration look bigger than it was. The backtest above and the direction of this bias pull in opposite directions; the rise in the middle case from 33.9% to 67.4% is larger than either.

Looking back: where the runs began (hindsight, described not selected)

For each leader: the lowest low between the start of the year before and its highest close in its leadership year. The same rule applied to every company of comparable size that did not lead gives the yardstick. The lows look alike; what separated the two groups came after the low.

3 · The rotation question: hold the leader after a big up-swing, or move to a calmer name in a strong trend?

Alan's words: "the market is more bullish than bearish — better to be long-leaning; if I take profits on a volatile name, shift the money into another good one with a lesser risk profile." This is a research question, not advice. The test: every completed up-swing (pivot rule, 10 bars each side) of every name that was ever a top-20 contributor. The swing high is only known 10 sessions later, so both paths start at that close. Hold = stay in the name. Switch = an equal-weight basket of every other company in the cache that, on that same day, had the Station bull order with price above all four lines AND had moved less than the name over the same swing. Where every number comes from

Index weights 2020 → 2026
SPY's quarterly holdings as filed with the SEC (N-PORT), read through the FMP connector (mutual-fund-disclosures), 30 Sep 2019 → 30 Jun 2026. Share classes of one company are merged by CUSIP issuer.
Company prices
The chart API's split- and spin-off-adjusted daily bars (the durable cache). Where the cache has no bars for a company, the fund's own prices (value ÷ shares, split-corrected) stand in; for the last quarter FMP's previous close (25 Sep) does.
Index return
^GSPC daily closes (FMP, cached): price only, no dividends.
Market caps 2004 → 2019
FMP historical-market-cap for 113 companies; before Nov 2006 the first cap is scaled back by price. Membership from FMP's S&P 500 add/remove list.
Growth
FMP quarterly income statements (119 companies), only quarters filed before the date.
Swings, clouds, RSI
The S9 research code on this branch (research/statistics/s9-research.mjs): 10/10 wick pivots, Station cloud order, RSI(14) own-history percentile.

What could be wrong

  • Hindsight in the leader list. Parts 2 and 3 study names because they became leaders. Holding them looks better than holding a random stock would have; the rotation result is, if anything, flattering to "hold".
  • Survivors. The switch candidates and the pre-2020 cap list are today's companies. Names that collapsed are missing from both.
  • The chart API's META history was a different security before 9 Jun 2022 (a fund that used the ticker, with a 4-month gap). It was replaced here with FMP's daily prices for Facebook/Meta. The same rule (drop history before any gap of more than 20 days) removed older, reused-ticker history for WM (Washington Mutual), MS (pre-2006), BNY, SPCX, SHAZ and others. This is a data fault in the chart API worth fixing at the source.
  • Overlap. Up-swings in different names often end in the same weeks (the whole market turns), so the 13,107 events are far fewer independent observations than they look.
  • Price only. No dividends, no costs, no taxes. Switching every time a swing completes would trade a lot; taxes alone could swamp a 1-point difference.
  • Three companies' statements came back too small to file (PLTR, SNDK, CRWD) and six leaders have no bars in the cache (PYPL, ACN, CI, VLO, MCK, HES), so they are missing from parts 2–3.

What was not done

  • No full reconstruction of S&P membership and float before 2019 (would need every member's caps, including dead companies — a keyed bulk job).
  • No sector or theme split of the leaders, no valuation (P/E) at the start, no analyst-estimate revisions.
  • The switch basket is not optimised (no ranking by trend strength or by volatility); it is simply "every calmer name in bull order that day".
  • Nothing was deployed, no database was touched, no trade was made.
