Where it plugs in. The allocation page already runs heat → sectors (tape heat × rotation × cohorts) → knockout, with a breadth leg whose weight is a slider. NARROW is a fourth reading on the same footing, computed nightly by this study's run (one JSON line per day) and read by the page like the breadth snapshot, with its date printed.
1 · Position caps that tighten with narrowness. Single-name cap = base cap × (1 − 0.5 × NARROW/100), so at today's 92 a 10% base cap becomes 5.4%. Reason: in a narrow tape the winners are few and the giants carry the index by arithmetic (leaders study); a cap that shrinks as narrowness rises stops one name from becoming the portfolio, without forbidding the leaders.
2 · Tranche pacing keyed to fear, not to narrowness alone. Narrow readings did not forecast the index (3.1), but the put/call and VIX did mark shallow lows (statistician-2, bottoms). Pace = the state-sized ladder of section 4: the first tranche's share = max(equal share, VIX own-percentile ÷ 200), the rest at the fan lines below. Today the VIX percentile is 38 and the put/call is at its 8th percentile, so the front tranche stays small and the ladder waits; when the put/call 5-day average crosses its own 90th percentile (1.09) the front share rises.
3 · Leader bias, not catch-up. After narrow readings equal weight kept trailing (3.1, 2), and after wide ties the red wing rose in Geiger terms without cap weight giving back the lead. So the sector leg should keep its leader bias (the tape heat × rotation score as it is) and the knockout should not admit a name only because its sector is the red wing and "due". A red-wing name earns admission by its own Geiger components: trend turning up with weak momentum (Alan's screen), which is what "the red wing rises" looks like name by name.
4 · What the page prints. NARROW and its percentile, the six components, the cap it implies, and the pacing state (waiting / front-loading) with the two triggers named. A tranche that fired writes a line in the actions list with its level and rung name, the same as the existing actions.

4 · The tranche playbook, back-tested (research, not advice)

The fan. The Lab's EMA/SMA Fan (Pine v19, the "Lengths" group) has thirteen lines: EMA 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 50; SMA 50, 100, 150, 200 daily; and the 200-week SMA. Alan's memory of 13 is right. The Station's nested clouds draw four of them (e13, e21, s50, s200) and the Geiger reads nine. Here every line is computed from daily closes (the weekly one from completed weeks) and a level for day i is the line's value at the close of day i−1, exactly as the Pine reads the previous completed bar.

The design. A campaign opens at the first close a stated depth below the 252-session high (one per pullback; the next needs a new high). Money = 1, spread over 60 sessions. single: all the money at the trigger close. dca: five equal parts at the trigger close and every 12 sessions after. fan: the trigger tranche plus one limit at every fan line below the trigger close (each line's value that day, known at the open); equal parts. pivots: the trigger tranche plus the last three swing lows below, the rising diagonal through the last two swing lows, and its lower parallel; equal parts. rsi: the trigger tranche plus one part at the first close whose RSI own-percentile is at or under 20, 10, 5, 2, 1. all: fan + pivots + RSI (10, 5, 1) together, equal parts. state: the fan ladder, with the trigger tranche's share raised by the VIX's own percentile at the trigger (100th → half the money at once). a price rung fills at min(open, level) on the first day the low touches the level; a close-based rung fills at that close; whatever is unfilled after 60 sessions is bought at that close, so every strategy ends fully invested and is compared on the same money; the next campaign needs a new 252-session high first. Outcomes: average cost against the trigger close; return on cost at 63, 126 and 252 sessions; the worst mark-to-cost within 126 sessions (the pain); time in market (average share of the money deployed over the window); the share left unfilled and bought at the window's close; and, separately, the cost and return of the money that did reach a level.

What the back-test says. (1) Levels buy cheaper when they come: the money that reached a fan line, a pivot or an RSI rung was bought +0.8% to +1.2% under the trigger on SPY, and its six-month return was +9.5% to +8.2% against +6.2% for the trigger buy. (2) Levels often never come: 29% (fan) to 50% (pivots) of the money was still waiting at 60 sessions and bought at that day's close, usually higher. That is the whole cost of the ladders, and on the Nasdaq-100's 138 campaigns since 1985 it is luck-proof: -2.0 points at six months for the fan, -2.7 for the RSI ladder. (3) The pain benefit is real and luck-proof on SPY and the leaders: worst mark-to-cost improves by +0.7 (fan) to +0.9 (all) points, and only 48% to 69% of the money sits in the market over the window. (4) Time DCA is the worst of both: no cheaper cost (-2.0%), no pain benefit, and the return cost. (5) The state-sized ladder (front tranche grows with the VIX percentile) kept the pain benefit (+0.6) with the smallest return cost among the ladders on the Nasdaq-100 (-1.5), and returned +10.5% at a year on SPY against +10.0% for single entry.

Proposal · the playbook as a rule set. Trigger at 3% below the high (the depth ladder says the median such pullback ends at 5.6% and 30% go 5 points deeper). Front tranche = max(equal share, VIX percentile ÷ 200), at the trigger close. The rest at the fan lines below the trigger, one limit each, plus the last swing low and the rising diagonal when they sit under the lowest fan line. Add an RSI rung (own percentile ≤ 5) that fills at the close whatever the price. Unfilled money after 60 sessions: keep it, do not force it — the back-test forced it only to compare on equal money; in practice that cash is the next campaign's front tranche. Re-size by market state: below a falling 200-day, halve every tranche and double the window (the 1-in-10 hard falls; not reached at a 3% trigger, so this rule is untested here and is the next study).

The worked example (2026-06-10, trigger 725.43, average cost 756.64) is the honest picture of a shallow pullback: 4 of 13 rungs filled at their levels (trigger 725.43, e50 724.42, s50 732.03, rsi≤p10 729.46); the rest waited for lows that did not come while the index recovered.

8 · What could be wrong

  • Breadth history is survivors only. The % above the 50-day is measured on today's served list backwards, so early years read healthier than they were. The whole-market table on the B1 branch fixes this once it is on live.
  • Today's readings are live, not closes. SPY's depth, VIX's fan position and USUAL DAY's 0 / 20 are from about 13:50 UTC; the close can differ. The put/call for today does not exist until tonight.
  • The analogs are a description. Seven gauges, equal weights, root-mean-square distance: a different choice of gauges gives a different twenty. That is why three K's are shown and nothing separates from any day.
  • The bow-tie replay reads completed 3-day and weekly buckets only (the same rule the sector-rotation lane used), and the 1d rung is fast: the "back to median in 16 sessions" is partly the 1d rung's own speed.
  • The tranche design forces unfilled money in at 60 sessions so strategies compare on equal money; a real campaign would keep the cash. The "money that reached a level" columns show the other side. Fills assume a limit at the line's prior-close value gets the line price (or the open on a gap); real fills can be worse. No costs, no dividends, no slippage.
  • Pivots and diagonals are one definition (symmetric 10-bar swings; the diagonal through the last two lows; the parallel at the deepest gap between them). The Station's diagonals may be drawn differently; the Lab should confirm.
  • MU's earlier report days are inferred from volume (the highest-volume day in each quarterly window, at least twice its prior month). A few could be wrong days. The eight dated calls are exact.
  • PCE history is short (June 2024 →). Older release days are not inferred.
  • Multiple testing. 773 comparisons were made on this page; the false-discovery check was applied within each section, not across the whole page.

9 · What was not done

  • Nothing was deployed and the allocation page was not changed: section 3.3 is a proposal.
  • The deep-state (below a falling 200-day) tranche rule is stated but not tested (section 4).
  • The whole-market breadth (B1) was not read: it is not on the live branch.
  • Kimi's and other public tranche / DCA repositories were not searched this lane; the design here is Alan's own description (fan, diagonals, supports, RSI) made measurable.
  • Today's close was not waited for; re-running node research/statistics/pullback-playbook/run.mjs after the bars update refreshes every number and chart (charts.py, page.mjs).
