What this page says

  1. Alan is right that the statistics are weak, and the weakness has a shape. Our studies count days as if they were independent, test dozens of cut-offs and report the ones that cross a line, measure today's winners backwards, and stop the clock at the next leg. Each of those has a standard fix, and the fixes are cheap.
  2. "Deep below the 200-day is a problem" is not yet a fact; it is five bear markets. SPY's 275 days more than 10% below its 200-day are worth about 5.4 independent observations (33 visits in 8 years). Its "up 60 sessions later 61.5%" has an honest 90% range of 41.6–89.6%, not 56.5–66.1%. To establish the fact Alan wants, the study must pool many instruments and eras (N5).
  3. The RSI ladder shows no rung that beats luck once the 100 tests are counted. 14 rungs look "different from any day" on their own; about 10 would by chance; 0 survive a 10% false-discovery check. The slope across rungs is the finding, not any rung.
  4. S8's best number, low RSI in an uptrend, is "leaning", not "luck-proof". Up 74.8% of 103 episodes; 74.1% in 2004–2015 and 75.5% in 2016–2026, both above their period's any-day line, so the direction is consistent. But each half's resampled range covers its any-day line. That is the honest reading and it should be the wording on the stats tab.
  5.   6. The universe problem has a cheap solution already on the table. The scout design's nightly whole-market file, built from the R2 copy of Massive's day files (2003 →), is exactly the survivorship-free, point-in-time universe every leader and stock study needs. 1 · The method standard for Scintilla statistics

Ten rules in plain words, each with the one-line reason. The technical version is section 7.

R1 · Universe without survivorship: point-in-time membership from the whole-market day files; leader sets are percentiles recomputed each month
Why: A list of today's winners measured backwards can only show winning
R2 · Full distributions, never one cut-off: every percentile 1..100, every depth, every horizon; report the whole curve
Why: A cut-off chosen by eye is a test we ran without counting it
R3 · Count what is independent: episodes and years, not days; report the effective sample size
Why: 275 days deep below the 200-day are five bear markets, not 275 coin flips
R4 · Resample runs of days (stationary block bootstrap, block at least the horizon) for every range
Why: Overlapping forward windows and clustered episodes make coin-flip ranges too narrow by 3 to 5 times
R5 · Count the tests: false-discovery control (Benjamini-Hochberg) over every rung, bucket and rule tried; SPA / Reality Check when rules are searched
Why: 100 rungs at a 10% line give 10 winners by luck; 254 rules give 25
R6 · Walk-forward: choose on the first half, report the second; both halves shown side by side
Why: A rule that only holds where it was found is a description, not a finding
R7 · Condition on the market state first (above/below and slope of the 200-day, VIX percentile, breadth); pooled numbers come after
Why: Rising tide lifts all boats: most stock outcomes are the market's outcome
R8 · Effect size before significance: the shift of the whole outcome distribution against any day, in the instrument's own usual days
Why: A tiny, certain edge and a big, uncertain one need different decisions
R9 · Outcomes with no lookahead and no truncation: read only from bars up to the entry close; open episodes are censored, not dropped; waits are survival curves
Why: 'Every pullback made a new high' is true of any index that went up; the wait is the finding
R10 · Report to Alan in three numbers and one word: how many (independent episodes), how sure (90% resampled range against any day), how big (middle shift), and a status word: luck-proof / leaning / a list
Why: A non-technical reader needs to know whether to act, watch, or ignore

How a result is reported to Alan

Every finding carries three numbers and one word. How many: independent episodes (and years), never days. How sure: the 90% range from resampled runs of days, against the any-day line. How big: the middle shift against any day, in the instrument's usual days. And the word: luck-proof (the range clears the any-day line after the test count is corrected, in both halves of history), leaning (same direction in both halves, range still touches the line), or a list (fewer than 20 episodes: the cases are shown one by one and no rate is claimed).

2a · One number, three ways of saying how sure

S9 reported that on days when SPY sat more than 10% below its 200-day, it was up 60 sessions later 61.5% of the time (169 of 275 days; any day 72.1%). Counted as 275 coin flips, the 90% range is 56.5–66.1%, which reads as "clearly worse than any day". But those days come in runs (middle run 2 sessions; the 2008–09 run was 146), in 33 visits over 8 years (2008, 2011, 2016, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2025). Resampling whole runs of days (mean block 60 sessions, the forward horizon) gives 41.6–89.6%, and the chance of a gap this size from any day by luck is 0.575. The 275 days are worth about 5.4 independent observations. Verdict: a list, not a rate. The fact Alan asked for needs the pooled study (N5).

2b · A hundred rungs, and how many are real

Alan asked for every percentile 1 to 100 instead of a forced 10%. That is right, and it brings the second half of the rule: a hundred rungs are a hundred tests. On SPY (own prior-3-year percentile, the S8 rule; median 20-session result against any day's 1.39%): 14 rungs have a resampled p-value under 0.10 (rungs 3, 5, 11, 12, 25, 32, 53, 66, 71, 78, 84, 87, 99, 100); luck alone gives about 10. With Benjamini–Hochberg at a 10% false-discovery rate, 0 survive. The rsi-ladder lane saw the same thing from the other side (207 above and 200 below out of 2,600 pooled rungs, "about equally on both sides"). The finding is the slope: the low rungs lean up and the top rungs lean down, which is a trend test across rungs, not a claim about any one rung. That is how the ladder should be read on the stats tab.

2c · The same rule in each half of history

S8's headline: SPY RSI in its bottom 10% while above the 200-day was up 20 sessions later 74.8% of 103 episodes (any uptrend day 67.2%). Re-measured here to the same number. Split: 2004–2015 74.1% of 54 (any uptrend day 64.4%; resampled 90% range 60.4–85.7%, p 0.217); 2016–2026 75.5% of 49 (69.8%; 57.5–87.5%, p 0.756). The direction repeats, the size is not proven. Verdict: leaning. The stats tab card that calls it "yes, on the record so far" should carry that word and both halves.

Corrections to the Kimi survey. (1) It says the SPA / Reality Check "is not pre-built" in arch; the multiple-comparison module ships SPA (Hansen 2005, with White's Reality Check as the "upper" p-value), StepM and MCS, taking a benchmark loss series and a matrix of model losses with a block size. (2) It dates pandas-ta's last release to ~2023; PyPI shows 0.4.71b0 from 14 Sep 2025, a beta with one maintainer requiring Python 3.12, which is a reason not to depend on it rather than a sign of health. Its ranking (arch, statsmodels first) agrees with this page.

The RRG formula we will use (the public approximation every open implementation shares; JdK's constants are proprietary): RS = 100 × price ÷ benchmark; RS-Ratio = 100 + (RS − mean over the window) ÷ standard deviation over the window; RS-Momentum = the same z-score applied to the rate of change of RS-Ratio. Window and momentum period are parameters, so N1 reports every window from 4 to 26 weeks rather than picking one.

N1 · Sector rotation at three horizons

Question
How long does a leading sector stay leading, how do sectors move between rank quintiles / RRG quadrants, and what does a shift in the Hub's cohort-compare bow tie mean historically?
Data
11 SPDR sectors (Dec 1998->) + SMH, SOXX, XBI, KRE, ITB, XOP, IGV; SPY benchmark; weekly and daily closes from the chart API cache
Method
RS = ETF/SPY. Horizons 1, 3, 12 months. (a) rank persistence: Spearman of rank(t) vs rank(t+h) at every h from 1 to 52 weeks; (b) transition matrices of rank quintiles and of RRG quadrants at weekly steps, with expected time in each quadrant; (c) RRG reimplemented in Node (z-scored RS over the window +100, momentum = ROC of the ratio z-scored); (d) bow tie: cross-sectional dispersion of 3-month RS across sectors; its own percentile; what SPY and the leaders did after dispersion widened or narrowed
Test
shuffle sectors within each date (keeps the market, kills persistence) for the null; stationary bootstrap by week; walk-forward halves 1999-2012 / 2013-2026; FDR over the 52 horizons
What result changes Alan's decision
whether 'move to another good name with lower risk' should be read from sector rank; how many weeks a leading sector can be trusted; whether a bow-tie shift is a signal or noise

cheap: bars cached; ~1 day

7 · Technical appendix

Universe (R1)
Monthly cross-section from the Massive grouped-daily files: every ticker that traded that month with median dollar volume above the month's 20th percentile; membership is decided from data known at month end. Dead and renamed tickers stay in the months they existed. Reused tickers are cut at holes longer than 20 calendar days (leaders-lib rule). Leader = percentile rank of trailing 6- and 12-month return within that month's set.
Dependence (R3/R4)
Stationary bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1994): resample index positions in blocks of geometric length with mean b, wrapping; b = max(horizon, first Bartlett-insignificant autocorrelation lag + 1). Effective n = n ÷ (1 + 2Σρ_k) over the same lags. 90% range = 5th–95th percentile of 1,000–2,000 statistic draws; two-sided p against a base value = 2 × the smaller tail share. In arch: StationaryBootstrap(block_size, x).conf_int(func, reps, method="percentile") with optimal_block_length.
Test count (R5)
Benjamini–Hochberg over every rung, bucket or horizon reported on one page (q = 0.10 for "leaning", 0.05 for "luck-proof"). For searched rules (S7, S7b, parameter sweeps): Hansen's SPA with the naive rule as the benchmark, StepM for which rules, MCS for the set that cannot be told apart. statsmodels.stats.multitest.multipletests(p, method="fdr_bh"); arch.bootstrap.SPA(benchmark_losses, model_losses, block_size).
Walk-forward (R6)
Two fixed halves (2003–2015, 2016–2026) always shown side by side; for searched rules, choose on the first half only. For monthly cross-sections, expanding-window fits scored on the next 12 months.
Regime conditioning (R7)
Market state at every date: SPY above/below and slope sign of its 200-day; VIX own-history percentile; breadth percentile once the daily capture exists. Every stock result is split by state before pooling; the pooled number is shown last.
Effect size (R8)
Shift of the outcome distribution: median and 10th/90th percentile differences against any day, expressed in the instrument's usual days (60-session spread of daily moves, S7 convention). A shift under 0.25 usual days is reported as "no practical difference" whatever its p-value.
Outcomes (R9)
Forward returns from the entry close, read only from bars up to that close; pivots count from their confirmation date. Waits (to a new high, to a reclaim of a line) are survival curves with censoring for open cases (Kaplan–Meier); the median wait is reported with its range. Hindsight lows are description only, with a shuffled-price control as in S7b.
Weighting (R1, Alan 27 Sep)
Recency: full history and the last 5 years side by side, never blended. Size: cap-weighted variants once historical caps exist (FMP shares outstanding × price), reported beside the unweighted.
Data faults
Impossible wicks clamped and listed (S9 rule); holes over a year cut (S6 rule); every page names its source, first and last bar, and the bars it cleaned.
