1 · Why you could not see New Home Sales

It was there, and the calendar was hiding it. Your saved Economic view is MONTH. A month cell only has room for five entries, and the old rule kept the first five of the day by time and pushed the rest behind “+12 more”. New Home Sales is at 10:00 ET — tenth in the day — so it fell off the bottom, even though it is a High-impact release and the four Fed speeches above it are not.

Measured on the live release, before any change: today’s cell read “Fed Williams Speech 04:10 · Fed Barkin Speech 08:00 · Building Permits 08:00 · Jobless Claims 08:30 · Current Account 08:30 · +12 more”. New Home Sales was inside that +12.

The fix: a full cell now keeps the loudest releases of the day — high impact first, then earliest — and still draws the ones it keeps in time order. A High-impact print can no longer be the entry that falls off. “+12 more” still says how many are not shown, and its tooltip says they are the quieter ones.

Filters were not the cause: the release is graded High, its category is HOUSING, and the region is US, so HIGH ONLY, the category chips and the region tabs all keep it. In DAY view it was always visible.

2 · The now line

Every list of releases in time order now carries one marker, drawn between the release that has already happened and the next one that has not, saying now HH:MM ET. It moves on its own: the room already wakes up once a minute to re-read, and the line re-places itself on that same tick — no second timer, no extra reading, nothing new fetched.

3 · Units: one scale per release

This morning New Home Sales carried actual 684 and previous 607 — thousands of homes — with estimate 0.62, which is millions. Subtracting those gives a surprise of +683.38 for a release whose surprises are normally a few thousand. The Hub would have printed that, and the scintilla detector would have called an ordinary print the largest miss in the series’ history.

The rule — “the same-scale trio”

  • It looks at one row at a time: its own actual, estimate and previous. Never across rows.
  • A value is corrected only when it sits a factor of 1,000 or 1,000,000 from the scale the rest of the row uses (the band is 316× to 3,163×). A 10× or 100× difference is never touched — that can be a real miss.
  • The winning scale has to be a majority: at least two of the three values sharing it. Two values a thousand apart with nothing to break the tie are not corrected — the row says so and no surprise is computed from it. Guessing which of the two is right would be inventing a number.
  • Zero and missing values take no part. The feed’s own number is kept and named in the tooltip.
  • The same rule, in the same words, now lives in three places: the Hub page, the scintilla detector, and a SQL view.

So this morning’s row would have read estimate 620 (shown with a dotted underline that explains itself on hover) and a surprise of +64.

5 · Forwarding your Investing.com alerts

Short answer: possible, but not trivial, and I would not build it yet.

  • They have no API and no webhook. Their RSS (above) is the only machine-readable route, and it carries news, not your personal alerts.
  • Their alerts page did not answer this machine (HTTP 403), so I could not confirm from here which delivery channels your account offers. Their feed list page answered 200.
  • If their alerts can be sent to you by email, the route is: a Gmail filter puts them on a label (say scintilla/investing) → a small scheduled function reads that label through the Gmail API → it stores the subject, time and link in a table beside the news.
  • What it would take: a Google Cloud project and OAuth consent in your name, a refresh token kept as a secret, one new edge function, one cron line and one small table. Half a day, plus your Google sign-in. A mail-forwarding service that posts to the Hub instead would skip the Google project but needs an inbound-mail provider on the domain.
  • My recommendation: leave it. The RSS lane already brings their newsroom in every minute, and the alerts you would forward are mostly price, earnings and economic triggers that the Hub already computes for itself. Revisit if you find their alerts say something the Hub cannot work out.

7 · What could be wrong

  • The unit rule can only see one row. If the supplier ever files all three numbers on the wrong scale, every value agrees with every other and nothing is corrected — the row is simply wrong, consistently. Catching that needs the release’s own history, which this rule deliberately does not read.
  • A genuine 1,000× move would be flattened. No economic series I can think of moves a thousandfold between a forecast and a print, but if one did, the rule would treat it as a units slip. The feed’s own number is kept, so it is visible in the tooltip and recoverable.
  • The month cell now hides quieter releases sooner. Keeping the loudest means a Low-impact 08:00 print can be pushed behind “+12 more” where it used to be shown. Opening the day still shows everything.
  • The Investing.com lane has not run in production. The dry run proves the feeds, the parsing and the counts; it does not prove the function's behaviour under its 100 s caller timeout, or that they will not rate-limit a request every minute from Supabase's addresses.
  • Cross-source duplicates are possible. A story that reaches us from Investing.com and also from Google News under a real ticker is two rows by design. The lane de-duplicates inside its own bucket; de-duplicating across every ticker needs the index in migrations/20260924_news_url_index.sql, because a lookup by link alone currently times out.
  • The news screenshot is a simulation. The rows are real and come from the live feeds; the way they were put in front of the page is not how they will arrive.
  • I could not read the live functions. There is no Supabase MCP in this session and no access token on this machine, so the candidate is built on the last capture of the deployed news-feed (v21, sha256 3a3ac01b…, taken 19 Sep). If the live function has changed since, the block still applies but the line numbers will not.
