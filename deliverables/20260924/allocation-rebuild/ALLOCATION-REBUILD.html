5 · The equal-weight basket

RSP has lagged SPY by 4.4% over 60 sessions and 7.4% over 120. The page measures where that lag sits: by sector, the furthest behind are Utilities (−17.2%), Industrials (−10.5%) and Real estate (−10.5%), against Energy +12.8% and Technology +1.7% ahead. By size, the middle third of the covered names carries a much weaker average Geiger than the largest third. The basket is then the best-ranked names inside those lagging sectors, equal weighted, measured against RSP itself over 60, 120 and 252 sessions.

Read that comparison carefully. It is a backward measurement of today's basket, so it carries hindsight: the names were picked with today's rankings, not a year ago. It is not a return you could have had, and the page says so in those words.

6 · What could be wrong

  • The clock number is bounded by its own window. "How long a daily signal takes to play out" measures the sessions to the best close within the next twenty, so it can never exceed twenty and today it reads fifteen. My first version measured the days to simply recover the drop and answered one — true and useless. Neither reproduces the "about six days" from the M60 note, which used a different definition; do not read the two as the same measure.
  • Strength is a proxy. "Pullback leaders" uses distance above the 200-day average as the stand-in for 6–12 month strength, because the published table carries averages for every name while a true 6–12 month return needs a history read per name. The plan section does read real history, for your twelve.
  • Today's move is only priced for the rows on screen. That is why the USUAL DAY column carries zero weight by default: a leg only some rows have would entrench the rows that have it.
  • The Fed rate is monthly and 55 days old. It is a direction, not today's decision, and the page prints its age everywhere it appears.
  • Quality still rests on however many measures came back — some names score on two of five. The count is printed in the cell, and under three measures no combined score is given at all.
  • Nothing here is verified against the market's future. Every probability is measured on that name's own past, and past episodes are not a forecast.
