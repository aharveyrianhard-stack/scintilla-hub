Dilution, as a scintilla

M59 · 24 September 2026 · branch hub/dilution-20260924 · nothing deployed, nothing applied to the database

“Convertible note… I love those.” — Alan, 23 September, on Beyond Meat’s 8-K

What this is, in one paragraph

A company can hand out new shares. When it does, every share already held owns a little less of the company. That is the whole signal, and it is not a price move — it is a number the company itself filed with the government. This adds a new kind of scintilla, dilution, that flashes in the two places you already look: the company ident (on the ticker) and the board row (also on the ticker), plus a line in the bell. No new strip, no new tape, no new page. It uses the same one glow the rest of the Hub uses.

Where the filings come from

Straight from the SEC, which publishes all of it free and needs no key — it is the original that paid providers resell. Each run reads one file: EDGAR’s daily index, which lists every filing made in America that day (2,376 lines on 23 September). It keeps only the lines whose company is one of your 364, and only the forms that can issue shares, and only then opens those few documents.

The three real ones, exactly as the code prints them

BYND — dilution $15.0M of convertible notes exchanged for shares · floor $7.40 (36% below last close) · up to 11.3% more shares
8-K filed 23 Sep, items 3.02 and 8.01 — the filing. Beyond Meat swapped about $15.0m of its 0% convertible notes due 2027 for stock: 1,097,444 shares now and up to 848,265 more in a true-up on 28 Sep — 1,945,709 shares at most, every number the filing’s own. Against a share count of 17,193,966 that is 11.3%.

The trap in this one. Beyond Meat’s newest filed share count is 515,818,978, from 5 August. On 13 August it did a 1-for-30 reverse split, which was never restated into that number. Divide by it and this 11.3% reads as 0.4% — under any sensible bar, and invisible. The rule divides the filed count by the split, and says so on the row. A second, independent check (what the company is worth today ÷ its price) lands within 1% of the same answer.
EVTL — dilution $130M convertible notes · converts at $2.75 (273% above last close) · up to 46.5% more shares
424B5 filed 10 Aug. Vertical Aerospace fixed a $2.75 conversion price for about $130m of convertible notes: 47,272,727 shares if it all converts, against 101,602,621 shares outstanding. The conversion price is far above today’s $0.74, so nothing converts at this price — but the shares are promised, and the row says both things rather than one. Its share count is from a 20-F dated 31 December 2025, nine months old; the row carries that date.
DUK — no scintilla, on purpose the filing sells equity units, and this detector could not read a share count from it — so it stores nothing
Duke Energy’s 10 Aug 424B5 is a real equity-linked offering, but the share count depends on a settlement formula this reader does not parse. It is reported as NO_SHARE_COUNT and no row is written. A missing number never becomes a guess. This is a known gap, not a silent pass.

The rule

A filing becomes a scintilla when all four are true:
  • it is a form that can issue shares (8-K, 424B5, 424B3, 424B4, S-1, S-3 — never 424B2);
  • its words match one of the patterns above;
  • none of the exclusions below applies;
  • the shares it can add are worth at least 1% of the shares outstanding (or, when only dollars are given, at least $100m).
The thresholds live in data/scintilla-rules.json beside the price ones, so changing them changes the detector and the Hub together. There is no “×its usual” number here: a company has no usual convertible note, so unlike a price day this kind stores no multiple, and its size is the percentage. Direction is always down — more shares is bad news for shares already held.

What could be wrong

  • Share counts go stale. A company files its share count four times a year. Between filings it can issue more, and after a reverse split the old number is 30× wrong. The rule handles a split it is told about and cross-checks against market value, but a company that quietly issued shares last month is measured against a slightly small number, so a percentage can read a little high. Every row carries the date of the count it used.
  • Reading numbers out of English is imperfect. Duke’s equity units defeated it. Two at-the-market programmes gave a dollar size the reader missed. In every one of those cases it stores nothing rather than a wrong number — but “nothing” means you would not see a real event.
  • A 1% bar is a judgement. It was not measured over years; it is the size below which an issue sits inside an ordinary day’s noise. Twelve events in 90 days across 298 companies is roughly one every eight days.
  • Convertible notes are not certain dilution. Shares appear only if the notes convert, and some companies settle in cash instead. The row says “up to”, and quotes the conversion price so you can see how far away it is — EVTL’s is 273% above today’s price.
  • Only 298 of 323 companies were surveyed — the SEC rate-limited the rest and I stopped rather than hammering it. Nothing suggests the missing 25 behave differently, but they were not read.
  • This has never run against the live database. Every number here came from the real filings through the real code, on this machine.
