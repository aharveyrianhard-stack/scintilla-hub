Scintillas that pop and fade

“Where do you think a good home for those Sigma events in a swipeable left to right scrollable tape style could go? … we need to start using this left to right scrolling tape a lot more broadly.” · “I just noticed there were 99 news alerts… It says no favorite ticker news yet.” · “A notification center style card popping out and disappearing, that’s a scintilla.” · “The sigma event is easy to express… how do we overlay the reason?” — Alan, 24 September

What changed, in one paragraph

The one-line TODAY’S SCINTILLAS strip is now a tape: one chip per event, “EOSE −12.3% · 1.8× usual”, green up, red down, swipeable left to right. It is the same strip in the same place at the same height — no new row. A chip goes to the name: its board row scrolls into view and glows through scScint, the page’s one glow. Things that arrive while he is watching also pop out as a card at the bottom right for six seconds, three at a time, and every card is written into the bell as well. The bell’s news is his favourites only, one row per name, and its badge is now the number of unread rows rather than a counter that only ever went up. Where the store already knows why a name moved — a dilution filing, an earnings surprise, an index change, a stored headline — the chip, the card and the row tooltip all say “why: …”. Where it does not, they say nothing.

Three layout faults were found by that measurement and fixed before this was written. (1) The cards were content-box, so a card at width:100% plus its padding hung 20 px off the right edge of its own stack. (2) At 390 the label, the count and the cohort chip left the tape only 190 px of a 390-wide screen; below 430 px the label now steps aside and the tape gets 318 px. (3) At 1680 the card held its line to one row, so “EOSE −12.3% · 1.8× usual · why: dilution · $15.0M” was cut at the ellipsis and the reader lost the reason — the line wraps now, and two lines is still a glance. Every number above is after the fixes.

The chip. EOSE −12.3% · 1.8× usual why: dilution · $15.0M — the name, the move, how unusual the move was against that name’s own usual day, and the reason if the store holds one. Tapping it scrolls the board to EOSE and glows the row. A drag is not a tap: past four pixels the click that ends the swipe is swallowed, so a swipe never opens a company by accident.

Where this tape should go next (same component, scTapeHTML / scTapeBind, nothing scintilla-specific in it): the earnings week band in EVENTS; the economic day band; the dilution filings of the week; the favourites’ news itself; the X and YouTube rails, which are lists in boxes today; and the company page’s own events. Each is a row of short facts that currently either wraps, truncates, or gets a box of its own.

2 · The cards, and how many a day

What pops: a scintilla on a favourite or an index, a dilution filing, an earnings surprise, an index change, and a favourite’s news. Nothing else — an economic release already has the top tape’s own flash.
How it behaves: bottom right, six seconds, at most three on screen, the rest queued; a ✕ dismisses one; tapping goes to the thing. The stack is bottom-anchored on purpose, so it cannot cover the board’s first row — measured above, no overlap at either width. prefers-reduced-motion stops the movement, not the notification.
Only what arrives while he is here. An event older than the open tab is history, not news, so it goes to the bell and not to a card. Opening the Hub therefore never dumps the day at him.

3 · The bell: favourites only, and seen

What the “99” actually was. Two separate things, both true at once. The badge was a free-running counter: every added row bumped it and nothing ever took anything off, while the list itself is capped at 50 rows and can be cleared — so the number stopped meaning anything. And the list was one row per story: with 1,686–2,095 favourite stories a day over ~36 names, the list was full of things nobody reads, which is why the panel could look empty of anything worth opening.

What it is now. The badge is the list, counted — unread rows, nothing else. News is one row per name: the newest headline, with “+N more on META today” under it. The watch list is his favourites plus any name that has scintillated today — on the day EOSE falls 12%, its headline is the one he wants, favourite or not.

The empty text says what would land, in his own numbers: “Nothing yet today. What lands here: a story on one of your 72 favourites (one line per name, newest headline), a name that moves further than its own usual day, a dilution filing, an earnings surprise, or an index addition or deletion.”

4 · The reason, overlaid

When a name has a sigma move, the Hub looks for another stored fact about the same name on the same day and ranks them the way a desk would:

—Nothing found—nothing. No guess, no “probably”, no filler

Yesterday’s headline does not explain today’s move: a stored headline counts only if it was published on the same day as the move. Google’s “ — Publisher” tail is taken off the headline, because the publisher is not part of the story. The same sentence appears on the chip, on the card and in the board row’s tooltip, so the three can never disagree.

Read from tests/fixtures/dilution/BYND-2026-09-23-8K.txt, the filing verbatim.

The tag is a word, not a colour: DILUTION sits on the board row’s ticker and on the company ident, with that sentence as its tooltip. A share count is a fact about the company, so it reads the same whatever the price did today, and it does not fade with the day’s glow.

The chart marker, described — not built. Station draws its chart on a canvas from provider candles (chart/), and I checked: it has no event-marker layer and does not read public.scintillas at all today. The change is therefore new work, and it is one read plus one draw: read scintillas?kind=eq.dilution&subject=eq.<T> when a symbol is opened; for each row, draw a thin vertical rule at the filing day’s x with a small DIL label at the time axis and the stored sentence as its tooltip; skip rows outside the visible range. No new store, no new provider call, and the marker says the filing date — never the price date.

6 · Index changes: the catalyst we were missing

“Bloom Energy … just got added to the S&P. Do we capture those kinds of signals?” We did not. Now: index_change is a scintilla kind, S&P 500 / 400 / 600, additions and deletions, each with the date it binds — the day every fund tracking the index has to own the name. One provider row that adds BE and drops MRO becomes two events, one per company, because it is two facts about two companies.

What is deliberately not claimed: no magnitude (a company has no “usual” number of index changes), and no announcement date — the endpoint publishes the effective date only, so the screen says “effective” and nothing else. A change dated in the future is kept, because an announced change that has not bound yet is exactly the signal; anything older than 21 days is dropped as history.

9 · What could be wrong

  • Nothing can pop until the collectors run. The dilution pass and the index pass are not running, and the scintillas table holds 19 rows over two days, all price_outlier. Until those run, the “why:” overlay has only headlines to work with, and cards will be rare.
  • “Only what arrives while he is here” is a judgement. If a card is missed the row is still in the bell — but if he wants the day replayed on open, this is the switch to change, and it is one condition in scintToastPass.
  • One news card per name per 90 minutes is a guess at his taste, not a measurement. On 23 September that would have been up to 36 cards. If that is too many, the honest lever is the interval, not hiding rows.
  • A headline is not proof of causation. “why: headline” says only that this was the newest story stored for that name on that day. The three stored reasons above it are facts; the headline is an association, and it is ranked last for that reason.
  • The tape is measured, not yet lived in. The numbers here come from a headless render of the real markup and CSS with real rows; they are not a morning of using it on a phone with a thumb.
  • The index provider’s date is taken as the effective date. If that endpoint ever means “announced” by it, the label would be wrong — it says “effective” because that is what the endpoint documents.
  • Yahoo can stop answering at any time. It is an unofficial courtesy feed. The collector stops on a 429 and the Hub loses nothing it has today, because Yahoo is additive.
  • The common brief was not on this machine. handoffs/BRIEF-20260923-COMMON.md is absent from the repo, its history and the operations tree; I searched for it before starting. This work follows the rules stated in the dispatch itself — one glow primitive, monochrome with daily up green / down red, headless tests only, nothing deployed.
