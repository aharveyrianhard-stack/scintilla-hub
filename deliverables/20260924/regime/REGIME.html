1 · The curve, live — and why it cannot go stale again

Alan: "why would the yield curve thing lag doesnt make sense if we have the data yields is easy". He was right, and here is what was wrong. The ECONOMIC room read treasury_rates, which is written once a day by the job treasury-curve-daily. So the room could only ever be as fresh as this morning's write — and when that job was late, the room said "September 22" on the 23rd.

The board already had live yields. The macro rail on the dashboard reads the chart API's /macro route every 30 seconds, and that route carries FMP's live quote for the 3-month, 5-, 10- and 30-year. The REGIME view now reads the same route. Tonight the 10-year printed 5.12% observed at 14:59 ET; the stored table's close for the same day was 5.11%.

  • Four tenors are live (3M, 5Y, 10Y, 30Y) and each one shows the time it was read, in ET, on the chip under the chart.
  • Eight tenors come from the stored close (1M, 2M, 6M, 1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 7Y, 20Y) and say "close 09-23". The live route does not carry them.
  • NO FEED, the dashboard's own rule. A live point that stops updating for 15 minutes while the market is open reads NO FEED and prints no value at all. A stored point reads NO FEED once the table falls more than one session behind. Outside market hours the last live point is the close, not a fault — the page knows the difference.
  • The comparison Alan asked for: the curve is drawn against 1 week, 1 month, 3 months and 1 year ago, as four dashed lines behind tonight's, and 2s10s and 3m10y are drawn as lines over time with the zero line marked, so an inversion is visible the moment it happens.

The equal-weight index (RSP) is -4.27 points behind the index too, which is the breadth half of the same story. 6 · What could be wrong

  • The live 3-month is not the same instrument as the stored 3-month. The live quote is the 13-week bill (^IRX, a discount rate); the stored table is the Treasury's constant-maturity par yield. Tonight they read 4.03% and 4.19% — 16 basis points apart. The brief asked for the live tenors where they exist, so the live one is drawn, but that difference is real and the 3m10y spread inherits it. Every other live tenor matches its stored close to within a basis point or two.
  • The measured history is a file, not a live read. The cycle tables, the liquidity series and the credit/froth numbers were computed once, tonight, and shipped as a dated file the page reads. They do not update by themselves yet. The curve and the catalysts do.
  • FRED cannot be read from the browser (no cross-origin headers), which is why the liquidity work is precomputed rather than live.
  • QQQ's daily history has a hole. The chart API is missing every session from 1 December 2004 to 22 March 2011 — 1,588 of them. QQQ was therefore dropped from the month tables entirely; the Nasdaq record here is FRED's Composite. Worth fixing at source.
  • Small samples. 13 midterms is 13 observations. Five S&P midterms is five. The tables print n beside every number for that reason.
  • One month of the year is not a forecast. September's mean is −0.68% with a median of +0.35%: a couple of very bad Septembers move the mean.
  • The regime score is an average of six bounded measures. It is a summary, not a model; the six inputs are printed beside it so the number can be argued with.

7 · What is not free, and so was not used

  • No paid API was called. Everything here is the chart API we already run, Supabase, FRED's free CSV, Polymarket's public gamma API and Kalshi's public v2 API.
  • S&P 500 history before 2003 is licensed: FRED serves only the last ten years of the index. The long record therefore uses the Nasdaq Composite, which is free back to 1971. Wilshire's total-market index was checked and is no longer published on FRED.
  • Real global liquidity (CrossBorder Capital, Bloomberg) is a paid product. The proxy here is built by hand from central bank balance sheets.
  • The PBoC is not included — there is no free, reliable dollar series for it on FRED. The proxy is the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of Japan, and the page says so.
  • Leveraged ETF volume was not bought from anywhere; the substitute is named above.

What was not done

  • Nothing was deployed, pushed or scheduled, and no migration was applied. The two migrations and the collector are written with their rollback.
  • No price table was written to. The view only reads.
  • The daily market_regime_daily row is not yet written by a job: the table and the formula exist, and the page computes the same numbers on screen. Wiring the writer is the next unit.
