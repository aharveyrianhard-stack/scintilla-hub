SCINTILLA · TRENDOSCOPE — PHASE 0 AND 1

Built 2026-09-24 03:48 UTC · nothing was deployed, nothing was pushed, no live table was written · the Indicator Lab's own files were read and left byte-identical

The job was to take the pattern detector you watch on TradingView — the one that draws the two rails around a wedge, a channel or a triangle — and prove we can replay it ourselves, count what it finds, and describe the shapes it finds. Counting and describing only. Nothing on this page says a pattern predicts anything.

The short version. The replay cannot be built on this Mac, and I stopped rather than guess. The indicator file you have is only the picture: the rules that decide "this is a rising wedge, accept it" live in six Trendoscope library files that are not on this machine — I checked all 2,355 Pine files here and not one of them is a library. Everything that comes after the detector is built and tested: the permanent event record, the replay machinery that cannot peek at the future, the geometry maths, the duplicate-clustering, the four tables, and 8 symbols of real bars (148,389 of them) with their provenance. One of two things unblocks it, and both are Rob's to get: the library source, or a small export added to the indicator itself inside TradingView.

1 · What the detector is

It watches for a zig-zag of 5 turning points and then tries to draw two straight lines — an upper rail and a lower rail — through them. If the lines fit well enough, it keeps the shape and gives it one of thirteen names. The canonical settings under evaluation:

Turning-point finderZigZag lane 1 only · length 8 · depth 55 · lanes 2–4 off
Points per pattern5
How loose a line may fit20% error threshold · 20% flat threshold · bar-ratio check on at 0.382
Overlapavoid overlap ON — so what it keeps depends on what it already kept
How many it remembers20 patterns (this is part of the detector, not just the display)
Redrawingrepaint OFF — it only judges finished bars
Names13: channels, wedges and triangles, rising / falling / flat, parallel / expanding / contracting

The names are shapes, not forecasts. "Ascending" and "Rising" describe which way the lines lean. They do not say which way price will break, and nothing in this work treats them as if they did.

2 · Is our replay faithful? No — and here is the evidence

The two indicator files (V3 for review, V2 saved on your chart) hold the inputs, the memory of 20 patterns, the drawing and the alerts. The decision itself is a single line inside them:

[valid, currentPattern] = mlzigzag.find(this.sProperties, this.dProperties, this.patterns, ohlcArray)

Everything that matters — how five turning points are chosen, how a line is fitted, what "20% error" measures, what overlap compares, and how a shape becomes one of the thirteen names — is inside find(...), which is imported from libraries that live on TradingView's servers, not here. What I searched, and found:

WHAT I LOOKED FORRESULT

Writing my own version from the label names would be a different detector wearing this one's name, and its numbers would describe that other detector. The study packet forbids exactly that, so I did not do it.

What unblocks it (either one is enough, both are Rob's):
1. The source of the six libraries at their exact versions — openable from the import line in the TradingView Pine editor.
2. Better: a small export added to the indicator itself, which prints, at the moment it accepts a pattern, the name, the lane and level, the five points and both rails. That needs no library source and is the only way to learn the one thing no screenshot can recover — when the detector first accepted the pattern.

5 · What the bars say about matching TradingView

Before any replay can be trusted, our bars must be the same bars you see. They are not yet, and the reason is the bar boundary rather than the price. Our chart API cuts intraday bars on the clock in UTC; TradingView cuts them from the trading session's start. Measured on the real series:

TIMEFRAMEET TIMES OUR BARS OPEN ATWHAT THAT MEANS
1H04:00, 05:00, 06:00, 07:00, 08:00, 09:00, 10:00, 11:00, 12:00, 13:00, 14:00, 15:00, 16:00, 17:00, 18:00, 19:00cut on the UTC clock, so it does not line up with a session-anchored chart
3H02:00, 03:00, 05:00, 06:00, 08:00, 09:00, 11:00, 12:00, 14:00, 15:00, 17:00, 18:00cut on the UTC clock, so it does not line up with a session-anchored chart
4H03:00, 04:00, 07:00, 08:00, 11:00, 12:00, 15:00, 16:00, 19:00two clock sets, because of daylight saving: it matches a TradingView extended-session 4H chart in summer and is an hour out in winter
1D00:00one bar per session date — this one lines up

Three more facts worth knowing about the source:

  • Our bars are split-adjusted and provider-built (PROVIDER_BUILT) — we do not rebuild them ourselves.
  • The API serves one stream and ignores any request for a regular-session-only version, so the packet's "extended and regular as separate runs" cannot be done from this source without rebuilding bars from minutes, which would no longer be the provider's bars.
  • No crypto and no futures. The served universe is 364 US equities and ETFs. BTCUSD, ETHUSD and NQ are not in it — and ES in that list is Eversource Energy, the utility, not the E-mini future. Both of your existing captures are BTCUSD, so they cannot be checked against our bars at all.

9 · Three decisions for you before Phase 2

1 · Which bars are the truth? Our provider's bars are cut on the UTC clock; your chart's are cut from the session. My recommendation: make the provider's bars the study's truth and re-read the detector against them, rather than rebuilding bars to imitate TradingView — but that means the study describes the detector on our bars, and the parity table must be published beside every result.
2 · Crypto and futures, or equities only? The packet asks for BTC, ETH and four futures; our source serves none of them. My recommendation: run Phase 2 on equities and ETFs only and say so plainly, rather than adding a second price source now — a second feed would mean a second set of parity work before any number could be trusted.
3 · Who adds the export? The cheapest unblock is a few lines inside the indicator that print the pattern, the points and the rails at the moment it accepts one. My recommendation: the Indicator Lab session owns that file, so it should make that change; the pipeline here already accepts its output unchanged.

10 · What could be wrong, and what I did not do

  • The geometry numbers come from a drawing snapshot, not from a replay. The rail pairs were matched by the two boundary colours and by how closely their ends line up; a mismatched pair would distort a row in section 4.
  • No pattern names exist in that snapshot, so no geometry figure can be attributed to a wedge rather than a channel.
  • The daylight-saving finding is measured from our own bars' New York clock times. It predicts a mismatch against a session-anchored chart in winter; that prediction is exactly what Rob's parity sample will confirm or refute.
  • I did not touch the Indicator Lab, did not write to any price table, did not deploy, did not push, and did not open a visible browser.
  • No outcome statistics. No breakout, retest, MFE or MAE number appears anywhere here — those are Phase 3, after preregistration, and only if the extractor gates pass.
  • The migration is written but not applied. Two new tables with read-only access and a rollback file; the coordinator applies them.
