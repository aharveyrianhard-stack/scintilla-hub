ONE DEFECT THIS CHECK CAUGHT The first build replayed every column except the percentage. The live tick has a second, earlier branch for a price that repeats — and with the market closed that is the branch that runs all day. It was repainting the change cell behind the replay. Every writer now asks one reader, scRewoundNow(), before touching a cell. Without the check against the provider the board would have looked right and been wrong by about two percentage points a row.

PART TWO · SCINTILLAS: ONE LINE, AND A NOTIFICATIONS CHANNEL

You said the strip was becoming another tape. It is now one line: how many today, and the newest one said in plain words. The list moved into the bell you already have in the header — one line each, tapping through to the place the thing lives.

WHY THE LINE AND THE BELL, RATHER THAN ONE OR THE OTHER

  • The line answers “is anything happening?” at a glance, with no click. That is the part that has to be front and centre, and it costs one row.
  • The bell answers “what were they all?” It already exists, already keeps a rolling list, already carries an unread badge — so a scintilla becomes another kind of notification rather than a second inbox.
  • Tapping the line opens the bell. They are the same thing at two depths.

The glow is the one primitive the hub already uses — 520 ms, colour and text-shadow only, so it can never move the layout — and it happens once per surface per event.

HOW MANY A DAY THE NOTIFICATIONS WILL CARRY Measured against the stored rows, not estimated: the scintillas table holds 17 rows, all from one day (24 September) — it is the only day the detector has run — and the Hub’s own strip counted 12 for today at the moment of capture. M48’s rules were sized for about 7 a day. So expect roughly 10–20 a day, all price outliers until the earnings and economic detectors have run through a cycle. The bell keeps 50 notifications, so a heavy day of scintillas plus favourite-ticker news will push older news out within two or three days. Decision below.

WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • The replay cannot reach past 20 August. The rewind’s list of days comes from fan_daily for SPY, and SPY’s newest row there is 2026-08-20. Across the whole universe that table holds 362–382 names a day up to 20 August and then only 22 a day. Prices, changes and RSI would replay any date you can reach — the trend leg is what stops the clock. That is a data-pipeline question, not a Hub one, and I have not touched the writer.
  • Momentum’s stored values look frozen. momentum_daily carries 386 names a day through 23 September, but AAPL’s read and RSI are identical on the 21st, 22nd and 23rd (0.0205 and 48.93). Either the writer is carrying values forward or it has stopped updating. Worth checking before anyone trusts a recent replayed RSI.
  • One request per ticker. Scrolling a long way down a rewound 364-row board will ask the chart API for more series. It is capped at four in flight and cached for the session, but it is more traffic than the board used to make.
  • The board is still cramped at 390. The columns collide on a phone — that is how it ships today, unchanged by this work, and it is a separate job.
  • Nothing here proves the flashing during a real approach. The market is closed and the next release was 3.5 hours out at capture, so what I measured is the chip’s own animation and the shared plan, not a live countdown crossing 15 minutes. That can only be watched during a session.
  • The notifications are only as good as the detector. One day of stored rows, all price outliers; earnings and economic scintillas have not been produced yet, so their routes are proved by test, not by something you can tap today.
