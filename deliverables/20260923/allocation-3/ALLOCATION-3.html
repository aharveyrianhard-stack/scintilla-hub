That check found a real mistake, and it is worth knowing about. My first version read the Geiger artifact alone, and four cohorts came out different from the board — CRYPTO, INDEXES, METALS and MACRO. The reason is that the board's cohort number is fed by two engines:

  • a declared equity takes its number from the Geiger artifact and nothing else — if the artifact has nothing, the board shows nothing, which is the right rule;
  • anything that is not a declared equity — the crypto pairs, the metal futures, the yields — keeps the number in the older composite_staged table, computed by a different path.

The allocation page now applies that same precedence, which is why the twelve match exactly, and it prints how many of each cohort's members came from the older engine — something the board itself does not currently show.

6 · THE TWO ODD READINGS

IEF is the iShares 7–10 Year Treasury Bond ETF — a basket of US government bonds, not a company. It has no earnings and nothing to beat; what it has is a price, and that price falls when long-term interest rates rise. The Geiger reads price and nothing else, so a deep red IEF is the Geiger saying this has been falling — another way of saying yields have been climbing. Tonight: Geiger −0.89, last close 90.19, 50-day average 92.59, 200-day 94.71, −3.6% over 20 sessions and −5.1% over 60. Not a warning about the government, and the page never buys it: it is not in an equity sector.

Gold and GLD disagree for four reasons, and the fourth is the real one.

  • Different instruments. GLD is a share in a trust that owns bullion, priced while the US market is open and shaved by a yearly fee. The future is a contract for a delivery month, trading nearly around the clock and carrying the cost of holding gold until then — tonight 4,376.40 against GLD's 392.88.
  • Futures roll and the ETF does not. When the quoted contract hands over to the next month its price steps even though gold has not moved.
  • They are not on the same day. Tonight the future's newest daily bar is 2026-09-22 and GLD's is 2026-09-23 — one session apart.
  • They are not produced by the same engine. GLD's read, −0.38, is a Geiger from the artifact. GCUSD is not a declared equity, so its number, +0.28, comes from the older staged table on a different schedule. One positive, one negative, on the same metal, on the same board. That is the mismatch you saw, and it is not a fault in either number.

Silver is the same story: SLV −0.02 from the artifact against SIUSD +0.31 from the older engine, with bars a session apart.

WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • One cohort number, two engines. The page now matches the board exactly, but matching it means inheriting that. Where a cohort leans on the older engine — MACRO, CRYPTO, and the two metal futures — the read is a blend of two calculations on two schedules. The page names them; it cannot fix them.
  • The cohort leg changed tonight's sectors (Financials in, Materials out) on a 20% weight I chose as a starting point. It is a slider; move it and the page rewrites itself.
  • Favourites are per-browser. On a machine where you have never starred anything you see only the seed three.
  • Breadth uses FMP's published averages against the price on file, not a fresh bar pull per name. If those averages go stale the share drifts; the page prints their date.
  • A name in two cohorts is pulled two ways and its tilt is the average — deliberate, but worth knowing.
  • The tape cap still hides most names from the last three steps. With four sectors and 191 names entering, 60 get their tape read. The others are now labelled "not judged" rather than "held back", which is honest but not the same as judging them.
  • Nothing here validates the Geiger itself. If a rung is stale this page inherits it, and says which session it read.
