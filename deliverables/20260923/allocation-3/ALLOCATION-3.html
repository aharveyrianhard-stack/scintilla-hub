5 · THE TWO ODD READINGS

IEF is the iShares 7–10 Year Treasury Bond ETF — a basket of US government bonds, not a company. It has no earnings and nothing to beat; what it has is a price, and that price falls when long-term interest rates rise. The Geiger reads price and nothing else, so a deep red IEF is the Geiger saying this has been falling — which is another way of saying yields have been climbing. Tonight: Geiger −0.89, last close 90.19, its 50-day average 92.59 and its 200-day 94.71, −3.6% over 20 sessions and −5.1% over 60. It is not a warning about the government and the page never buys it: it is not in an equity sector.

Gold and GLD disagree for four reasons, all measurable.

  • They are different instruments. GLD is a share in a trust that owns bullion, priced while the US market is open and shaved by a yearly fee. The future is a contract for a delivery month, trading nearly around the clock and carrying the cost of holding gold until then — so it normally sits above spot (tonight 4,376.40 against GLD's 392.88, which is roughly a tenth of an ounce).
  • Futures roll and the ETF does not. When the quoted contract hands over to the next month its price steps even though gold has not moved.
  • They are not even on the same day here. Tonight the future's newest daily bar is 2026-09-22 while GLD's is 2026-09-23 — one session behind, so any straight comparison is comparing two different days.
  • Only one of them has a Geiger. GLD reads −0.38; GCUSD has no Geiger at all, because the artifact covers the declared equity universe and the metal futures are not in it. That is exactly why the METALS cohort's read is taken over 17 of its 19 members — and the page names the two it left out.

Silver is the same story: SLV −0.02 with a bar dated 2026-09-23, SIUSD no Geiger and a bar dated 2026-09-22, 20-session returns of −6.7% against −4.0%.

WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • A cohort read is only as good as its coverage. CRYPTO is two names of eleven and MACRO eight of nineteen. The page prints the count beside every read, but if you glance at the number alone you could over-read it.
  • The cohort leg changed tonight's sectors (Communications in, Materials out) on a 20% weight I chose as a starting point. It is a slider; if 20% is too much, move it and the page rewrites itself.
  • Favourites are per-browser. On a machine where you have never starred anything you will see only the seed three.
  • Breadth uses FMP's published averages against the price on file, not a fresh bar pull per name. If those averages ever go stale the share would drift; the page prints their date.
  • A name can sit in two cohorts and count in both — deliberate, but it means a name in AI HW and BLUE CHIP is pulled two ways, and the tilt is the average of the two.
  • Nothing here validates the Geiger itself. If a rung is stale, this page inherits it and says which session it read.
