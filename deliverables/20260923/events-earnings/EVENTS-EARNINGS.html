2 · WEEK — the working view, and where the tab opens

What that screenshot says: MON SEP 21 → SUN SEP 27 across the top with ◀ ▶ and TODAY, then one row per day. Monday has NO REPORTS. Tuesday carries CBRS under “TIME NOT SET · 1”. Wednesday — today — is tinted and carries CTAS +3.0% under “BEFORE THE OPEN · 1”, the surprise in green because it beat. Thursday carries COST. Friday, Saturday and Sunday are empty and say so. At the foot, the legend for the two cohorts on screen (BLUE, GROWTH) and the line “3 reports in this week · click a ticker for its card”.

The groups are the four the data actually has: before the open, at a set time (when the supplier gives a clock time), after the close, and time not set — which, as your proposal measured, is almost everything still to come. It is a group with its own heading, not an afterthought.

3 · MONTH — the shape of the season

This is the picture your proposal was built around, and the number it named is really there: Wed 4 Nov carries 38, in red. Around it, 28 and 29 October carry 33 each, 5 Nov 24, 3 Nov 23, 27 Oct 19, 2 Nov 12. The load bar under each date fills in proportion to the heaviest day in view and changes colour with the load — cyan on a quiet day, orange from 12, red from 25. Then the three biggest names: 4 Nov gives GOOGL, CAT, ARM and +35 more; 29 Oct gives AAPL, AMZN, LLY and +30 more. Days from the neighbouring month are dimmed but kept, so the weeks stay whole, and clicking any day opens its week.

On the phone the month keeps its seven columns and loses only the report time inside a cell. The counts, the bars and the three names survive at 390px; the span controls wrap onto their own line under the date.

6 · The band at the bottom and this calendar cannot disagree

The EARNINGS band you asked for this morning already knew how to say all of this, so the calendar uses its functions rather than writing its own: the week (Monday to Sunday), “before the open / after the close”, the order within a day, and the result with its surprise. One consequence you will see: both start the week on Monday, so the band's “WEEK OF SEP 21” and the calendar's “MON SEP 21 → SUN SEP 27” are the same seven days. If they ever disagree it will be because the data changed between two reads, never because they were written twice.

7 · What it reads, and what it never does

  • It reads earnings_events — the EVENTS room's own table and columns — for the span on screen only, and keeps only the tickers in cohorts, the universe the room already filters by.
  • One request per span, cached by that span. Walking to November and back does not ask twice. The five-minute quiet re-read that the room already had now refreshes the calendar too, and only repaints when the rows actually differ — your scroll position is kept.
  • Market caps and cohort colours come from what the board has already loaded. The calendar never asks for them itself. While they are still arriving it says “sizes have not arrived yet, so names are in alphabetical order for the moment” and redraws itself once they land.
  • Nothing is written anywhere. No new table, no new schedule, no provider call.

8 · What I found while building it

  • Alphabet is dated twice. GOOGL sits on both 28 Oct and 4 Nov, and GOOG on 28 Oct only — none of the three confirmed. The month view makes it visible because GOOGL appears in two different weeks. That is what storage holds; I have not touched it, and the calendar shows both rather than picking one.
  • “Time not set” really is the normal case ahead. In the week of 26 October, 99 of the 100 reports carry no time at all; the single exception is FDX after the close. The proposal's measurement holds on live data.
  • A defect I made and fixed: entering EVENTS while the board was still loading drew November with its names in alphabetical order (AAPL, AEP, AME) and grey dots, because the sizes and the cohort map had not arrived. It now says so while it waits and redraws itself once. The first screenshots I took showed the wrong order — that is what looking at them is for.

The three honest gaps from the proposal, as they stand here

  • The transcript link still never resolves — no row carries a URL. The Hub's card does not print a dead link: it shows the Transcript button only when the stored call content really exists for that company and date, and the AI summary behind it is the half that works.
  • Report time is unknown for almost everything upcoming. Handled as a real group, above.
  • Press release, summary and metrics are thin. The card shows the gap (“Release pending”) rather than collapsing it.
