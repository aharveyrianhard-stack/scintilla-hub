The concept in five lines

  1. Who may face whom. Only two companies in the same business line may duel. The line comes from C5's comps method: what each company actually sells (FMP's revenue by segment, read for every served company), so Micron and Sandisk are memory, Nvidia and Marvell are data-center chips, Lam and Applied are semiconductor equipment. RMBS vs LRCX, ADI vs ASML, SIMO vs IREN cannot happen: no shared line. A duel across business lines is impossible, by rule and by test.
  2. How a duel is scored. Fundamentals decide. Four readings, each on its own scale against the name's field (the served names of its line; the family when the line has fewer than five measured names): price (P/E, or P/S for a loss-maker; lower is better), revenue growth, net margin, leverage (debt to equity; lower is better). A reading is where the name sits between the field's first and third quartile: 0 = in the worst quarter, 1 = better than three quarters. The score is the average of the four (a missing reading counts one half). Before the field is measured, C6b takes out any peer whose price is strange next to the group (P/E, P/S, P/B voting; 3.5 spreads on a log scale; two of three votes) — a fast grower with normal multiples is never an outlier. The Geiger never scores a duel: it decides only when two names are even (less than 0.02 apart), and then the more washed-out name goes through — the Geiger says when, not what.
  3. Seeds. Inside a line the names are seeded by their fundamentals score (ties by Geiger timing). A name with fewer than two measured readings sits out and is named.
  4. Rounds. A line of n names plays ⌈log₂ n⌉ rounds: seed 1 against the last seed, 2 against the second-last, and so on; an odd name out gets a bye; the winners re-seed and play again until one is left — the line champion. A line with one name has a champion by walkover, marked "unopposed". Line champions never duel each other: they stand on a podium ordered by their score, each on its own line's scale, and the podium is the cohort's pick order.
  5. What a win means. A win says "inside its own business, this name's fundamentals are the stronger of the two tonight". It is not a buy: the Geiger (step 1 and the card) says when, and KEEP / DROP on the podium is Alan's decision, saved with the reason. Scores are relative and move with the field and with the data; the card always shows the field it was measured against.

C5 gives half credit for the same family (SEMIS, HARDWARE, SOFTWARE …) when building a comps set, because a set needs twelve seats. The knockout does not: a duel needs a shared line at fifteen cents of every revenue dollar or more. The family is used for one thing only — as the measuring scale when a line is too small to have quartiles (memory has three served names; its readings are measured against the 42 SEMIS names and the card says so).

4 · What could be wrong

  • The comps feed is unstable tonight. The same request answered differently within an hour: MU revenue growth +146% then −59%; MU P/S 20.0 then 8.1. Every reading on this page comes from one snapshot (2026-10-06 13:34Z) and the live page will show whatever the feed answers when it loads. Until the feed is steady, growth is the reading to trust least; price, margin and leverage moved little between the two calls.
  • Forward P/E is zero for every name in the feed, so the price reading is trailing P/E (or P/S for loss-makers) only.
  • AXTI at 3,169× trailing earnings stays in the field: C6b flags it on P/E but needs two of three price votes; its P/S (92.7×) and P/B (5.0×) are not strange next to the group. The rule is the Hub's and was kept as is (3 Oct: "no new rule without its neighbours"); the card shows the field's quartiles so the reader can see what AXTI did to them (very little — the quartiles are robust to one extreme value).
  • Lines come from last fiscal year's segments (FY2025 for most, FY2026 for the fiscal-year-end-in-summer names); a company that changed business this year reads as last year. The five hand rules (SNDK, WDC, STX, TSM and the four equipment makers) are stated and cited to the 10-K.
  • A name whose revenue splits across lines plays only in its biggest line (AMD, INTC, AVGO, IREN). The alternative — entering it in every line it has at 15% or more — is a decision for Alan (below).

5 · Decisions for Alan

  • A split name — one line or every line? AMD (48% data-center chips, 52% other chips) plays in semiconductors tonight. Recommendation: one line, the biggest — a name should not be able to win two championships in one cohort.
  • The even line — 0.02. Two names within 0.02 of each other are "even" and the Geiger decides. Recommendation: keep 0.02 (one fiftieth of the scale; two of tonight's twenty-eight duels fell inside it).
  • The line's minimum field — five names. Below five measured names the readings use the family. Recommendation: five, the same floor C5 and C6b use for an outlier judgement.
