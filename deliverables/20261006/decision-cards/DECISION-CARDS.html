HOW TO READ A CARD

  • Fundamentals, growth first. Revenue and earnings per share: the next twelve months, the fiscal year in progress, the one after, and the two together as a yearly rate. Then next year's price-to-earnings, how the analysts' estimate for the year has moved, how many price targets were raised and lowered in thirty days, the last report against what was expected, and the next report date.
  • The comps picture. One cyan bar per yardstick (price to earnings, enterprise value to EBITDA and so on): the bar is where the middle half of the peers would price the company, the bright tick is the middle peer. The numbers on the left are the company's own multiple against the peers' middle one. The thick bar underneath is the range that comes out of all of them — low, centre, high. The dashed line is the price. A grey row marked "left out" is a yardstick the rules set aside for this name. "Whole set" is what every peer together would say, for comparison.
  • Technicals. The Geiger and where today's reading sits in the name's own last year (the darker stretch on the little bar is where it spent the middle 80% of that year; the thin tick is its usual level). The same for the 14-day RSI. The 21-, 50-, 100- and 200-day averages with the distance from price. The nearest reviewed lines above and below, named exactly as the Lab labels them.
  • Risk. The name's usual day against the S&P fund's. Micron moves 4.78% on a usual day and SPY 0.70%, which is 6.9 times as much — so a position carrying the same daily risk as a SPY position is about 15% of the dollars.

THE FIXES — EACH RULE STATED

All of them live inside the existing comps code: the business lines (comps-c5/lines.mjs, which the allocation tool's knockout already imports as it is), the price from the whole field (comps-c5/field.mjs) and the outlier rule (comps-c6/outliers.mjs). The four sources still vote, the business line still decides who is a peer, size still only ranks. Nothing is a new method.

  1. Memory and storage are one business line, and the memory leaders abroad are reference peers. We serve two memory makers and two disk-drive makers, so they count as one line. The set a company already has is kept as it is; the same-business companies it lacks are added until it carries six or the line runs out. SK hynix, Samsung Electronics and Kioxia join as comps-only reference peers: never on the board, and priced only when a dated file carries their figures. One small correction came with it: FormFactor was being counted as a memory company because its test-equipment segments are named "DRAM" and "Flash".
  2. 1b · On the lines you named the comps of, the same-business peers set the price. This one is my addition and is yours to accept or reject. Adding Western Digital and Seagate to Micron's set changed almost nothing (+324% to +307%), because nine chip companies still outvoted three memory ones. So on the memory-and-storage line and the data-centre landlord line, when fewer than half the peers share the business and at least two do, those peers set the price; the others stay on the page, greyed, and the whole-set reading is shown beside it. I did not apply it to every line: on Nvidia's three "data-centre chips" peers it reads +195%, which is worse than what it replaces.
  3. Tighter outliers where one peer or one multiple dominates. One multiple: a single figure more than 3.5 typical spreads from the group leaves that yardstick only, and the peer stays everywhere else. One peer: a peer more than 2.5 spreads away on the same side on four of the five price votes leaves entirely, even if no single figure reaches 3.5; and a peer whose removal alone moves the centre by more than 10% leaves if it is also marked on a multiple. When several peers each move the centre that much, nobody is cut — the card says the centre is fragile. The AVGO table shows it on your example: Palantir's 120-times forward earnings no longer counts in that yardstick, ARM's two earnings multiples do not either, and both stay in the set because they are not far on enough of the votes.
  4. Growth credit. The PEG row is the price-to-earnings yardstick with growth already in it, so its weight is multiplied by how much faster the company's earnings are expected to grow than its peers' — never less than once, never more than three times.
  5. Property trusts on their own yardstick; data-centre landlords their own line. A property trust is weighed mostly on price to funds from operations and on EV/EBITDA, hardly at all on earnings, which are struck after heavy property depreciation. Equinix, Digital Realty and Iron Mountain are one line and price each other.
  6. Sales multiples leave when margins are more than twice apart. When the company's operating margin is more than double its peers' middle one, or under half, EV/sales and P/S (the same reading twice) are left out. A loss-making company keeps them, since nothing else prices it. Two-year forward growth is now carried beside next-year growth.
  7. No peer set. The company is measured against its own peers the way a peer is. When it is itself far from the group on three of the price votes, the card shows "no peer set — valued on growth (PEG) and estimates" and keeps what the peers would have said beside it, greyed. Bloom Energy is the one name this catches: 391 times earnings against its peers' 38.

WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • Three peers is thin. Micron, SanDisk, Western Digital and Seagate each rest on three peers, and the data-centre landlords on two. Read those centres as a direction until the reference peers have figures.
  • The growth credit does not help Eli Lilly (−33% → −36%). Lilly's earnings are expected to grow about 25% a year, but its peers' "growth" reads almost as high, because it is measured from last year's reported earnings, which write-offs had pushed down at AbbVie, Pfizer and others. The fix that would help is measuring peers' growth from one forecast year to the next instead. Not built.
  • The margin rule cuts both ways. It is right for Alphabet. For Oracle it removes the two yardsticks that looked sanest and the reading jumps from +91% to +175%, on a centre the card already marks fragile. On Micron's three-peer set it also removes the sales yardsticks, because Micron's margin today is 2.1 times its peers'.
  • Fragile centres. Broadcom (7 of 12 peers each move the centre more than 10% when taken out alone), Nebius, IREN and CoreWeave (9 or 10 of 12). Their numbers should not be read as targets.
  • Funds from operations is approximated as net income plus depreciation and amortisation from the statements we hold. The companies' own reported figure is not in our data. On Equinix the approximation gives 27 times, in line with what it reports.
  • Iron Mountain is seated with the data-centre landlords because you and the tree put it there. By revenue it is 13% data centres and 87% records storage.
  • "Next fiscal year" growth in the comps table skips the year in progress. For a company whose year has just turned it misses the year the growth is in: Micron reads +18% there and +55% a year on the two-year figure. The knockout re-run uses the two-year figure for that reason.
  • Estimates as the table has them. Alphabet's earnings read down 10% over the next twelve months, Amazon's down 3%, Digital Realty's down 10%. NQ1 saw the same. Unchecked.
  • Revisions cover a short window. The stored daily copies of the estimates start on 2 Oct for Micron (three copies) and on 11 Aug for SanDisk. "+1.7% since 10-02" is four days.
  • The Geiger was read at 2026-10-07 04:21 UTC, after the close; "its own year" is 251 sessions to 5 Oct replayed by NQ1's method. DRAM is too new for a yearly place or a 200-day average (129 sessions).
  • Reviewed lines come from LB1's extract of 2026-10-06 18:13 UTC. Sloped lines move a little each day and are valued as of that moment. Only 9 of the 26 names have been reviewed by the Lab.
  • Prices are the regular-session close of 6 Oct from the chart API, captured once for all 590 names at 23:57 ET, so every set of every name is on the same close.
  • The estimates moved while I worked. The analysts' estimates table was refreshed between 00:11 and 00:24 ET on 7 Oct: Micron's reading before the fixes went from +321% to +324% and nothing else moved by more than half a point. Every figure on this page is from the runs made after that refresh, at 00:25 ET. The comps are read from a live table, so the same run tomorrow will differ a little.
