CHN1 · SPY and QQQ inside their long-term channels — where we are, and how rebounds from the 200-day have travelled

SPY has 1.2% of room left to the top of its long-term channel. QQQ has none: it closed 0.5% above its top rail on 6 Oct 2026, the second close in a row above it. Your read was right in direction; the room on SPY is small.

In plain words

  • The channels. Each index has one rising top rail, 1W B2, which is the same line as 2W B2, and two floors under it: the weekly one (1W B6) and the wider two-week one (2W B6), each with its midline (1W B4, 2W B4). Every line climbs the same dollars each week: SPY $1.36, QQQ $1.41. The Lab's strip prints that as +0.18% and +0.17% a week for SPY, the first two of the three numbers you read out (the third is the strip's 200-day cell), and +0.19% for QQQ. That is 0.76% a month for SPY and 0.81% a month for QQQ at today's prices.
  • Where we are, 6 Oct 2026 close. SPY 779.09 is 92.6% of the way up its weekly channel (95.6% of the wider one). The top rail is 788.34, 1.19% above. The weekly midline 1W B4 (725.61) is 6.9% below and the 200-day (718.13) 7.8% below. QQQ 759.66 is over its top rail (755.65), at 102.5%. Its 1W B4 midline (674.11) is 11.3% below and its 200-day (668.78) 12.0% below.
  • The pierces. Since the channels began, SPY has closed under its 200-day in 5 separate episodes (15 pierces) and QQQ in 4 (10 pierces): the 2022 bear market, March 2023, October 2023 (SPY only), March to May 2025, and March 2026.
  • The rebounds start fast and slow down. Off the low, the first 20 sessions ran at a median 7.0× the channel's slope for SPY and 8.4× for QQQ; the first 60 sessions at 4.4× and 5.6×; the whole trip up to the top rail at 1.9× and 2.8×.
  • Bottom to top, the two full trips. From the April 2025 low, at the floor of the wide channel, SPY reached the wide midline (2W B4) in 41 sessions, the weekly midline (1W B4) in 109 and the top rail in 338; QQQ took 54, 114 and 283. From the March 2026 low, at the floor of the weekly channel, SPY reached 1W B4 in 11 sessions and the top in 94; QQQ in 12 and 39.
  • At each low the oscillators were at their own extremes. SPY's RSI at every one of its five lows was in the lowest 5% of its own days since 2003, and in the lowest 1.5% at the last three.
  • What to hold loosely. These rails were drawn this month, with everything since 2022 in view, and QQQ's top rail was drawn through Monday's high, so being at the top is partly how that line was built. The rebound figures rest on 5 episodes for SPY and 4 for QQQ.

What this page shows

  • The Indicator Lab's retained long-term parallel channels for BATS:SPY and BATS:QQQ, exactly as the Lab holds them, measured against daily closes. The Lab owns these lines; this page reads them and changes nothing.
  • For each index: the six lines by their exact labels (1W B2, 1W B4, 1W B6 from the weekly source; 2W B2, 2W B4, 2W B6 from the two-week source). 1W B2 and 2W B2 are the same line on both indexes. Both channels carry the Lab's own flags: drawn (context selected) and slope-approved, on both timeframes. Neither is recorded as an approved attention range.
  • Every close under the 200-day average since each channel began (3 Jan 2022 for SPY, 22 Nov 2021 for QQQ), and what price did after each low.

Where each number comes from

  • The lines. LB1's extract of the Lab's V24 pack (provider branch provider/lb1-reviewed-lines-20261006, commit 99772da), checked against the Lab's pack files themselves: the channel constants are identical in V24, V25, V30, V31, V33, V34. Clean Reviewed V34 · IndexesMacro is the pack the Lab's registry records as installed and saved on 2026-10-07; its file hash matches the one read here. The registry records it as not yet visually approved by you.
  • The equations. The Lab's own rule: a line's level this bar is its level at the snapshot bar plus its slope times the number of source bars since. A weekly line steps once a week; a two-week line once per two-week bar. Both clocks were rebuilt from the Lab's saved bars and agree with the Lab's own bar counts (248 weekly bars and 124 two-week bars from SPY's origin to the snapshot).
  • Slope units. Native: US dollars per source bar. The strip: the Lab's own formula (native slope × anchor span × 168 ÷ elapsed calendar hours, shown as a percentage of the close per 7 days). Per month: the strip's dollars per 7 days × 30.44 ÷ 7, as a percentage of today's close, and again as a percentage of the line's own midline. The channel is straight in dollars, so the same dollars were a larger percentage when prices were lower.
  • Prices. Daily bars from the Scintilla chart API, final through 6 Oct 2026 (5,804 sessions back to 11 Sep 2003). The Lab draws on dividend-adjusted prices, so every bar was moved to that basis with a factor measured from the Lab's own weekly bars. The check: after the move our bars reproduce the Lab's saved bars to within $0.005 on 299 days and 299 weeks, and the dividends the factor implies match the funds' actual payments.
  • The 200-day, RSI and Williams. The 200-day is the simple average of the last 200 daily closes on the Lab's basis. RSI is 14-day; Williams %R is 14-day (0 at the 14-day high, −100 at the 14-day low; the Lab's pane shows it plus 100). All three equal the values saved in the Lab's own daily capture on all 299 days compared, to six decimals. "Own history" is where a reading ranks among that index's own daily readings since 2003.
  • A pierce is a close under the 200-day after a close at or above it. Pierces fewer than 20 sessions apart are one episode; its low is its lowest close. A day whose low dipped under the 200-day but closed above it is not counted.
  • × channel slope is the dollars gained per week over the stretch, divided by the dollars the rail gains per week. † marks a stretch during which the 200-day was pierced again before the line was reached.
  • Sessions to the rail walks the calendar forward from today's close at the stated pace, with the rail stepping up each week, and counts trading days until they meet.

What could be wrong

  • Hindsight. The rails were captured on 5 and 6 October 2026. Measuring 2022's lows against them uses lines that did not exist then. The 2W B floor was placed on the April 2025 low and the 1W B floor on the March 2026 low, so those two lows sit at the bottom by construction.
  • QQQ's top rail ends on its own capture week. It joins the November 2021 high to the high of the week of 5 October as it stood on Monday afternoon (755.65). A fresh capture would draw it through the new high and QQQ would read as at the rail, not above it.
  • Small samples. 5 episodes for SPY and 4 for QQQ, and the stretches overlap: three of SPY's lows reached the 2W B4 midline on the same day, and all five reached the top rail on the same day. A median of five is a description of five cases.
  • The pace depends on the stretch. "Median rebound slope" is 4.4× over the first 60 sessions but 1.9× over the whole trip to the rail, which is why SPY's session count is given as a range.
  • The price basis moves a few pierce days. On the Hub's own charts (dividends not taken out) SPY shows the same five episodes with the same lows, but 20 more closes under the 200-day, and three episodes begin one to three sessions earlier. QQQ's 2022 low falls on 28 December 2022 on the Hub's prices and on 3 November 2022 on the Lab's.
  • The third strip number. The strip's SMA200 cell uses only the last two completed daily values. Across a weekend it reads about +0.16% a week for SPY and midweek about +0.44% to +0.52%. Over the last 20 sessions SPY's 200-day has risen +0.30% a week, 1.7× the channel's slope (QQQ +0.40%, 2.1×). This is a note for the Lab, which owns that cell.
  • Future dividends will shift the Lab's adjusted history down a little each quarter while the saved rails stay put.
