3 · THE OPINION

OPINION · THE AGENT'S OWN READ, NOT A MEASUREMENT

The answer

In my opinion the leaders led because their numbers were going up, and the laggards lagged because theirs were going down. It was not about who is forecast to grow fastest. At their last report, 24 of 25 leaders gave a higher outlook: 8 raised full-year numbers they had already published, and 16 set the next quarter above what analysts were expecting. Only 3 of 20 laggards did. Over the last 90 days analysts raised their profit estimate for 21 of 25 leaders, and cut it for 15 of 21 laggards. (Each count is out of the names where a reading could be found, which is why the totals change from line to line.)

Your growth rule

Nothing here contradicts your rule that growth, revenue and earnings, drives everything. But it fits in a narrower form than "the biggest forecast wins". The laggards have the bigger forecasts: the middle laggard is expected to grow sales 98% next year against 21% for the middle leader, and 7 of the 8 biggest forecasts among the 52 names read belong to laggards (the other is Nebius). What went with leading was growth already showing in results and being revised higher: sales grew 20% or more last quarter at 22 of 25 leaders against 9 of 21 laggards, and the middle leader grew 36% against 14%. Most of the laggards' growth is a promise from a small base, and over these three months the promise was being marked down.

Who pays for the growth

22 of 25 leaders make a profit and 23 of 25 bring in more cash than they spend; 8 of 22 and 9 of 22 laggards do. The number of shares is up more than 3% in a year at 16 of 22 laggards against 6 of 25 leaders. Half the laggards (11 of 22) are bitcoin miners turned data-centre landlords, or nuclear, uranium and battery companies, which pay for growth by selling stock. I read this as the kind of company they are more than as the reason the stock moved, because losing money is not what makes a laggard: 7 of them (AppLovin, Booking, Netflix, NRG, IBM, Reddit, Western Digital) make a profit and bring in cash and are still at the bottom, and most of those had estimates cut or left flat.

Only some of them bounced

10 of the 25 leaders fell and came back: they were 12% to 36% lower on 15 September than on 30 June, and 8 of the 10 are chip companies. Another 13 never fell: mostly cybersecurity, software and storage names that were higher on 15 September than on 30 June. The other 2 (Lumentum and Analog Devices) sit in between. So the industry counted about as much as the company. The chip fund fell 17% over the summer and rose 17% in the bounce, and for 23 of 25 leaders the comparable companies were up over the last month too, against 8 of 22 laggards. Inside an industry the same thing shows again: of the names that beat their own comps during the bounce, 16 of 22 had a raised profit estimate; of those that trailed their comps, 9 of 24.

Cerebras (CBRS): numbers up, stock down

Cerebras is the exception. Its numbers went up like a leader's. On 12 August it raised its full-year guidance, its core sales were up 103%, and analysts' forecast for 2027 went from a profit of $0.96 a share to $1.27, while the forecast loss for 2026 was halved. Its comps rose 26% in the bounce. Cerebras fell 4%. On the 6 October close it ranks 127th of 145.

Cerebras: the stock for sale

I think it shows the other thing that matters: how much stock is for sale. What Cerebras has that AMD, Marvell and TSMC do not is a calendar of new sellers. It listed on 14 May, selling 34.5 million shares. Early holders were barred from selling at first (the lock-up) and are being let out in steps. About 100 million shares have been freed since the 12 August report, nearly three times what was sold at the listing. Another 19.4 million come free on 14 October, 19.4 million on 28 October, and whatever is still locked on 9 November (about 32 million by my arithmetic). Its officers and directors sold about $388 million of stock between 14 August and 29 September. It is also expensive, at 52 times sales against 9 for its comps. Three customers were 76% of last quarter's sales, and $377 million of its $477 million operating loss that quarter was pay in stock. It is not short of cash: it holds $6.4 billion more than it owes. I read it mainly as good numbers meeting a scheduled supply of stock at a rich price. I cannot rule out a business reason: by the first run's reading the same report missed analysts' forecast for reported sales, and on 2 October there was market talk that OpenAI had routed a showcase fast mode through Nvidia chips instead.

Raised numbers that did not get paid

Five names outside the leaders had all four (a higher outlook, raised estimates, profits, cash coming in) and still did not lead: Micron, Seagate, SanDisk, Western Digital, Reddit. On the 6 October close they rank 80th, 114th, 109th, 139th, 128th of 145. In each case I think the market doubts the numbers will last. Seagate and Western Digital each fell about 10% on 2 October, the day Toshiba was reported to be doubling its hard-disk output, and fell again on 6 October: a competitor adding supply threatens the very estimates that were just raised. Micron is priced at 6 times next year's expected profit. Reddit beat and guided above forecasts on 30 July and fell 21% the next day, when its US daily users dipped and management said AI answers were diverting search traffic (the first run's reading of the report). So raised numbers were needed to lead, but they were not enough on their own.

A test on names the finding was not built from

The same rule on the 6 October close puts six new names in the top 25: Zscaler, Synopsys, Dell, Palantir, Rambus and Zeta. None had been read. I read four of them first-hand, and all four had their profit estimates raised over the last 90 days (Dell by 41%, Palantir by 12%, Synopsys by 8%, Zscaler by 3%). Three of the four raised full-year guidance at their last report; Zscaler gave a first outlook for its new year that did not beat expectations. The estimate pages for Rambus and Zeta would not load.

Three cautions

The leaders are not cheap by count: 19 of 25 cost more per dollar of next year's expected profit than their comps do. By money it is the other way round: the 6 that cost less include TSMC and Meta, and those 6 are 61% of the leaders' value. They are also stretched on your own gauge: the Geiger is above +0.5 for 22 of the 25, and that is the "when", which this study does not answer. And all of this is what went with leading over one three-month stretch. The groups were picked on price after the fact, so it does not prove cause.

Market value

The 25 leaders were worth $6.74 trillion on 30 June, $6.59 trillion on 15 September and $7.71 trillion at the 6 October close: about $1.1 trillion added in three weeks. Three of them are 71% of that value: TSMC ($2.15 trillion to $2.50 trillion), Meta ($1.71 trillion to $1.88 trillion), AMD ($823 billion to $1.06 trillion). The middle leader is worth $72 billion. The 22 laggards went from $1.17 trillion to $1.07 trillion over the same three weeks. Cerebras was worth $52.5 billion on 30 June, $43.7 billion on 15 September and $42.1 billion at the 6 October close. On your point that these names pushed the indexes higher: I did not measure the indexes themselves, but inside this field the 25 leaders were about a third of the dollars added. The 145 names together added about $3.3 trillion in the bounce and the 25 leaders about $1.1 trillion of that. Nvidia alone added $656 billion and Microsoft $239 billion, and neither is among the 25. Five names (Nvidia, TSMC, Microsoft, AMD, Meta) are 51% of the field's gain. Over the same three weeks the Nasdaq-100 fund rose 7.8% and the S&P 500 fund 2.9%, while the equal-weight S&P fell 0.8%: the average stock did not rise.

WHAT ARGUES AGAINST IT
  • The groups were picked on price after the fact, and the estimate readings cover the same three months as the returns. This shows what went with leading. It does not prove what caused it.
  • A higher outlook means two different things here. 8 leaders raised full-year numbers they had published. The other 16 guide one quarter at a time (or gave a first outlook for a new year) and count as raised because the outlook was set above what analysts expected. That second test is looser. The same test was applied to the laggards.
  • Raised numbers were not enough on their own. Micron, Seagate, SanDisk, Western Digital, Reddit had all four (higher outlook, raised estimates, profits, cash coming in) and did not lead. Meta led with its outlook only kept and its estimate cut, and CrowdStrike and Palo Alto led with estimates that did not move.
  • Leaders and laggards mostly come from different industries: cybersecurity and chips on one side; miners, nuclear and consumer internet on the other. Part of every gap above is the industry, not the company.
  • The cut at 25 is arbitrary. On the 6 October close 19 of the 25 are still in the top 25; the other 6 sit between 26th and 37th. 17 of the bottom 20 are unchanged. Cerebras moved from 112th to 127th, into the bottom 20 on its own numbers.
  • Only 52 of the 145 names were read one by one: the top 25, the bottom 20 and seven more that were asked about. The other 93 were not.
  • Cerebras is one stock, and the supply reading is a reading. One fact cuts against it: the 27.7 million shares freed on 25 June were followed by a 56% rise into the 12 August report, so a release on its own did not sink the stock; the fall came after that report, with five releases in seven weeks. A check on it: if Cerebras still trails its comps well after the last locked-up shares are freed on 9 November, that counts against it. A rise after that date would not prove it.
  • The company figures were collected from public web pages by AI readers on 6 October, not from a checked database: one reader per company, a second that corrected something in every one of the 52 files, and a third that read guidance and estimates without knowing which group a company was in. On 7 October the figures this opinion leans on were re-read first-hand (section 11). Trust the counts across many companies more than any one company's single figure.

  • The comps rule ran without its small "also named by FMP" bonus, so a peer at the edge of the 12 can differ from the live tab.
  • The page's green and red are the Hub's own direction colours. A reader who cannot tell red from green cannot tell them apart by colour alone, so every coloured figure also carries a sign or an arrow.
