Keeping the data honest, part 1 — bars, histories, closes and Geiger inputs

Q2a · 3 Oct 2026 · provider branch provider/q2a-rules-audit-20261003 @ae2b466 (not deployed) · suite 833 → 844 green · read-only evidence from R2 and the live counters

The short version

What went wrong. Our own bar service refused its own refresh for 56 names for three and a half days, and every one of those refusals was counted as a "provider error". Nothing wrong-company was ever shown. But the Hub Geiger was frozen for most of three trading days without saying so, and the daily chart for those 56 names answered "unavailable" during those hours.

Why. Three rules that are each right alone met each other: the gatekeeper at the bar door ("never let a stored history get shorter"), the floor ("never pull the earlier company's bars") and the widening ("re-ask for the whole stored window"). With the floor missing from one image the gatekeeper let the old company's bars grow back; with the floor present the gatekeeper then refused forever. The 4-minute Geiger robot publishes nothing unless all 590 names compute, so one refusal silenced everyone. The evening sweep rewrote the daily objects each night, which hid the pattern until the next midnight.

What public projects do instead. They key history on a permanent id (CRSP's PERMNO, zipline's sid, OpenFIGI's share-class FIGI) and keep the ticker-to-id mapping as dated rows (LEAN map files); they store history as versions that are never shortened in place (ArcticDB); they run named checks on a schedule and fail by name with a severity (dbt, Great Expectations); they treat "final" as a date, not a flag (Massive revises daily bars 15 minutes after the close and again at end of day; corrections can land days later).

What was built today. Every acquisition failure now carries one named cause and is counted under its own name; the chart API relays the real reason instead of "provider acquisition failed"; the floor cut corrects the object's own bookkeeping; and the night watchman — a job that checks all 590 closes against Massive's whole-market file and FMP's end-of-day, every Geiger cell, every rung's freshness and every floor, and writes one verdict per night with named causes. Its first run, on 2 Oct's session: 590 of 590 closes exactly equal to Massive's file.

The characters

  • The gatekeeper at the bar door (rule BF5) — refuses any rewrite of a stored history that would start later or end earlier than what is stored. Right alone. Reports its refusal as a "provider error" (fixed today).
  • The floor (FL1–FL3) — for a reused ticker (META, T, MS, SERV…), the first day that belongs to the company that holds the ticker now. Lives in a file baked into each image, which is the weakness: an image without it does not know the floor.
  • The widening (BF3) — when refreshing, ask the provider for the whole stored window again so a short chart request cannot shrink a long history. After a floor cut it kept asking from 2003 (fixed today).
  • The morning bell (CA1/CA2) — at midnight and again at 16:15 ET the chart API forces a fresh daily pull; if the pull fails it serves nothing rather than something stale.
  • The 4-minute Geiger robot (GP1/GP2) — reads 7 rungs for 590 names every 4 minutes and publishes only when all 4,130 cells computed; otherwise it keeps the last complete reading and writes a note nobody read until today.
  • The evening sweep (DC1/TR4) — after the close, refreshes every frame of every name through the tail path and rewrites the daily objects, which made the morning bell happy again each evening.
  • The counter clerk (BS4) — counted every failure as the provider's. Now counts by name: ours, the provider's, storage, timeouts.
  • The night watchman (new) — once a night, compares what we serve with the outside world and says GREEN, AMBER or RED with the names.

The five most dangerous interactions

  1. BF5 (never shorter) × FL1 (never before the floor) × BF3 (widen to the old requested_from): right alone, a deadlock together — and the OPPOSITE failure (regrow the old company) when the floor is missing from one image. Rule: the floor must live in the DATA (the object says where it begins), not only in an image.
  2. CA1/CA2 (refresh at midnight, refuse to serve on failure) × GP1 (publish nothing unless everything computed): one name's refusal silences 590 names' Geiger for the whole trading day, and the Hub shows no age.
  3. GP2 (retry 3 × 3) × a deterministic refusal: 56 names × 9 attempts per cycle = ~3 requests a second against the bar service for days, each a wasted Massive pull, each counted as a provider error.
  4. DC1 (post-close 1D pass rewrites deep D) × CA1 (acquired today = current): the evening pass hides the morning's deadlock, so the failure looks intermittent and nobody sees a 3-day pattern.
  5. SY2 (universe digest baked into images and env) × every reader: an admission that moves most pins but not all splits the estate silently; the Geiger boot file is injected by machine config, not by the image.

Outside help — how maintained public projects do it

one security many tickers

What they do. Every public store keys history on a PERMANENT id (PERMNO, sid, FIGI) and keeps the ticker-to-id mapping as DATED ROWS. We key on the ticker string and bolt two side tables onto it (floors, joins) that only work if every image carries them. The 29 Sep regrow and the 30 Sep deadlock are both consequences of the id living in images instead of in the data.

What we do about it. Yes, the store should key on a permanent id. The cheapest permanent id we already have is Massive's share_class_figi (scripts/history-cut.mjs reads it today). Plan: (1) every stored object carries identity { share_class_figi, ticker_segments:[{ticker, from_et, through_et}] } written by the one writer; (2) floors and joins become rows of that identity, derived from Massive's ticker events, with the reviewed overrides kept; (3) R2 keys stay by ticker for now (the readers address tickers), but a writer refuses to touch an object whose stored FIGI differs from the request's — the identity check (BF6) then catches a reused string without any floor file. This is LEAN's map file / zipline's sid, done inside the object.

when a bar is final

What they do. Massive says the daily bar is recalculated through the session and revised about 15 minutes after the close and again at end of day; the official close lands ~16:15 ET since Sep 2025 (before that, midnight — which is the 'why so late' Alan asked about: the midnight wait was Massive's old behaviour, and our 04:00 deadline and P10's own-close-first were built around it). Corrections can land DAYS later, so 'final' is a label with a date, never a flag.

What we do about it. Our FIN1 (values captured after the close) matches Nautilus's two-clock idea and Massive's advice. What we lack is the 'check again a few days later' part: a settled close is confirmed once and never re-read. The night watchman re-reads yesterday AND the four sessions before it (REPLACE verdict on 'confirm once').

adjustments

What they do. LEAN and zipline store UNADJUSTED prices plus factor files and adjust at read time; Massive's own advice is the same ('store unadjusted … your history will quietly change under you'). We store ADJUSTED bars, so a split rewrites 23 years of history and the tail merge correctly refuses the overlap and sends the cell to a full rebuild — right, expensive, and invisible.

What we do about it. Not changed today (every reader expects adjusted). Plan: pull adjusted=false into a sibling namespace for D only, keep the split ledger we already have (AD1), and let the night watchman name every name whose adjusted history changed shape (a split happened) so a rebuild is expected, not a mystery.

history storage and replacement

What they do. ArcticDB never shortens in place: append must start after the last row, update replaces a stated range, every write is a new VERSION and the old one stays unless pruned, snapshots pin states. Our 'never shorter' is a GUARD ON A REWRITE; theirs is a property of the storage.

What we do about it. Replace the guard with versions: write the new object to a versioned key (…/<name>.json.gz is the pointer, …/_v/<name>/<acquired_utc>.json.gz the version), keep the last 3 versions, and let a writer SHORTEN on purpose when it says why (history_cut) — the cut becomes a version with a reason, not a fight with a guard. Until that lands, the P11 cut-before-compare stays (KEEP) and the object's requested_from is corrected by the cut (CHANGE, done).

data quality tests

What they do. dbt/Great Expectations run named checks on a schedule and fail by name with a severity; Soda adds freshness. We have the checks scattered inside writers (assertStrictSeries, validBar, finality) and no nightly run that reads what we SERVE against an outside source.

What we do about it. Built: the night watchman (services/stocks-ops/nightly-verdict.mjs) — Massive's whole-market file and FMP's end-of-day vs our daily close and the board's previous close for all 590 names, every Geiger cell, every rung's freshness, every floor — one JSON per night with named causes and one alarm. First run on 2 Oct's session: 590/590 closes exactly equal to Massive's file.

Alan's side question — why does Massive wait so long for an official close? Since 16 Sep 2025 it does not: Massive's changelog says aggregates "that should have included the official close price" now land "15 minutes after market close"; before that they "were delayed until midnight". The daily bar is "continuously recalculated through the session", "revised for about 15 minutes on the stream and again at the end of the day", and cancellation marks "appear days after the session" — so "final" is a date you re-check, which is exactly what the night watchman does. Our previous-close robot's 04:00 deadline and own-close-first design were built around the old midnight behaviour and are still right as a fallback.
