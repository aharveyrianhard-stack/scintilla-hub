What snapped before: the wheel moved the camera itself, one jump per tick (one tick = 5 % of the travel in a single frame). Now a tick only moves a target distance (× 0.946 per tick, the pace OrbitControls had), and the camera eases toward it every frame with a 120 ms time constant — the biggest single-frame step is 1.2 % of the travel, there is no frame gap over 18 ms, and the home fit (the bisection) runs once per load, never on a tick. The label size and the thinning are laid out every frame; the thinning keeps the labels already on screen first and a label that has just gone is held off for 400 ms, so what changes while you zoom is the count (more names as the coil fills the screen, fewer as it shrinks) and not a flicker: 1 re-flip in three and a half seconds on either screen, against the 2 a second allowed. THE LABEL RULE (label-scale.mjs, one file the coil and the tree both read): a label's type size follows the camera distance — 13 px at the home distance, and from there it grows as you come closer and shrinks as you pull back, in proportion, with a floor of 11 px (the Hub's smallest body text) and a ceiling of 24 px, on a 1080-tall screen (the three numbers scale with the screen's height; on the MacBook's 1050 they are 12.6 / 11 / 23.3, and the floor never goes under 11). Measured on the coil at 1920 × 1080: 13.2 px at home, 24.7 px at half the distance, 11.1 px at twice it; at 1680 × 1050: 12.8 / 24.0 / 11.1. When two labels would overlap at that size, the weaker reading gives way (the strongest |Geiger| is placed first, and any label whose ink box, plus 4 px of air, would touch one already placed is hidden); pulling the camera closer spreads the boxes and the hidden ones come back — at 1920 home shows 42 of 58, half the distance 50, and at a third of the distance all 58 (55 on screen, 3 past the edge). Zooming out past the floor keeps the type at 11 px and thins harder (18 of 58 at twice the distance), which is the right failure: a few readable names rather than fifty-eight unreadable ones. The pattern is the collision rule of Mapbox and deck.gl's labels (priority first, greedy box test) and CSS clamp() for the size. The labels are drawn after the bloom pass, so the type stays crisp while the beams glow.

THE SPHERE'S SCALE · the sphere's volume is the sum of its members' market caps, so its radius is the cube root: r = 80 U × (Σ cap ÷ $1 T)^⅓ — $1 T of market cap = 80 U of radius (a tread is 30 U). FAVORITES' 56 names with a cap on file sum to $40.5 T → r 275 U, which happens to be the coil's own envelope; RADAR's 16 sum to $17.6 T → r 208 U; LIKED's 142 sum to $46.6 T → r 288 U. On screen at 1920 × 1080 the two side by side print at 98 px per 80 U, both — one scale, so the eye compares them. Market caps come from the Hub's company_profile table (the board's MKT CAP cell reads the same column), read 5 Oct through the Hub's public read path; BTCUSD and GCUSD have no cap on file and are listed, not counted. The title above each sphere says so. Both sets sit level (the camera looks straight along the row), and the coil's own scale inside is untouched — the data.

RECOMMENDATION · MARKET CAP Take the sphere, not the step width. The sphere answers Alan's question — "how much of the market is this set?" — in one glance, and two sets side by side rank themselves; it leaves the coil alone (a step's height stays the reading, its width stays the slot, so nothing competes with the Geiger). Step width fights the reading: the cube root flattens a thousand-fold spread into 0.2–1 of the slot, so NVDA and a $5 B name look alike, and the thin steps make their beams harder to find. Where a name's own size matters, print it on the label (the alternative already does: +0.78 $5.7T) rather than bend the geometry. One caution for the sphere: at home the coil inside it is smaller than in the plain view (the camera fits the sphere, not the coil), so most labels thin at home — a scroll in brings them back; if Alan wants the names first, the plain view is one click away.
