What "statistically sound" means, in two sentences. A cohort's average Geiger is sound when it has enough members with a reading that no single name can swing it (we use at least 8) and the names agree enough that the average's standard error is at most 0.10 on the −1 … +1 scale. It is steady when that held on each of the last six closes (25 Sep – 2 Oct, the close Geiger table), and it is a real business group when its biggest FMP industry is at least 60 % of it — or it is defined by one fund's holdings.

Read: members = tickers in the group · on hub / off = served by the Hub today or not · mean today = the plain mean of the members' readings (live Hub for served names, the 2 Oct close for the rest) · std error = sd / √n · mean at each close = the same mean from the close Geiger on each of the six closes · swing = highest − lowest of those six means · the weak reasons name the rule that failed. A Hub tab's funds (FUNDS, INDEXES, MACRO) have no FMP industry, so their mix reads 'fund'.
