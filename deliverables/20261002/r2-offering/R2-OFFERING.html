02 · THE SHORT VERSION

Where you see it. Open any company → FINANCIALS tab. It now opens with CAPITAL & DILUTION: that company's offering paperwork at the SEC, newest first — the date, the form, what kind of sale it is, how big, the sentence on the document's first page that says so, and a link to the document. Beside it, as plain numbers: the cash on hand and the capital spending of the last four quarters.

What fills it. A small robot in Supabase (offering-watch) asks FMP for every offering filing made that day, keeps the 454 stocks we serve, reads the first page of each new one at the SEC (free), and sorts it into one of five kinds. It writes into a new notebook table (offering_filings). Tonight it went back 120 days.

04 · HOW IT WORKS, IN THREE CHARACTERS

The night clerk · offering-watch

A Supabase function. Each weekday it asks FMP "which 424B5, 424B4, S-3, S-3ASR and S-1 forms were filed today?" — one question per form, one day at a time, because the whole market's list overflows FMP's 1,000-row page in a month. It keeps only our 454 stocks and skips anything it has already read.

The reader · the first page at the SEC

For each new filing it opens the document at sec.gov (free; at most 4 reads a second; it stops after about 60 KB) and reads the cover. The earliest decisive phrase wins: "Convertible Senior Notes" → CONVERTIBLE, "Senior Notes due" → DEBT, "35,000,000 shares of … common stock" or "$25,000,000 of Shares" → EQUITY, "equity distribution agreement / at the market" → ATM. It keeps the sentence that decided.

The notebook · offering_filings

One row per filing: company, form, date filed, the SEC acceptance time, the kind, the deciding sentence (300 characters at most), the size as printed, and when the clerk first saw it. The Hub reads it with its public read key; only the clerk can write. The universe it checks against is public.tickers, active rows that are stocks or carry no type (the 90 untyped rows are core stocks — Apple, Nvidia, CoreWeave, Zeta), never a fund, never a crypto pair: 454 names. R1's "364 stocks" counted only rows typed "stock" and would have missed CoreWeave and Zeta.

05 · WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • The SEC refuses Supabase's default region. From us-east-1 the SEC answered "Request Rate Threshold Exceeded" to the clerk's very first two reads — other customers' traffic shares those addresses. From us-west-2 every read worked (552 reads tonight across the history runs, none refused). The clerk must be called with the header x-region: us-west-2. If the SEC ever refuses that region too, the clerk stops reading at once, writes nothing for the refused filings and picks them up on the next run.
  • A resale is filed as EQUITY. Qualcomm's 2 Oct S-3 registers 25,000,000 shares for resale by a selling stockholder — shares coming to market, but not new shares. Lowe's 2,500,000 are for an employee plan. The sentence shown says so; the chip does not (decision 2).
  • Shelves are UNCLASSIFIED on purpose. 32 of the 41 are S-3ASR shelf registrations — permission to sell "from time to time" any of common stock, preferred, debt, warrants. They are not a sale; the takedown comes later as a 424B5, which the clerk reads too.
  • Sizes are missing on 95 rows. Preliminary covers leave the numbers blank ("$ % Notes due 20"); shelves rarely print one; Alphabet's and Super Micro's mandatory convertible preferred print their size in depositary shares, which the size reader does not pick up. The final filing a day later usually carries the number.
  • The class rule is a reading of one page, not a judgment. 133 of the 134 424B5s were decided; one still says UNCLASSIFIED, and six S-1s by SHAZ printed nothing readable in their first 240 KB. Open the SEC link before acting on any row.
  • FMP's acceptance time is taken to be New York time (EDGAR's clock) and converted to UTC — assumption, consistent with every row seen tonight.
  • Cash and capex are what our statement tables hold. Axon has no quarterly rows, so its block says "no quarterly balance sheet stored". Sysco's fiscal year ends in June, so its quarters read Q1·26–Q4·26 for Sep 2025 – Jun 2026.
  • The search for S-1 and S-3 also returns their amendments (S-1/A, S-3/A); they are kept and labelled as such.

07 · DECISIONS FOR ALAN

1 · Capital spending vs cash → expected dilution. The block now shows how much cash a company holds and how fast it is spending on equipment, next to the share sales it has filed. A projection would say "at this pace, the cash covers N quarters; the gap is likely filled by X". Recommendation: yes, as the next step — first the plain "quarters of spending the cash covers" line, then a model that takes your assumptions (no provider's number), shown in the same block.
2 · Resales and employee plans read as EQUITY. Qualcomm's 25,000,000-share resale and Lowe's plan shares carry the same red chip as a company selling new stock. Recommendation: keep the kind, add a small "resale" / "plan" tag when the cover says so — cheap, and it stops a false alarm.
3 · When alerts come, which rows should ring? Recommendation: only EQUITY, CONVERTIBLE and ATM from actual sales (424B5 / 424B4), never shelves, resales or bonds — about 48 rings in the last 120 days across 39 companies, roughly two or three a week.
