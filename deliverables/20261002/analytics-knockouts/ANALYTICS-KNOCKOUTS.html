Step 3 · the knockout a round robin anyone can follow

The rule, stated once (bracket.mjs, the file the page and the tests both import). Every pair in the group plays once. A game is decided by a small, named set of comparisons, each a visible win or loss with its two numbers: upside by way A, by way B, by way C (higher wins); revenue growth next FY and EPS growth next FY (higher); operating margin and free-cash-flow margin (higher); net debt over EBITDA (lower); the Geiger, seven rungs (higher — the condition); stretch against its own history (lower — the less extended name wins). A comparison with a missing number on either side is not played: a blank is a blank, never a loss. A game with fewer than three comparisons played is not played at all, so a name with blanks cannot collect draws (the fixture in the tests shows exactly that: with a one-comparison floor a name with only a Geiger drew every game and passed a real one). The side with more weighted comparison points takes the game; equal points is a draw. A win is one point in the standings, a draw a half; ties are broken by the head-to-head game, then by comparison points over the whole round. The top three advance, each with one sentence saying what it won on and what it gave up. Every comparison's on/off, direction and weight, the number that advance, the draw tolerance and the three-played floor are toggles with their baselines printed; changing any one re-runs every game on the page (the harness proved it: with the Geiger comparison turned off, UCTT passes MU and AVGO enters the three).

Step 4 · the swipe keep · pass · later for the finalists; the Tinder mechanic

One card per finalist (twelve today) with its price, the three upsides, Geiger and stretch, its place, and three buttons. A press writes one append-only row to public.comps_decisions with the page's public key — the table's shape is unchanged: company and peer are the name, measure ALL, off true only for PASS, reason carries the verdict, the group and the date, source is analytics-knockouts. A later row reverses an earlier one; nothing is updated or deleted. The page reads back its own rows (source = analytics-knockouts) and shows the last decision under each card. With no key or no table it keeps the row in the browser and says so. A PASS also moves that name's share to cash in step 5.

In the test browser every write was blocked and counted: the harness answered the one POST to comps_decisions locally (one at 1680, one at 390) and nothing reached the table — comps_decisions had 0 rows before and has 0 rows after. The page's own counter says "1 saved to the table" in those runs because the harness replied as the table would; the harness's count is the truth.

Where each number comes from

  • The Geiger, seven rungs: /v1/scout-geiger?reading=seven on the chart API — the Hub's seven rungs (3h 4h 6h 12h D 3D W) and the Hub's equalizer (receipt d0da9a46…), computed off the Hub nightly; run scout-geiger-nightly-20261002T002101Z-d9e8e4, as of the 1 October close, 5,641 rows. A sector's or group's reading is the equal-weight mean of its served members' readings. The Hub's Geiger: /geiger?symbols=… for the 590 served symbols, shown beside it as a check (Technology 0.22 vs 0.26).
  • Stretch against its own history: the daily rung replayed on finished daily bars with the Hub's maths (deliverables/20260927/geiger-review/geiger-replay.mjs, 230-bar window), one reading per SPY session for 500 sessions (3 Oct 2024 → 1 Oct 2026), per served company; a group's series is the equal-weight mean of its members on sessions where at least half of them have a reading; the stretch is today's value's percentile in that series' own past (ties count half). Bars: /candles?symbol=…&tf=D, 454 symbols, 0 errors.
  • Rotation: the equal-weight mean of the members' 72-session (medium, one swing cycle per the 28 Sep sector-rotation study) and 16-session (short, one leg) log returns minus SPY's, in percentage points; SPY itself 2.3% and 0.2%. Rank 1 is the strongest.
  • Groups: an industry group is an FMP industry on the served names (ticker_industry.fmp_industry, else company_profile.industry); a sector is the FMP sector. 453 served companies, 11 sectors, 96 groups.
  • The comparable set and the ways: deliverables/20261001/comps-mechanic/peers.mjs and read.mjs (the C4 rule), the read through comps-template/cohort.mjs (fundamentals, analyst_estimates, fundamentals_history, cashflow_history, balance_history, filer_currency, fx_rates with 4,729 rate-days, the tree's 51 industry funds, fmp_peers 4,455 rows and peer_sources 8,897 rows), the ways from comps-template/template.mjs. One read per group for the union of its members and their sets (Semiconductors: 46 names), then each company's snapshot on its own ten. Prices: the chart API /quotes at build time, 2 October, 13:51Z.
  • The 16 measures: the comps table's own components for the company (snapshot.table.company), unchanged.
  • The bracket, the stretch percentile, the agreement measure, the allocation: bracket.mjs, stats.mjs, allocate.mjs beside the page — pure, imported by the page and by the tests.

What could be wrong

  • The stretch is on the daily rung, the Geiger on seven. The own-history percentile replays one rung (the daily) because that is the history that can be rebuilt from bars with the Hub's maths; the reading shown beside it is the seven-rung composite. They move together but are not the same number (Technology: 0.22 on seven rungs, 0.27 on the daily rung). A seven-rung history would need the intraday bars for 453 names over two years — a Fly job, not this Mac.
  • Two years is a short history. 500 sessions back to October 2024: "oversold against itself" means the lowest tenth of two years, not of a cycle. Nine of eleven sectors at or below the 10th percentile is a real reading of that window and should be read as such.
  • Equal weight, served names only. A sector here is its served members averaged equally, not the sector fund: Energy reads (5.6%) against SPY over 72 sessions on its 25 served names while XLE, cap-weighted, is up about 15% over the same window. Both are true; the page says which one it is.
  • FMP's industries are fine-grained and sometimes odd. Block (XYZ), CoreWeave and Core Scientific sit in Software – Infrastructure; 65 of 96 groups are thin. The chosen groups are right by the stated rule; the rule's grain is a decision for Alan (below).
  • The comps upsides include the outliers. MU 211%, ORCL 426%, CHTR 432%, SIMO 578%: these are the comps' arithmetic on a company whose multiples sit far below its peers' medians, kept in as asked. They decide many games today (three of the ten comparisons are upside, and A and B share a centre, so upside effectively counts three times). The first decision below is about that.
  • Blanks advance. UCTT, XYZ and TOST have no margin or net-debt figures on file and still advance, because a blank is not a loss and they win the rows they do have. The three-played floor stops a name with only a Geiger from drawing its way up; it does not stop a name with half the rows. A higher floor is a toggle.
  • Dates differ by a day. Prices are the 2 October quote at build time; the Geiger and the bars are the 1 October close; fundamentals carry their own dates on the comps read.
  • The swipe's owner. Rows carry the placeholder owner and set_by alan, like the COMPS tab's, until the operator authenticates.

Decisions for Alan at most five, each with a recommendation

  1. Which comparisons decide a pairing. Today ten: upside by A, B and C; revenue and EPS growth next FY; operating and FCF margin; net debt over EBITDA; the Geiger; the stretch. A and B share a centre, so upside counts three times out of ten and an outlier upside carries a name a long way (CHTR, SIMO). Recommendation: keep the ten as the menu, set the baseline to upside A and upside C on, upside B off (two independent votes on value, not three), everything else as is. It is one toggle on the page; the tests cover it.
  2. How blanks are handled. A missing number is not played and a game needs three comparisons played. UCTT, XYZ and TOST advance with no margin or net-debt figure on file. Recommendation: raise the floor to five played so a name must show at least half the field to take a game; or, stricter, require the two margin rows to be on file to advance. I would take five.
  3. The grain of a group. FMP's industries leave 65 of 96 groups with fewer than five served names, and odd placements (Block in Software – Infrastructure). Recommendation: keep the FMP industry as the group for the comps (it is the standard's rule) and, for the heat and the field, fold thin industries into their sector's "rest of sector" group so every sector's names can run a knockout; a later lane, not a toggle.
  4. How the heat picks the groups. The page chooses by the Geiger now (rotation breaks ties). Semiconductors ranks first on Geiger and 82nd of 96 on 72-session rotation; by the sum of the two ranks the leaders today would be Software – Infrastructure, Medical – Diagnostics & Research and Software – Application, with Semiconductors and Computer Hardware out. The sector sentence already uses the sum. Recommendation: use the sum of the Geiger rank and the rotation rank for the group choice too (heat × rotation, as the 25 Sep pipe says), so the field and the sentence follow one rule.
  5. The allocation baselines. Cash 25%; 3 : 2 : 1 across the leaders, 1 for the oversold leg; a group at or above the 90th percentile of its own history keeps half its share; finalists equal; a PASS to cash. Recommendation: accept these as the first baselines and run the page for a week of mornings before changing any; the only one I would move now is the oversold leg, which at weight 1 of 7 gives a (0.65) group the same money as the third leader — 0.5 reads better to me, and it is a toggle.
