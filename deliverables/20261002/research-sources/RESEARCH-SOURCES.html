00 · THE SHORT VERSION

1 · IPO lock-ups

Neither FMP nor Massive carries the lock-up date. Both carry the IPO date. The lock-up term lives only in the prospectus text, which FMP links to and the SEC serves for free: one fetch per name reads it (ARM, Astera, CoreWeave, Cerebras: "180 days after the date of this prospectus"). Two unlocks matter now: Cerebras (CBRS) by 10 Nov 2026 — earlier if its Q3 earnings come first — and SpaceX (SPCX) ~9 Dec 2026 (180 days assumed; clause not isolated yet).

2 · Dilution announcements

The signal exists and we are not watching it. FMP lists every prospectus supplement (form 424B5) the day it is filed: 30 filings by 12 of our names in the last 30 days. But a 424B5 is also how bonds are sold, so the filing's first page has to be read (free, SEC): that sorted them into CoreWeave — up to 35 M new shares (17 Sep), Axon — $1 B convertible, Sysco — a stock sale on 15 Sep then bonds, Kroger/Amazon/FedEx/Aon/nVent — bonds only, ONEOK/Corning/Oklo — "at-the-market" share programmes.

3 · Analyst revisions and the DCF

FMP has the revision stream we do not show: every target change by firm, with the price that day (Micron: CLSA $1,700 this morning at $1,097), plus monthly rating counts. Estimates reach four fiscal years out but thin fast (Micron FY2030 revenue rests on 11 analysts, EPS on 5). Massive has no estimates at all on our plan (Benzinga 403) — only SEC actuals, on a route being retired 9 Oct 2026. FMP's own DCF extrapolates last year's growth forever (Micron: +70 %/yr to 2031, beta 4.0) — that is why it looks like garbage.

The topic rule, in one paragraph

A market earns a card when it passes one of three gates, and keeps it while our own reel keeps talking about it. SCHEDULED: it prices a release that sits on the ECONOMIC tape — CPI, core CPI, PCE, payrolls, GDP, the Fed, ECB and BoJ meetings — mapped by Polymarket series so it rolls to the next print by itself (nine topics today; payrolls is the one missing). SERVED: its title names an instrument we serve — Bitcoin, Ethereum, gold, WTI, the S&P, the 10- and 30-year, "largest company", a megacap by name — and it is shown on that instrument's page as well as on the master tab (today Polymarket has 11 open Bitcoin markets and 7 Ethereum; we track none; single stocks are rare on Polymarket beyond "largest company" and "best AI model"). REEL: a geopolitical or policy theme whose headline count in our 7-day reel is at least 100 — today China/Taiwan 225, Iran 148, the AI bubble 280 pass; tariffs (27), Russia–Ukraine (20), the ECB (19) and the yen (14) would sit in a quieter "watching" row rather than disappear, because Alan's rule stands: enduring topics roll forward and never vanish when one question ends. Everything else — Brazil, Nobel, aliens, tweet counts, temperatures — stays out of the cards and lives only in a one-line "what Polymarket is trading" footer. The gate is measured nightly from the news table (one SQL) and the count is printed on each card, so a topic's place is a number, not an opinion.

06 · WHAT I DID NOT DO, AND WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • Nothing was bought, enabled, deployed or written to a table. Two throw-away Fly machines ran the probes and were destroyed; the keys were read from the machine's environment and never printed (the raw files were scrubbed and checked).
  • SpaceX's lock-up term was not isolated: the regex found three "lock-up" mentions in the first 4 MB of its prospectus but no day count. "180 days" for SPCX is an assumption until someone reads the Shares Eligible for Future Sale section. Reddit's day count was not isolated either (its unlock is long past).
  • The 424B5 classifier was run on 12 filings, not on history. Zeta came out "unclassified"; a fifth pattern (or a human glance) is needed before the alert is trusted blind.
  • Two of our names on the 2026 IPO calendar were found; names that IPO'd and were admitted under a different symbol would not be. The universe used is public.tickers where active (612 rows, 364 stocks).
  • FMP's 12-month IPO calendar silently truncated (380 rows, October only). I did not find the documented cap; month-by-month calls are the safe form.
  • Massive's new financials routes were called with a sort parameter they do not accept; the rows came back oldest-first (2010). The fields are real; the ordering needs the documented parameter.
  • The news-reel scan is keyword counting on titles (regular expressions), good enough to rank topics, not a sentiment read. Counts include duplicates across the Google, Investing.com and FMP feeds (31,294 distinct titles of 35,592 rows).
  • Not verified: Polymarket's data-use terms (still unread, as on 28 Sep); Kalshi's remain the open question.
