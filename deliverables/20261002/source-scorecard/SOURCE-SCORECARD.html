Which comp-set source is better?

Alan, 2 Oct: I read it: one source, two, three, four. Is there any gauge of which sources are better — which get to the same things, better or worse? This page is that gauge. The four sources that feed a comp set were measured on all 437 served companies that have a comp set, against the ten names the rule keeps. Nothing was changed: not the rule, not a table, not the tree.
The answer. Massive's related companies is the sharpest (49% of the served names it offers end in the kept ten), FMP's peer list just behind (47%); when the two lists agree on a name it is kept 85% of the time. A shared industry fund is the widest net (28 names per company on average) and mostly noise: 17% of its names are kept and 72% are in another industry. The same industry is the gate, not a voter: every kept name is in it by construction, and 711 of the 3,061 kept (23%) were found by it alone — names no list and no fund offered. FMP's list reaches furthest outside the Hub (55% of its names have no figures here, against 37% for Massive); Massive is missing for 61 companies and 136 of the 138 served funds.

How to read it. Kept share is precision: of the names a source offers for a company (counting only names with figures on the Hub, because a name without figures can never be kept), the share that ends in the kept ten. Pass the cuts is the share that is in the company's industry and inside the size band — what survives before the nearest-ten cut. Agreement with the others is the share of a source's names that at least one other source also offers. The industry source offers every served name in the industry; where an industry has forty names on the Hub it can keep at most ten of them, so its kept share is capped by arithmetic — read its pass the cuts column (70%) as its quality.

What it says. Agreement is the strongest signal there is: a name all four sources offer is kept 99% of the time, three sources 83%, two 31%, one 6%. Read the other way, the kept ten are mostly two- and three-vote names (37% and 28%); 23% of the kept names were offered by one source only, and that one source is always the industry (the only source that can offer a name on its own and still pass the industry gate). Neither provider list nor the fund ever finds a kept name alone — by construction, because the rule keeps same-industry names only and the industry source offers every one of them. So the honest reading is: the industry test decides what can be kept; FMP, Massive and the fund decide the order.

8 · The method in plain words

  1. The companies. The 590 names the Hub serves, less the 138 funds (a fund has no industry and no comp set) = 452 companies; 437 of them end with at least one kept name.
  2. The four sources, as C4 names them. FMP's peer list (public.peer_sources, source fmp, plus public.fmp_peers); Massive's related companies (public.peer_sources, source massive); the same industry (public.ticker_industry: every served company with the same FMP industry, or the same SIC code); a shared industry fund (the tree's fund nodes with at most 60 holdings — broad and style funds do not count — every served holding of a fund that holds the company). All read-only, all dated snapshots committed beside this page.
  3. The rule, unchanged. the standard's rule, unchanged: same industry by the authority (FMP industry, or the same SIC) → market value inside ÷10…×10 (widened to ×30, then any, when fewer than 5 survive) → ranked by votes then closeness → the nearest 10 kept. It is the standard's own selection from derive.mjs, re-stated in scorecard.mjs and tested to reproduce the standard's six worked sets exactly.
  4. Kept share (precision). For one company and one source: the names it offers that have figures on the Hub, and of those, the share in the kept ten. Pooled over all companies: total kept ÷ total offered. The median per company is in the JSON.
  5. Overlap (agreement). For a pair of sources on one company: of all the names either offers (with figures), the share both offer — the Jaccard index, in plain words. Pooled over the companies both cover, and the typical (median) company. "Kept of names both offer" counts the names both sources offered and how many were kept.
  6. Reach. A company is covered by a source when the source offers at least one name for it. Names per company counts every name offered, empties as zero.
  7. Provenance. Every kept name across the Hub, with how many of the four sources offered it (its ring on the cone) and which ones. "Found alone" = kept names only one source offered.
  8. Against the authority. A name with figures that a source offers whose FMP industry is not the company's and whose SIC is not the company's. The industry cut removes exactly these.
  9. By industry. The pooled kept shares again, inside each FMP industry; thin = fewer than five served names in the industry.

9 · What could be wrong

  • The measure rewards agreement with the rule, not truth. "Kept" means the rule kept it. If the rule is wrong somewhere, a source that agrees with the rule looks better than one that is right. The rule was decided on 2 Oct and this page does not question it.
  • The industry source cannot lose. Every kept name is in it by construction. Its kept share is capped by the size of the industry on the Hub (at most ten of forty); compare sources on pass the cuts and on found alone, not on kept share alone.
  • The provider lists are penalised for reaching outside the Hub. FMP offers 55% names with no figures here; these may be excellent peers. Admitting them (the standard's decision 2) would change the lists' scores.
  • The SIC is on 417 of 452 companies. Where it is missing, "same industry" is the FMP industry alone; where it is present it widens the industry test. Loading the rest changes a few sets at the edges.
  • One day's snapshots. Lists of 2026-10-01, industry of 2026-10-02, market values of 2026-10-01, fund holdings of 26 Sep. Market value moves the band; the lists change when the providers refresh them.
  • Massive's reach is partial. It is missing for 61 companies; its kept share is measured on the companies it does cover, which may be the easier ones.
  • Fund membership depends on the tree's fund list. Only funds on the tree with at most 60 served holdings count as industry funds; a missing fund lowers the fund source's reach, not its quality.

10 · What was not done

  • No source's vote was re-weighted and the rule was not changed; this page measures, it does not decide. A weak source's vote counting less is a decision for Alan (the recommendation is: not yet — measure first, which this is).
  • Nothing was written to a table, nothing deployed, no key used: the lists came by read-only SQL into a dated snapshot beside this page.
  • The screener's unserved names (decision 2) were not admitted or scored.
  • The measures were not repeated on an older snapshot, so there is no "before" to compare against; this page is the first reading.
