How many names each fund and sector needs on the Hub, what could move off-Hub, and the IWM rule

Alan, 2 Oct: I feel I over-added companies to the Hub that could be off-Hub. We should go through this at a parent level — ETF level, sector level — tracking enough tickers for each parent that the aggregate is statistically strong enough; weight matters where it makes sense. IWM is so spread out — I would put the IWM names that belong to other areas where they coincide. That's the rule.

The short answer. Measured the brief's way (every parent's bar within ±0.05 of the bar ALL its holdings would give), the Hub is not over-served — it is under-served: the equal-weighted industry funds (banks KBE, biotech IBB, retail XRT, pharma XPH, low-vol SPLV, drillers XOP, builders XHB) cannot be pinned by any small top-N, and they alone would ask for about 400 more names. Only 36 names on the Hub today are needed by no parent, no list and no cohort. Today's served names already give 42 of 51 measured parents a bar within ±0.05 of the full one on the close, because the served names are the heavy ones.

The proposal is the rule in section 5 with a tolerance of ±0.10 and a cap of 30 names per parent: a parent that needs more than 30 names is read off-Hub, nightly, from all its holdings (exact, from the seven-rung close that already covers 5,560 instruments) instead of admitting dozens of live lines; its own fund line stays on the Hub as its bar. That gives 590 → 574 lines (95 out, 77 in). The brief's literal setting (±0.05, no cap) would give 590 → 1012. The three decisions are at the end.

Why the tolerance matters so much: inside one fund the Geigers spread about ±0.4 (the last column of the table in section 3 is each parent's spread). To pin an average to ±0.05 takes roughly (0.4 / 0.05)² ≈ 64 names; to ±0.10 about 16. That is why ±0.05 asks for a bigger Hub and ±0.10 lets it shrink. The equal-weight definition always asks for more, because the biggest holdings are not a fair sample of an equal-weight average.

5 · The rule numbered, plain lines — proposed, nothing applied

  1. The parents are the funds on the tree that have a holdings file (51 today, FMP holdings of 26 Sep) and the eleven sector headings, each read through its sector fund (XLK, XLF, XLV, XLE, XLI, XLP, XLY, XLU, XLB, XLRE, XLC).
  2. Every parent gets a measured N: the smallest N on the ladder 5/8/10/15/20/30/40/50/75/100 whose top-N-by-weight aggregate stays within 0.05 of the aggregate of ALL its holdings — on the seven-rung close reading and on at least 90% of the extra draws (the seven rungs of that close and the five daily three-rung closes; 12 draws today, days as they accumulate).
  3. A name is served live on the Hub if it is inside the top-N of a parent (by weight), or on LIKED / FAVORITES / RADAR, or a member of an adopted cohort (the registry, step 1).
  4. IWM admits nothing by itself: an IWM name is served only where another parent, a list or a cohort places it (Alan, 2 Oct: 'put the IWM names that belong to other areas where they coincide'). The same holds for every parent the ladder cannot reproduce (its N is 'more than 100'): it is spread out the way IWM is, its names are served where other parents need them, its own line stays on the Hub as its bar, and its full aggregate is read off-Hub nightly.
  5. Every fund on the tree stays served: the parents are Hub lines in their own right, and their own Geiger is the check on the aggregate.
  6. Everything else is off-Hub: the nightly seven-rung close reading covers it (5,560 instruments today), the tree shows it there, and nothing is lost — it is read once a day instead of live.
  7. Re-measured nightly after the seven-rung run. A name changes side only when its status has held for five consecutive closes, so the Hub does not churn on one day's reading (this guard is the builder's addition, not Alan's words).
  8. A fund with no holdings file (87 today) keeps its served names as they are until its holdings are fetched; it is listed, not guessed.
The recommended setting of the rule: tolerance ±0.10, the weight-blended aggregate, and a cap of 30 names per parent — a parent whose strong-N is above 30 is a spread-out parent under line 4 (read off-Hub nightly, exact, from all its holdings; its own line stays on the Hub as its bar). With that setting the spread-out parents today are: SPY, VTI, ITOT, IWV, RSP, QQQ, QQEW, MDY, IJR, VTV, QUAL, VT, KBE, IBB, XPH, XRT.

  • The holdings are a week old (26 Sep) and FMP's file lists part of some funds (VT is capped at 4,000 lines; DRAM lists 9 of 26). Weights are used as listed, re-scaled over the readable lines; nothing is filled in.
  • Readings of −1…+1 at one close are a single day's state. The strong-N moves with the market's dispersion; the rule re-measures nightly and the five-close guard (line 7) is there so one day cannot move a name.
What was not done. Nothing live was changed: no table, no universe, no registry, no tree, no admission or removal. The 87 funds without holdings were not measured. No name was actually moved — the lists in section 6 are proposals. The "9 days out of 10" test proper was not possible with one seven-rung close; it was stood in for by 12 draws, as said above.

9 · The three decisions each with the recommendation

1 · The tolerance. ±0.05 (the brief) makes the Hub grow to 1012 lines, or 627 with the cap; ±0.10 gives 867 without the cap and 574 with it; ±0.15 gives 548 with the cap. Recommendation: ±0.10 with the cap of 30. A tenth of the Geiger's range is about what the eye reads as "the same bar"; the cap keeps the live Hub the size it is while every spread-out parent still gets its exact bar nightly off-Hub.
2 · Weight-blended or equal-weight. Weight-blended (bigger holdings count more) is what a fund's own price does, needs fewer names everywhere (section 3), and today's served names already pass it for 42 of 51 parents. Equal-weight treats every line alike and asks for 627 lines at ±0.10 with the cap. Recommendation: weight-blended for every cap-weighted parent; equal-weight only where the fund itself is equal-weighted (RSP, QQEW, the RSPx family, XRT, XPH, KBE, SPLV) — which the cap already sends off-Hub.
3 · Alan's lists. LIKED (141), FAVORITES (57) and RADAR (17) are kept served whatever the parents say; they add 2 names today that no parent needs (BTCUSD, GCUSD). Recommendation: keep all three as an unconditional "serve" — they are Alan's hand, and 0 of the out-candidates would otherwise be on a list. The alternative (only FAVORITES and RADAR hold a name on the Hub; LIKED does not) is a smaller Hub by a few dozen names and is one line to change in the builder.
