03 · The topic method — our own news decides the list

Alan: "We never agreed on the topics … we need a methodology that scans the news from our streams, makes a list and tracks it." The method, in plain words:

  1. Read every headline of the last 7 days from our own news reel (public.news — tonight 36,174 headlines, newest 2026-10-03 01:35Z).
  2. Count headlines per theme. A theme is a short list of words (the Fed, inflation, payrolls, yields, the S&P, gold, oil, Iran, China–Taiwan, tariffs, Russia–Ukraine, the midterms, the shutdown, US debt, AI and data centres, the ECB, the yen, Bitcoin, Ethereum; plus four company-action themes that are counted but never proposed: earnings, price targets, IPOs and lock-ups, offerings). The theme names are R1's; R1's exact word lists were not saved with its page, so the lists are this lane's, printed in supabase/functions/prediction-topic-proposals/lib.mjs, and every count is printed with its proposal.
  3. A theme is heavy at 100 headlines or more in 7 days, quiet under 20 (R1's floor and retirement line).
  4. ADD: a heavy theme, no topic tracks it, and Polymarket's public search (no key) finds an open market that passes the exclusion rule — the market in a series is preferred, because it rolls to the next question by itself. REMOVE: a tracked topic whose theme is quiet and which has no live market reading; or a tracked topic that maps to no Polymarket market at all (Kalshi-only) while Kalshi is off, because it can never have a reading. A topic with a live market is never proposed for removal on news alone — Alan's rule that enduring topics roll forward stands.
  5. The exclusion rule, one sentence: markets about weather or temperatures, sports and games, tweet counts, prizes and celebrity novelties are left out whatever their volume, because none of them bears on a position. (Tonight's most-traded Polymarket markets include the Brazil election, college football games, the Ballon d'Or and an Elon Musk tweet count — all out by rule.)
  6. The method writes proposals, never the registry. Alan approves; topics.json is edited by hand; an approved topic keeps its first id so its history stays joined.

04 · Two things Alan asked

"Two threads on the same topic." Polymarket sometimes lists the same question again under a new id. The SPY "hit $770 in October" market was one id until 09:45 on 2 Oct and a new id from 13:00. The collector keeps both, so the history is whole and nothing is lost; the page shows one line per question and takes the newest reading, so the same question is never printed twice. When a topic truly has two live questions — the Fed's next meeting and the one after — both are rows, as Alan said: "fine, we track them both."

"The yesterday / three-day rule." Yesterday: the change on every row is today's odds minus the odds at yesterday's 16:00 ET close — the same clock the stock market closes on, so a prediction market's day is a stock's day. Usual day: we take that market's last 20 weekdays, measure how far it moved each day, and keep the middle one (never less than one point). Three days: until a market has three such days we do not claim to know its usual move, so it cannot flash — one or two days is a guess, not a habit. The ◆ means today's move is bigger than its usual day; the hover prints "N× its usual day" (never a sigma). The rule is printed in one sentence at the top of the section.

06 · What could be wrong, and what was not done

  • The theme word lists are this lane's, not R1's; counts differ from R1's page by a few percent (R1: Gold 271, tonight 259; AI 280 → 420 because "artificial intelligence" and "AI capex" are in the list). The lists are in one file and easy to tune.
  • Headlines are counted once per theme they match; a headline can count in two themes (a gold-and-yields headline counts in both). Duplicates across sites count; the distinct-title count is beside every number.
  • Polymarket's public search returns the twelve best hits for a phrase; a market phrased unusually can be missed. The payrolls stand-in (unemployment rate) is such a case and is flagged.
  • The venue link on a row arrives a few seconds after the rows (one public read per event, ~45 of them); until then the venue is named without a link. In the walk the links had arrived in the pane but not yet on the rows when the shot was taken.
  • Not done: nothing deployed, no table created (the migration and its rollback wait for the coordinator), the function not scheduled (a nightly run is suggested, no cron changed), the proposals not written to a table (the section shows the dry-run file and says so). Kalshi stays off. The old catalyst-odds collector was not touched (Alan: "your call" — recommendation below).
  • Not verified: the section against the live table once installed; the phone pane shot is the collapsed header only (the element shot clipped to the rail's first lines).
