WHAT THIS PAGE IS

Alan, 7 October: "Why is there inconsistencies everywhere on the forward P/E … if that's the right one, it should be everywhere", and "I need to see Google, Amazon and Broadcom — what the comps would look like … full treatment, comps, everything measured against the other leaders, critiquing our comps processes … TSMC is on radar, so that one too … debt should matter." This page is the answer, on a branch. Nothing on it is live on the Hub or in the allocation tool.

WHY THE NUMBERS DISAGREED

The dashboard divides the price by the next four quarters of expected earnings. The comps system, the decision cards and the feed the allocation tool reads divided it by the estimate for the fiscal year in progress. For a company whose year ends in December, that year is three quarters reported by October. Alphabet's 2026 carries about $8.92 a share of paper gains on its stakes, so it read 16.9 times in the comps while the dashboard said 24. A fast grower looked dearer than it is: Broadcom read 32.2 times on its fiscal 2026 and reads 21.6 on the next four quarters. The cards used a third figure, a blend of two fiscal years: 21.2 for Alphabet. Three numbers for one company.

THE ONE RULE

  • Forward P/E is the price over the next four quarterly earnings estimates, added up. That is the dashboard's rule, unchanged; it is now written once and the comps tab, the cards, the knockout and the feed read it from there.
  • Only when fewer than four quarters are on file does the nearest fiscal year stand in (ARGX, BNY, CRH, EH, SIDU today).
  • Estimates kept in another currency are put in dollars at the supplier's own rate for the same quarter, or the multiple is left out and says why. Never mixed.
  • Growth is the four quarters after those, over those four. P/E ÷ growth uses that same P/E and that same growth, so it is one number too.

THE ONE-BASIS TABLE

30 of 30 names print the same multiple on the dashboard, the comps tab, the card and the feed. Each column is that screen's own program run on the same rows and the same price: the dashboard's column is the code cut out of the Hub page as it stands. The tool column is what the allocation tool prints from the feed. A test repeats this, and a second test holds the dashboard's code and the shared rule to each other on 400 made-up companies. On the live screens the dashboard moves with the price during the day while the feed and the cards stay on the last close, so they differ by the day's move and by nothing else.

THE SET CHECK

Every set is now checked automatically, so a wrong set is caught without anyone maintaining it by hand. Two readings: how many of the peers the four sources kept share the company's business (flagged under half), and how far the peers that price it are from its size (flagged when the middle one is under a tenth or over ten times, or the largest is over a hundred times the smallest). The first would have flagged Micron (2 of 12), Oracle (2 of 12), Nvidia (3 of 12), TSMC (3 of 12) and Vistra (4 of 12) before anyone looked. The second flags Alphabet today: its ad-platform peers are about a fortieth of its size. A flagged set is still priced; the flag goes on the card.

PEER SETS

The four sources still vote (the dots beside each peer: FMP's peers, Massive's related companies, the same industry, a shared fund), and no peer was removed from any set. What changed is who sets the price: the peers that do the same business. For Alphabet, Amazon, Broadcom, Nvidia, TSMC, Vistra and Micron the same-business peers are named from the 7 October brief. For Oracle the list is this page's own proposal. For every other company, the same-business peers price it when the set holds four or more of them; with fewer, the whole set prices it as before. At three the rule made 50 companies thin, at four 9, which is why it is four.

WHAT EACH CORE PANEL SHOWS

Each panel leads with the blend, not with one metric: the comps range is every yardstick together — trailing P/E, forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/sales, price/sales and P/E ÷ growth (price over funds from operations for a property trust). The table under it gives each yardstick's own row: the company's multiple, its peers' median, the price that median implies, and the weight the yardstick carries. A yardstick is left out when the company's margins are more than twice its peers' apart.

Growth is shown two ways, because one number misled on Micron: earnings of the last twelve months as reported → the next four quarters (Micron +135%), and the next four quarters → the four after (+22%). The first tells how much of the growth is already in the forward P/E; the second is the growth the P/E ÷ growth yardstick uses. Where the reported year carries one-off gains (Alphabet, Amazon) the first reading is distorted and the card says so.

The growth credit is said on every panel whether it applies or not. Vistra gets none against the independent power producers: it grows 20% into the following year against their 27%. It earned a credit only against regulated utilities, which no longer price it.

Micron and the rest of its line are also read three ways: on the US-listed peers only (+90%), with SK hynix added (+51%), and with Samsung and Kioxia too (+13%).

The four steps under the range are the same company read four ways: as the comps system stands on the Hub today; with the 6 October fixes on the old fiscal-year basis (what the cards and the knockout carried); the same on the one basis; and on the same-business peers. The last is the proposal. The dots show every peer's forward P/E on one scale; filled dots price the company, hollow ones are shown and not priced.

A CRITIQUE OF OUR COMPS PROCESS

  • The set decides more than the method. Micron read +324% against chip designers and equipment makers, +99% against SanDisk, Western Digital and Seagate, and +13% once SK hynix, Samsung and Kioxia are in. Nvidia reads +54% on its old set and +136% on five chip designers, because AMD, Marvell and Arm trade at 48 to 117 times. That is the market paying more for smaller designers, not a price target for Nvidia.
  • The basis moved more than half the universe. 240 of 428 comps readings move by more than five points on the one basis, 82 by more than twenty.
  • Thin sets stay thin. Vistra has three peers, the two data-centre landlords have two each. A range from two or three companies is a comparison, not a valuation.
  • A property trust's P/E is on reported earnings. Digital Realty reads 76 times and Equinix 58 on earnings; they are priced on funds from operations instead, and that figure is approximated from the statements we hold.
  • The stored EBITDA carries paper gains for Alphabet and Amazon, so their EV/EBITDA and their debt ratio look better than they are.
  • Amazon is two businesses and we cannot split its profit. The two legs are shown side by side; the blend is by peer, not by each leg's profit, because segment profit is not in our data. With Alibaba, JD and PDD priced in (converted from yuan) Amazon reads +25%; without them it read +55%, so three Chinese retailers on 6 to 12 times earnings move it by thirty points.
  • Some estimate rows are wrong at the source. 110 estimates are exactly zero and 92 future years (61 companies) have quarters that do not add up to the year. Amazon's 2028 quarters add to 23.38 against a yearly 13.86. The rule keeps them out of growth; for 12 companies they sit inside the next four quarters, and those multiples should not be leaned on.

DEBT

Every card now carries net debt over the last twelve months' EBITDA, with a word for it, and the years of free cash flow it would take to repay all the debt. Interest cover reads "not on file" on every card: the statements we store have no interest line. One added column would give it; the change is written and not applied.

In the knockout debt never decides who passes the fundamentals round. Among the names that passed, it takes points off the score they are ranked on: nothing up to 2.5 times, 0.03 up to 4, 0.06 up to 6, 0.10 above or when there is debt and no positive EBITDA. Banks, insurers and asset managers are not read. It reordered the finalists of 17 branches; 89 names lose points. The steps are a proposal.

THE KNOCKOUT

The universe knockout was first reproduced on its own basis from today's tables: 63 of 63 published champions came out the same. It was then re-run on the one basis, with the same-business sets, and with debt. 13 champions change and 44 names change side in the fundamentals round. Growth, revisions, cash and timing are the knockout's own readings of the 6 October close, unchanged.

IN THE ALLOCATION TOOL

On its own branch the tool reads these cards. Its picks and its knockout print the same forward P/E; a new step between the knockout and the picks lists the ten core candidates with their comps range, the peers that price each one and its debt; one line under the brief names them with their forward P/E and their distance to the centre; each pick shows the peers that price it. With a live price the tool divides that price by the card's earnings, so it moves with the dashboard through the day.

On the branch a stored price older than four days is not used, which is the Hub's own rule; the figures then stand on the card's close and say so. The pictures here were taken in that state, so they are on the 6 October close.

WHERE EACH NUMBER COMES FROM

  • Prices: the 6 October regular-session close from the chart service.
  • Estimates: the Hub's analyst estimates table, read 2026-10-07 14:45 UTC. Statements, balance sheets and cash flows: the Hub's tables as read at 05:50 UTC on 7 October for the universe knockout.
  • SK hynix, Samsung and Kioxia: figures supplied from FMP at 2026-10-07 14:25 UTC, in won and yen, put in dollars at that day's rate. They carry no quarterly estimates, so their next twelve months are a blend of their two nearest fiscal years by days.
  • Geiger readings: the decision cards', from the 6 October close.

WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • TSMC's estimates are in Taiwan dollars per US share. At the supplier's own rate (31.7 to the dollar) it reads 23.1 times; at 30.5 it would read 22.2.
  • Vistra's, Talen's and NRG's quarterly estimates rest on one analyst for three of the four quarters. Their yearly estimates, with seven to nine analysts, agree within a few percent.
  • The foreign memory makers' multiples depend on which year is used: SK hynix is 4.8 times on 2026 alone, 3.6 on 2027 alone, 3.8 on the blend.
  • A statement table refreshed between 05:50 UTC and the estimates read could leave a few names on mixed vintages.
  • The five names on the fiscal-year fallback still carry a year partly reported; the dashboard does the same today.
  • For a growth company with little profit the debt reading is ruled by a small EBITDA: Cloudflare reads 48 times and loses the full ten points. The pre-profit study should replace this reading for those names.
  • Alphabet drops from 7th to 14th of 17 in its knockout branch, which is consumer internet (Lyft, Uber, DoorDash). Its real peers sit in other branches; that is a fault of the branch, not a verdict on Alphabet.

WHAT WAS NOT DONE

  • Nothing was deployed and no table was written. The feed with the one basis is ready as one command for the coordinator.
  • The dashboard's forward P/E code was not rewired to import the shared rule; it is held to it by tests instead. One thing on the dashboard did change on the branch: a foreign reporter's revenue (TSMC's "in TWD") is now put in dollars at the same rate its forward P/E uses, and stays withheld where there is no agreeing rate.
