2 · The mechanics, stage by stage

There are three voices (news, YouTube, X) and one separate thing (fear & greed). All three voices are built the same way: collect items, look every word up in a list, score the item as the balance of the words that fired, store the item with its words, roll the items up per ticker per day, roll the days up per voice. Nothing in any of it is an AI read. No model reads a headline or a transcript; a word is either on a list or it is not. Every number in this room is a keyword count, not an AI read.

Stage 1 · the word list

The list is a file: data/news-lexicon/lm-headline-v1.json, method lm-v1. It is the Loughran-McDonald finance dictionary (354 positive words, 2,355 negative, built from 10-K filings), plus 31 positive and 36 negative price verbs that headlines use and filings never do (beats, surges, plunges, downgrade), plus 25 negators. Because the dictionary carries 6.6 negative words for every positive one, a score is never a count of hits, always a balance. The sha of the file that scored each row is stored beside the row.

Finding · there are three word lists, not one lm-v1 scores every stored row (the three edge functions). sc-social-v2, a different list living inside the Hub page (38 bullish and 40 bearish plain words, 62 slang words, phrases and emoji, 13 negators), scores the X and YouTube cards, the anatomy ladder and the keyword tracker in the browser. yt-lexicon-v1, a third list inside the Python job, wrote the pooled YouTube roll-up (social_sentiment, 63 rows, last written 23 Sep 00:37Z). So the same X post can carry two different numbers on the same screen, and the keyword tracker you liked counts words from a list the stored rows never used. The feed in section 4 uses the stored rows and one list only.

Stage 2 · one item is scored

Every word in the item is looked up. If a negator sits in the three words before a match, the match is flipped and the flip is recorded. The score is (positive − negative) ÷ (positive + negative), so it always lands between −1 and +1. An item with no listed word gets no score, not a zero. An item that asks a question is scored and then left out of every total. A real headline from today, filed under META:

Meta Stock Fails at the Highs as Muse Demand Shows Capacity Strains and Oppenheimer Flags Monetization – FXLeaderstitle + snippet → words that fired: fails (−1), strains (−1), fails (−1), strains (−1) · positive 0, negative 4 · score (0 − 4) ÷ 4 = −1.00 · not a question · stored 19:20Z with the four words and the sentence they sit in

And one where a negator did its work, because this is the part people doubt:

Fed's Hammack says bond yield surge not about lost inflation confidencesurge (+1, a headline verb) · lost is negative on the list, but "not" sits two words before it, so it is flipped to +1 and marked · score (2 − 0) ÷ 2 = +1.00. Right or wrong, you can see why.

Stage 4 · a ticker's day

All of that ticker's scored items for one New York day and one voice are averaged: sentiment_ticker_daily, keyed by day, ticker and source, carrying the mean, how many items were seen, how many scored, how many leaned up and down, how many had no word, how many asked a question, and the top eight words with a sentence each. This is the row the tape draws, and the row a 90-day history lives in. It is rebuilt from all stored items for that day every time a function touches the day, so it never undercounts because of the slice that happened to be read. Today: NVDA news +0.49 over 34 items, NVDA X +0.22, NVDA YouTube −1.00 over one clip.

Stage 5 · the voice's day

Fear & greed: a separate thing, kept separate

Two gauges are shown that other people publish: CNN's Fear & Greed (today 37, fear; 36 yesterday, 30 a week ago, 60 a month ago; its seven parts today: momentum 36, 52-week strength 0, breadth 0, put/call 58, volatility 50, junk bonds 61, safe havens 54) and alternative.me's index, which is for crypto, not stocks, and the card says so. Beside them the room computes seven market inputs of its own in the browser, from chart-API daily bars, CNN's way: each one is today's distance from its own average in units of how much it normally moves (50 + 25 × that distance, two deviations either way is the end of the dial; volatility and put/call inverted). These seven are not stored: they are recomputed every ten minutes in whichever browser is open and forgotten, which is why they have no history and the CNN card does. None of this is a word count, none of it is sentiment in the voices' sense, and Alan's 23 Sep ruling stands: there is no blended Scintilla fear & greed number.

4 · The redesign: a feed, not a dump

The rule for every piece: small, dense, visual, in the idiom of the keyword tracker. Monochrome, no white, green and red only for direction. One list, the stored rows, no browser scoring. The mockup below is built from today's stored rows by build-mockup.mjs; open mockup-feed.html in this folder to use it.

The full-page captures at both widths are screens/mockup-1680.png and screens/mockup-390.png (no horizontal overflow at either).

The pieces, and what each one is for

  • The strip. One bar per voice, zero in the middle, today's balance as the bar, yesterday's as a notch, the count of scored items beside it, and THIN in red under 30 items. Read left to right in a second: news up a little, X flat, YouTube down on a thin sample.
  • The agreement line. How many names more than one voice talked about, and how many of those disagree. This replaces the "measuring stick" table.
  • Since yesterday. Which voice firmed or softened, which names flipped sign (with the numbers), which names are new today. One line, computed from the daily rows already stored.
  • Fear & greed, its own small card. CNN's number the way CNN prints it (dial, rating, yesterday / week / month, a 30-day line), labelled "theirs, not ours". Our seven market inputs stay in their own tab and are never mixed in.
  • Trending. Ten names by items today: lean, count, one dot per voice in its direction, SPLIT when the voices disagree, a 10-day line from the stored rows, ★ on the board's own names. Tapping a name opens the existing tape.
  • The words that did it. The keyword tracker, unchanged in idiom, one column pair per voice, counted from the stored rows' top words after negation.
  • The feed. The strongest items first (score × words that fired), one story once even when four outlets ran it, a NEWS / VIDEO / POST badge, the tickers as chips, a BULLISH / BEARISH badge with the number, the text with the scoring words in colour and struck through where a negator flipped them, the outlet, handle or channel, the age, and "scored on the title, no transcript yet" where that is true. Filters: all, my list, news, video, posts.

What was borrowed from StockTwits-style feeds, and what was left out

  • Borrowed: a sentiment badge on every post; trending tickers as a short ranked list; a watchlist filter; the ticker as a tappable chip; a "what changed" line as the first thing after the numbers.
  • Left out: the message volume charts (our volume is what the collectors happened to fetch, not the crowd); follower counts and reputation (nobody here has checked whether any voice was right last month, so no voice is ranked); the endless-scroll of unscored chatter (an item with no listed word is not shown as neutral, it is not shown at all); avatars, colour fills, white cards.

6 · What could be wrong

  • A word list cannot hear irony, a hedge or a joke. "Fails at the highs" is bearish; "no longer bearish" is caught by the negator rule; "bearish… just kidding" is not.
  • The dictionary was built from filings. "Despite" scores positive on it and fired 48 times in today's news; "force" scores negative ("Force Majeure" and "workforce" both hit). Every such word is visible in the tracker, which is the point of showing it, but the list has quirks and the supplement of 67 headline verbs is small.
  • One post, many tickers. A post naming five $TAGS casts five votes. It is honest and it is also why X can look busier than it is.
  • Two outlets, one story. The mockup collapses identical titles; the stored rows still count each URL once per ticker, so the daily numbers do not.
  • YouTube is titles until the transcript job runs, and 16 items a day is thin whatever list scores them.
  • Two lists on one screen today. Until the browser stops scoring with sc-social-v2, a post's badge in the feed (lm-v1, stored) can differ from its lean in the X card (browser). The redesign removes the second number; it does not reconcile the two lists.
  • My list is a stand-in. In the mockup, ★ marks ten names typed into the builder; the build reads the board's cohort instead.
