What this is, in one paragraph

A comparable companies analysis that is not a comp statement. One cohort at a time goes down a fixed path: the table (the source, every number with its date) → the field (the football-field view of the comps, on its own, where outliers and filters get knocked out) → the cards (the same table cardified, in the plant-library idiom, with filters) → round 1, fit (the theme you state, the allocation tool's two toggles, and the editable rules under them; a name that fails is greyed with one full sentence) → round 2, comps (a round robin: every name plays every other on every row that plays; the standings seed the elimination, and you knock names out on the field) → round 3, timing (the Geiger with its date, RSI against its own year, days to earnings, sigma events) → the final (PICK / PASS / LATER with a reason, under the sleeve caps and the book-wide hard cap). It ran on UTILITIES, AI POWERTRAIN and the eight Station targets. It is a separate workshop URL under deliverables; nothing is wired into the cohort board or the allocation tab.

The client designer, first

I read the Urth client designer for a measured property (460 SW 21st Rd), the properties list and the plant library before touching the workshop. What the designer does, in order:

  1. It reads the property before asking anything. Sun, water, salt and scorch are measured per square (21,936 squares on that lot), and the page lists which of the 36 possible combinations are actually present. On that flat inland lot only sun varies, so six combinations exist: three bed zones and three lawn zones.
  2. It analyses which themes can carry the property. A table counts, per theme and per zone, how many plants of that theme actually thrive there. Deep shade is the gate: "tropical-lush" and "colorful" fill every zone; "low-maintenance" and "coastal" cannot fill deep shade. The page says out loud that "cannot fill" is spot-specific, often fixable by swapping a plant or two, and a judgement to surface rather than an automatic ban. The picker only offers themes that can fill every zone, but the full numbers stay visible and you can override.
  3. The client states the theme they want (Alan's correction of 25 Sep: the theme is the one the client wants, not "whichever fills every zone"). Then they fill each spot under per-layer caps: up to 5 trees, 5 shrubs, 4 accents, 6 groundcovers, 2 climbers per spot. The caps keep one spot from getting busy; they do not limit the property.
  4. One property-wide rule narrows the options as you go: at most three colours across the whole yard. A tile that would add a fourth colour is flagged before you pick it; the palette so far is shown in the bar the whole time.
  5. The choice is saved as the property's look: a named look (theme + picks per spot + grass) goes to the browser's storage, and "Send my picks" hands it over. The look is the property's theme from then on.
  6. For an existing property the order reverses: the camera scan identifies the species already there, that drives the theme, and the client gives less input.

What I borrowed, and where it sits in the workshop. Round 1 opens with state the theme: four themes on file (the AI trade · power for AI · defensive while the indexes are stretched · mega caps that already earn), each a set of the Hub's own cohort tags, each button carrying "n of N fit" and a verdict in the designer's idiom (fills the large-cap sleeve · thin: 2 large caps for a sleeve of 4 · cannot fill the large-cap sleeve); the numbers behind the buttons are in a table beneath, and a thin theme can still be chosen. The per-layer caps became the allocation tool's per-sleeve caps (4 large, 2 small, read from its DEFAULTS on 26 Sep), and the "≤ 3 colours" rule became the tool's hard cap on total names (12): the final shows "picks so far · large-cap sleeve 1 of 4 · small-cap sleeve 0 of 2 · book-wide 1 of 12" as the designer shows the palette so far, and a PICK that would break a cap is refused with a sentence. The reverse direction reads your LIKED list first and says, in a sentence, which theme it is telling you. The save is the same idea: theme, knockouts and decisions go to this browser's storage per group, and the proposed operator_decisions / operator_preferences tables are the "send my picks" the coordinator would wire later.

What felt right and what did not

  • Which round did the work. On a small cohort (AI POWERTRAIN, 8) round 1 did it: four of eight out on rules and one more on the field, before any comparison. On a large cohort (UTILITIES, 21) the round robin did it: the rules only removed two, and the standings turned twenty look-alikes into an order. On the hand list the field did it (BE) and round 3 changed the answer (MU's report in three sessions). So the path earns its three rounds; none is decorative.
  • The round robin is the right group stage. Points are plain (a win is one, a draw a half, a game with a missing number is not played), the per-row wins say why a name leads, and the head-to-head is there when wanted. It knocks nobody out, which is right: the operator does that on the field, with the seeds as the guide.
  • The field is where the outliers show, and also where it gets crowded. With one name at +540% the other nineteen share 4% of the track. That is the field doing its job (it makes the outlier impossible to miss), but the labels overprint until it is knocked out. I stagger labels over three rows; a bunched row still needs the knockout.
  • Noise. "P/E, adjusted" exists for 3 of 21 utilities and duplicates the trailing row elsewhere; it sits out of the round robin by default. "Revenue, TTM" and "Market value" describe rather than judge and never play. "Revenue growth, next FY est" is noise on a pre-revenue name (SMR +540% on $3.7M of estimated revenue): the sentence under the number says so, and the earnings rule removes those names anyway. The Geiger row on the field is timing, not comps, and on the board's source it is 33 days old: it sits out of the round robin and lives in round 3 with its date beside it.
  • Forward growth as first-class rows works. The two estimate rows (EPS growth next FY, room to the analyst target) are what separated GOOGL and AMZN (EPS falling next year) from MU and AVGO (EPS rising), which the trailing multiple hides. P/E on the FY estimate is the single most useful row on the field.
  • The theme step narrows a mixed list, not a pure cohort. On UTILITIES every name fits "defensive" through the cohort's own tag; on the eight targets the theme is a real fork ("the AI trade" would grey WMT and VST). The theme analysis table is worth keeping for the weekend runs, where cohorts mix.
  • What did not feel right. The Geiger's own 1-year percentile is not reachable (no Geiger history is stored), so round 3 leans on RSI(14) from the statistics package as the stand-in. And the small-cap sleeve never fills under LARGE ONLY, so half the caps bar is empty by construction; that is the toggle doing what Alan asked, but it reads odd.

Saving

The workshop writes to this browser only: preferences (k1.prefs), the theme per group, the knockouts per group, and the decisions per group, each with the heat at the moment and the date. DOWNLOAD THIS RUN gives the same as a JSON file. The proposed tables are in supabase/migrations/20260925_operator_decisions.sql: operator_decisions (ticker, cohort, decision, reason, heat_at, made_at, expires_on) and operator_preferences (key, value, reason, updated_at), additive only, rollback written, no grants (the anon key must not write them until the coordinator decides how the operator authenticates). The migration is not applied; the coordinator does that. The reasons to mark: the cap floor reason, the earnings reason, one reason per theme marked OUT or CARE, and one sentence per decision that you would still agree with in a month.

What could be wrong

  • The Geiger is a month old on the board's source. Every composite_staged row read (all three scenarios, SPY and IWM too) is stamped 24 Aug 2026. The page prints the stamp and the age beside every reading, and the split it drives is labelled likewise; but any conclusion that leans on "the coldest Geiger" is leaning on August. Refreshing the source is not in this scope.
  • "P/E on the FY estimate" mixes fiscal years across names. It uses the first fiscal year ending on or after today: FY Dec 2026 for most, FY Aug 2027 for MU (which is why MU reads 6.9x), FY Jan 2027 for WMT. The date beside the number says which; a fairer row would roll to the next twelve months.
  • Sessions are weekdays. Exchange holidays are not subtracted, so "sessions to the report" can be one high around a holiday.
  • Sigma events use the finished bars the chart API serves and the 60-session usual day from the heartbeat table; if the API's tail is thin for a name the count is low, and the screen says how many bars were checked.
  • RSI is the stand-in for the Geiger's own percentile. They are not the same thing; the round says so.
  • Tags are today's cohorts. The themes are sets of the current tags; when the market tree is remapped the theme lists need re-reading (they are four short lists in field.mjs and are meant to be edited).
  • The quotes are Friday's and the API labels them stale on a Saturday; the card prints the observation date.

Tests and evidence
