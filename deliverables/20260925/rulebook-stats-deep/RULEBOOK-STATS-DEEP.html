1 · Rulebook v3, chapter 1 — every absolute level becomes the name's own line

Rulebook v2 names four absolute levels: RSI 30 and 70, Williams %R −80 and −20, a distance to the 200-day (the package's ±10% reference lines, not the book's), and the board's two-usual-day move. Each gets three per-name forms here, and each form was tested the same way.

P, the percentile form. Today's reading is placed among the name's own earlier readings, over three years or all history. The line "below its own 10th percentile" is the value that 10% of its earlier days sat under.

Z, the standard-score form. Today's reading minus the name's own average, divided by its own spread. The line Z ≤ −1.28 is where a bell curve would put the 10th percentile. Drawn on a chart it is the "average minus k spreads" band, so the band version and the Z version are one rule, counted once.

σ, the sigma form, in the board's own unit. The price move itself measured in the name's usual days (the 60-session spread of daily moves the board already shows). For RSI the move is the 14-session return. For the 200-day it is the distance to the average scaled by √(200/3), which is how far a random walk usually strays from its own 200-day. The line is again −1.28.

Every line for a day is drawn only from the days before it. A day counts as "fired" when the reading is beyond the line. An episode is one run of firing days; a new one starts after five quiet sessions. Lines need 252 earlier readings. Windows are three years (756 sessions; about 1,095 days for Bitcoin, which trades every day) or all history. 362 names: 314 companies, 47 funds and BTCUSD. Missing for short history: CBRS (93 bars), MRSH (176 bars), SHAZ (157 bars).

Four readings from this table.

  • RSI. The own-percentile, Z and all-history lines all fire near 10%. The fixed 30 fires on 2.7% of company days and 1.6% of fund days. Over all history it is 2.9% and 2.5%: funds reach 30 less often, but "never" is not true over three years.
  • Williams %R. The fixed −80 fires on 19.2% of company days and −20 on 26.6% (32.7% for funds). These lines mark ordinary days, not extremes. The own-percentile line fixes that. The Z line overshoots, because %R piles up at its ends.
  • Distance to the 200-day. No form is well behaved. The fixed −10% fires on 9.9% of company days, but the middle half of names runs 3.8% to 21.3%, and funds only 0.6%. The three-year percentile fires 8.6%, ranging 4.5% to 15.6%, because three years of a bull market pull the line up. The all-history percentile fires 4.4% lately and 10.5% over all history. The σ form fires only 0.35% (1.6% over all history): the usual day widens during declines, which shrinks the scaled distance exactly when the price falls. This family measures trend, not stretch.
  • The daily move. Two usual days down fired on 2.66% of company days; the name's own 2.5th percentile fired 2.66%. The board's sigma is already a per-name line, and it stays. Funds fall two usual days more often than they rise two (median gap 0.71 points, against -0.14 for companies): indexes carry negative skew, single names do not.

How S7 slots in. The parallel Opus lane S7 is measuring entry triggers inside the pre-report window: own RSI percentile under 20/30/40, touches of the 20/50/100/200-day, drawdowns in usual days, prior horizontal levels, and a parallel channel's lower rail, alone and in pairs and triples. Its results enter the rulebook and the loop without duplication.

  1. Into rulebook v3 chapter 1 as "entry lines". Each trigger is a condition, stated in the per-name forms above: own percentile, usual days, usual pullback. It is never an absolute level.
  2. Into the loop as study E1's second half and D1–D3's report-window cells. The loop runs the same triggers on ordinary windows, so a trigger only counts if it beats its own state's ordinary baseline, S6b's rule.
  3. Acceptance. At least 200 trades in each half, the number of combinations tried on the page, and discover and confirm both shown. A combination that passes only one half is written down as dead.
  4. Into the morning read as one column on the run-up line: which of S7's surviving triggers is live today for each name reporting inside 20 sessions.

Rulebook v3, chapter 1 — the text, drafted

  1. V3-1.1 · No absolute line on a bounded dial. Every threshold on RSI, %R and any gauge state is PERCENTILE mode (U-3 made the default, no longer a toggle): the name's own three-year window, prior readings only, lines at the 10th and 90th percentile. The fixed Wilder 30/70 and Williams −80/−20 are printed as reference only and gate nothing.
  2. V3-1.2 · Lines are drawn per trend state. An RSI line is read from the days in the same state as today: above or below the 200-day until the S-4 trend verdict exists, then by X-2's regime. This is the I-6 range-shift law, cashed.
  3. V3-1.3 · The usual pullback is a named per-name object. Its depth in % and in usual days, and RSI and both averages at its lows, are recomputed nightly from finished bars. Today's drawdown is stated as a multiple of it ("× usual").
  4. V3-1.4 · Leader-relative reading. Wherever a pullback is cited, the leader rank (the 120-session return minus SPY's, ranked among companies) is cited beside it. Depth never appears without rank.
  5. V3-1.5 · The daily move stays in usual days. Two usual days is the board's line; the name's own 2.5th / 97.5th percentile is shown beside it.
  6. V3-1.6 · The 200-day distance is context. Its own all-history percentile is displayed and gates nothing.
  7. V3-1.7 · Confluence is a count of independent witnesses. RSI and %R count once until A5 clears them. Any combination claims only a lift over the same state's ordinary baseline, in both halves of history, with the number of combinations tried printed.
  8. V3-1.8 · Nothing here is an entry. Lines describe where a reading sits. Entries come from studies that beat their own baseline (S7, D1–D3), and each carries its verdict and its sample.

measured here

inputs
RSI(14), Williams %R(14), distance to the 200-day, the daily move in usual days; 362 names and BTCUSD; finished daily bars from the chart API.
history

B · Parallel channels

Alan's channel stack combines four published TradingView methods: Auto Parallel Channels (HTF) by TradeSymbiotic, Auto Parallel Channel by sohaibnori, a transparent APC geometry mirror, and sunnywilson93's Auto Channel Detector. Every one of them is pivots plus a parallel offset, so every one can be rebuilt from daily or hourly bars without TradingView. The one discipline that matters: a pivot is only known after its right-hand bars close (25 bars late for method B, 3 for method C), and a study that uses it earlier is cheating.

B1

Proposal: the operator inputs and the news tags

Both are additive tables, written as one migration with its rollback and not applied: supabase/migrations/20260927_operator_inputs_news_tags.sql. The coordinator applies it. Until then the morning read keeps its inputs on the page only.

operator_inputs is append-only. A new decision is a new row, so the history of what Alan believed, and when, is never overwritten. Its fields are the name, the state, the reason in Alan's words, set_at, and review_on. A kind field separates states, plans and change requests, and supersedes links a row to the one it replaces. The K1 table operator_preferences keeps one row per key, which erases history, so it cannot hold a dated regime.

news_event_tags adds one row per headline × ticker × tag. It holds the counterparty, the stance the headline itself states (not a model's tone), first_seen_utc, the first session that could trade on it, a story cluster id, who tagged it (a rule version or Alan), and whether Alan confirmed it.

The tagging pipeline is rules first and uses no paid model.

  1. Match. Headline and summary patterns per tag: customer_concentration, hyperscaler_deal_named_provider, capex_guidance, export_control, supply_constraint, guidance_change, index_change, other. The entity lists are our universe's tickers and a counterparty list: the hyperscalers MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META and ORCL, plus the named neoclouds.
  2. Cluster. The same tickers and tag within 48 hours, with similar titles, form one story.
  3. Clock. Before 9:30 ET trades the same session; after 16:00 trades the next.
  4. Confirm. The morning read shows yesterday's tagged events on leaders, and Alan confirms or rejects each one.
  5. Count. Study H4 reads confirmed rows only, and the page says how many were rejected.

The Loughran–McDonald headline lexicon already in the Hub scores tone. Tone is kept as a column, never as the tag.

Where each number comes from, and what could be wrong

  • Bars. The chart API's finished daily bars (GET /candles?tf=D&limit=6000, origin scintillahub.ai), split-adjusted as served, fetched once into a scratch folder on 27 September. The newest bar is Friday 2026-09-25. BTCUSD comes from FMP through the same API and trades every calendar day, so its RSI(14) spans 14 calendar days.
  • Live reads. Headless Chromium read the deployed allocation tool and Hub at 2026-09-27T15:55:46Z. The raw text and a screenshot of each sit in evidence/. No database was read directly, and no key was used by this session. The pages used their own public read path.
  • Survivors only. Every historical count uses today's 364 names. The laggard and breadth numbers are flattered the most.
  • Hindsight in the pullback bands. Lows are chosen after the fact. That is why the leader table uses live-knowable triggers instead.
  • Overlap and clustering. One selloff triggers many names at once; the "months" column shows how many distinct months the triggers come from. No intervals are computed here. That is the loop's job, with block bootstraps.
  • My thresholds. 2.5 usual days for a pullback, 250 sessions to give up, five quiet sessions between episodes, the top and bottom thirds for leaders and laggards, and the +200% / −75% splice guard. Each is stated, and each changes the counts.
  • The splice guard. A real one-day move of +200% would be cut. None was found on inspection of the 60 moves over +100% or under −60%, but the rule is blunt. Six histories were cut: BNY on 2026-05-21 (+1,263%), META on 2022-06-09 (+1,395%), MS on 2006-01-17 (+4,605%), SPCX on 2026-06-12 (+632%), WM on 2009-08-05 (+17,718%), BTCUSD on 2010-01-16 (+294%).
  • The σ form for the 200-day uses a random-walk scale. It under-fires badly. That is reported as a finding, not tuned away.
  • Cohort labels are today's taxonomy. The AI-power branch is mostly regulated utilities, which is why the AI core is shown separately.
  • The heat is read as served. Several of its sources were flagged stale or fallback at capture; the page lists them in line 1.
