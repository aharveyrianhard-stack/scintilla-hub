This round does those four things. The page is index.html (tabs for MU, GEV, NVDA, or any ticker).

What the page shows, top to bottom

  1. One control row. Outliers IN or OUT of the band; not-meaningful multiples set aside or kept; the fair value overlaps on the middle half or the whole low–high; and a note that clicking any peer dot takes that name out of every row.
  2. The header. Price and its date, market value, net debt; the comps fair value; the analysts' target band; how many rows sit on the favourable side of the peer median.
  3. Market-cap range. One bar on a log axis: the smallest peer, the median peer (named), the largest, and where the company sits ("4th largest of 22, 58% of the way along on the log axis").
  4. The fair value field in dollars per share. Each valuation row is a bar: the peers' multiples from lowest to highest, applied to the company's own figure. On the chart itself: the left end says "low 1.0x (TSM) → $45.85", the tick says "median 45.2x (AVGO) → $1,996", the right end says "high 87.0x (MRVL) → $3,845". The right column gives the middle half and the low–high in dollars. The fair-value row names which bar's end sets its left edge and which its right ("$1,565 ← EV/EBITDA at its 25th-percentile multiple 23.2x"), how many bars agree, and which bars sit outside. The analysts' target bar is drawn under it on the same axis: lowest, median, average, highest, with how many analysts and the date.
  5. Every bar end, worked. A table with the formula for all 25 ends (5 rows × low, 25th, median, 75th, high) so any dollar can be checked by hand.
  6. Every row of the comps table, as a field. Eight groups: size, valuation, growth, profitability, CapEx and results, balance sheet, analysts, timing. Every column of the round-1 table is here (P/E trailing and adjusted, P/E on the FY estimate, EPS and revenue growth next FY, revenue TTM, analyst target over price, market value, net debt, Geiger, usual day, days to the report), and every round-2 row (EV/sales, EV/EBITDA, PEG, TTM growth, the three margins, the five CapEx rows, net debt/EBITDA). Each row has its own axis with numbers and words at both ends ("← cheaper · dearer →"), the low and high peer named at the bar ends, the median named, the company's dot and value, outliers as hollow diamonds with their name, and a line underneath: who sets the ends and the median, which peers are outliers, and the band without them.
  7. Proposed, not built. Two short boxes: price targets into the allocation tool; this view aggregated to a cohort.
  8. Annex. The full table, every company on every row, ◇ on outliers and NM on multiples set aside, the peer median row, and where each number comes from with its measured date.

How the bars, the band and the outliers are worked

A bar. Take forward P/E. Line up every peer's multiple from lowest to highest. Multiply each by the company's own EPS estimate. The lowest peer's multiple gives the left end, the highest the right end, the median peer's the tick; the 25th and 75th percentiles (straight-line between the two nearest peers) bound the darker middle half. For EV rows the multiple is applied to revenue or EBITDA, net debt is taken off, and the rest is divided by the share count. For PEG: PEG × EPS growth (%) × EPS estimate.

The band. The stretch of price that the most bars cover at the same time, on their middle halves (or their whole low–high, on the control). Its left edge is the highest 25th-percentile price among the agreeing bars and its right edge the lowest 75th-percentile price — the page names both bars. When no two bars overlap the page says so and draws no band.

Outliers. Tukey's rule: a peer more than 1.5 × the middle half beyond the nearer edge of that middle half. This is a choice, not a fact, and the control row says so. With fewer than four peers nobody is called an outlier. Not-meaningful multiples (P/E over 100x and so on) are set aside before the rule runs, so a 3,296x P/E is NM, not an outlier. An outlier is always drawn (hollow diamond) and named; the OUT control drops it from every band, median and implied price.

The $ axis. A window from half the lowest of the price, the comps band and the analyst band to 2.5× the highest. A bar end beyond it is cut (‹ ›) and its number written at the edge. Without the window, one wild multiple (AXTI's 92x forward P/E on MU → $14,566) squashed every other bar into the left quarter — that was round 2's chart.

What could be wrong

  • The outlier rule is a convention. 1.5 × the middle half is the textbook fence; 3× would call fewer. It is stated on the page and the toggle shows both readings, but the number is mine.
  • The axis window is a convention too (0.5× to 2.5×); it is written on the chart and every cut end still carries its number.
  • The peer set decides everything. SEMICONDUCTORS holds 21 names from AXTI ($4.6B, 3,296x trailing P/E) to TSM ($78.9T). The tiny names set most of the outliers. The cohort chips and the click-to-knock-out are there; a size filter is not.
  • Dates are mixed, as before: price 25 Sep, fundamentals 18–26 Sep, the quarterly history to 2 Aug, the Geiger 24 Aug, balance sheets at each company's own date. Each is printed.
  • The Geiger date. composite_staged returned 24 Aug rows on this read for the semiconductor names; the row is drawn as the board reads it, and dated.
  • GEV's trailing P/E rests on two peers (VST and CEG; the rest have no positive trailing EPS), so its bar is two points wide; the row says "2 peers".
  • MU's consensus row has no analyst count, so the page prints the band without one.
  • Snapshot mode on this machine (no ?live=1) draws the committed 26 Sep run, which has no history and no low/high targets; those rows say so. Every screenshot below is the live read.

Proposed, not built

Price targets into the allocation tool. The analyst band gives one number per name: room to the median target, in %, dated, with the analyst count. Proposal for the tool's per-name sleeve, beside the condition read and the cap class: (1) a name priced above its highest target cannot enter a sleeve — greyed with the reason, like the earnings rule; (2) inside a sleeve, weight tilts by room to the median target, capped at ±25% of the sleeve's equal weight, so no single target swings the book; (3) the comps band is shown beside it and does not weight — two measures, two bars. The tool would read price_target_consensus exactly as this page does; nothing is stored.

This view, aggregated to a cohort. Run this read once per member with the same functions. The cohort's score on a row is how many members sit on the favourable side of the cohort median, named. The cohort's field is one row per member — its comps band, its analyst band, its price — on one $ axis indexed to price = 100, so the names line up and "picking between comps" is a glance. The outlier rule then names which members move the cohort medians. Cost: 22 reads of the same tables for semiconductors; no new data.

Decisions for Alan

  1. Outliers in or out by default? Recommendation: IN, always drawn and named, with the OUT control — taking them out silently is what round 2 was faulted for.
  2. The peer set for the semiconductor names. 21 peers from $4.6B to $78.9T; the small ones set most outliers. Recommendation: keep the cohort as the default and add a same-cap-class filter in the next round, rather than a hand-picked list.
  3. Should the analyst band enter the fair value? Recommendation: no — keep it as its own bar on the same axis, and take it into the allocation tool as proposed instead.
