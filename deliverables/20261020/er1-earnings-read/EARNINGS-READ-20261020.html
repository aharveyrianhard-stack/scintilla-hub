What the season says so far

Beats everywhere, selling almost everywhere: 21 of 30 reporters fell on reaction day while EPS beat FMP's estimate at 27 of them (PLD's beat is not like for like). The market is paying for demand it can see (data centres, munitions, steel) and selling anything the September rate shock or $111 oil can touch.

Demand

Strong wherever AI touches it: TSMC guides Q4 +58% y/y and 2026 to ~44%, ASML's memory tool sales are up 75% with customers short through 2028, Fastenal gets 4–5 points of growth from data centres, the data-centre supply chain is 17% of Prologis's US leasing, 3M's data-centre revenue is ~$700M and Amazon just took a warrant on it. Industrial demand is improving (US PMI ~55, 3M orders +7%, US and Canada +7%). Aerospace and defence backlogs are records (GE orders +49%, RTX $340B, NOC $110B) with a decade of munitions now contracted. Consumer-facing demand is the soft spot: Domino's basket shrinking, consumer electronics down mid-teens at 3M, TSMC calling price-sensitive segments 'challenged', JNJ's devices soft outside the US.

Pricing

Whoever owns the scarce thing is raising price: ASML's first-ever price increase, TSMC's 'strategic' pricing by node and location, Cleveland-Cliffs' index up ~$40/t a month with take-or-pay minimums, aerospace aftermarket price at GE and RTX. Whoever buys those inputs is absorbing it: GM's commodity, logistics and DRAM headwind is ~$2B this year and could be another ~$2B in 2027; 3M is passing $185M of oil inflation through. Insurance is the exception, with Marsh's index −5%, property −10% and Travelers cutting personal-auto rates. Diesel and logistics surcharges arrive within weeks (GM, Fastenal).

AI capex

The scale is now stated by the financiers: hyperscaler capex ~$1 trillion a year (Morgan Stanley), a US data-centre build that could need over $10 trillion through 2032 (Goldman), $7–9 trillion of US power, grid and shell (BlackRock). The money is flowing through bank windows (SK hynix's record equity raise, SoftBank's $11B high-yield deal, Meta's $14B Texas data centre, Aligned at ~$40B) and the warnings are unanimous and specific: Dimon (nobody is immune to a sentiment change), Solomon (a recalibration 'for sure', neo-clouds price differently from hyperscalers), Pick ('we are going to make mistakes'), Fink (bespoke deals cannot fund trillions; compute must be securitised). The build-out is already crowding out: RTX cannot get circuit boards and passives, Prologis's and Fastenal's customers compete for the same construction labour, and chips are supply-bound through 2028.

Credit

The cleanest numbers in years: BofA's criticised commercial assets at the lowest since 2019, Wells' early-stage delinquencies at historic lows, Synchrony's charge-offs down to 4.79%, US Bancorp's card FICOs improving, CRE office at 'the very tail end'. The cracks are at the edges and in the terms: Dimon says covenants got loose and is tightening, Goldman took idiosyncratic impairments, PNC's non-performers rose 8% on one credit, Simmons says risk spreads are being 'competed away' and wholesale funding is 30bp dearer. The rate move did the real damage through AOCI: it paused US Bancorp's buyback, cut CET1 at BofA, PNC and Wells, and tripled Travelers' unrealised loss to $6.4B.

Consumer

Spending is up everywhere it is measured (JPM debit and credit +10%, Wells +8% and +12%, BofA +5%, Synchrony +8%, GM's SAAR in the upper 16 millions) and paychecks are growing in every income band. Underneath: gasoline spend up ~20% is crowding out a little, baskets are shrinking (Domino's items per order, Synchrony's discount-store growth), Domino's and GM both say sentiment surveys have 'disconnected' from behaviour, and every bank repeats the same conditional: fine as long as employment holds. Holiday: Synchrony sees October tracking the Q3 exit rate and retailers planning earlier, heavier promotions.

Rates and the backdrop

The Fed hiked in September 2026 for the first time in three years; rates rose ~85bp in the quarter and the ten-year sits near 5% with real rates near 3%. Banks now write further hikes into guidance (BofA: December, March, June; PNC: December, March; JPM: one in December and two in the first half). Brent is ~$111 and the Middle East conflict (Synchrony's CFO called it 'a war in Iran') is a named drag at JNJ, Marsh, GM International and Domino's. IEEPA tariff refunds are flowing back (RTX $0.25 of EPS, GM $600–800M of cash). The reaction pattern: beat-and-raise on AI, defence or steel demand was bought (MMM, NOC, CLF, MRSH, BNY, USB); beats exposed to rates or oil were sold (GE, C, BAC, JNJ, ASML, PLD, SCHW).
