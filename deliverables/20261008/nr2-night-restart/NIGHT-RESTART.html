The 4 am restart

Every night, at the first quiet moment after 4 am, TradingView, the Hub and Brave are closed and opened again, one after the other. Then the Station and X are reloaded and X is reconnected, as planned on 7 Oct. In the morning one line says what happened, and three lines give each app's memory before and after.

Written 8 Oct 2026. Nothing is switched on, and nothing on the iMac was closed, opened or installed. The job is written and checked on a pretend Mac; the iMac itself was only looked at. It needs the same two things from you as the 7 Oct plan: one switch in System Settings, and about ten minutes with you watching, after the close.

What happens at 4 am

1 · 04:00
Is anybody here?
Nothing happens unless the keyboard and mouse have been untouched for ten minutes. If you are working, it tries again every ten minutes until 05:30, then gives up for the night. Unchanged from 7 Oct.
2
Can it do the whole job?
Screen unlocked, the permission on, the Station open, exactly one X window. X and the Station are brought to the front once, to prove they can be reached, before anything is closed.
3 · NEW
Write down the memory
How much TradingView, the Hub and Brave are each holding, and how much the whole Mac has pushed out to the disk.
4 · NEW
TradingView: close, open
It is asked to quit the ordinary way. When it is gone it is opened again, with the same start-up options it had. The job waits until it is back.
5 · NEW
The Hub: close, open
The same, for the app called SCINTILLA. It comes back on its first screen.
6 · NEW
Brave: close, open
Closing Brave closes the Station and X. The Station is opened again, then X - which starts Brave underneath, the way it starts when you open them by hand.
7
Reload, and reconnect X
The 7 Oct job, whole: reload the Station, reload X, Option + Shift + S in X, wait for the Station to report that X is working. One retry.
8
Write down the memory again
One line per app, before and after, in a small file and in the log. The app that was in front is put back in front.

It never forces an app to quit, and never touches Chrome. An app that will not close when asked - because it is asking you a question, for example - is left exactly as it is, and a line says so in the morning. An app that closes and does not come back gets one more try, then a line for you, and no other app is closed that night.

How each app comes back

TradingView

On the iMac it runs with two start-up options that Scintilla's chart tools and your own "TV Launch" icon rely on. A plain reopen would drop them. The job reads the options the running TradingView has and opens it again with exactly those.

Your charts and tabs are TradingView's to bring back, as they are whenever it is quit and opened. Your "TV Launch" icon already quits TradingView and starts it again when it has to, so being quit and reopened is nothing new for it on this Mac.

The Hub

It is its own app on the iMac (made from Safari). It is opened again from the same icon.

It comes back on its first screen. Lists and stars are kept (they live on the server), and so are the company tab and the compare tab. The room, its sub-tab and where each panel was scrolled are not: only the Hub's own night reload can carry those, and it cannot survive a quit.

Brave, the Station, X

Brave has no window of its own on the iMac. It runs in the background, started by the Station's icon; the Station and X are its two windows. Brave is not set to bring back its last pages, so opening Brave itself would give an empty window.

So the job opens the Station, then X, by name. Each comes back where Brave last had it on the screen. X comes back on its opening page; if that is not the page it was on, the morning lines say so.

All of this was read on the iMac on 8 Oct, looking only: the list of running apps, the start-up options of each, Brave's settings file, the two web-app icons. Which X page was open could not be read; the job checks the X window's name itself, before and after.

The test, with you watching

What you should see if it works

  1. About 20 seconds of nothing: it is making sure nobody is typing.
  2. X, then the Station, come to the front for a moment.
  3. TradingView closes, and a few seconds later opens again with the same charts.
  4. The Hub closes and opens again, on its first screen.
  5. The Station and X close together. The Station opens, then X.
  6. Both reload once more, and within about two minutes the X box in the Station fills again by itself. Nobody presses Option + Shift + S.
  7. A box shows the end of the record: "X confirmed working" and three lines of memory, before and after. Click OK.

Three to five minutes in all. Chrome, YouTube and everything else stay as they are.

If it does not work

  • Nothing happens, then "hands were never off". Somebody kept touching the Mac. Nothing was closed. Run it again.
  • A box says macOS did not let it look at windows. The permission switch is off. Nothing was closed.
  • An app closed and did not come back. The box names it. Open it from the Dock. No other app was closed after it.
  • An app did not close. The box names it. It may be asking a question; answer it. Nothing was forced.
  • The X box stays dark. Click the X window and press Option + Shift + S, as you do today.
  • TradingView without its charts, sound from the wrong speaker, X on another page. Say so. Each app's restart can be switched off by itself.

What could go wrong at 4 am

  • No real app has been closed by this job yet. That each of the three closes when asked and comes back with its windows is proved only by the test with you watching. Everything else was checked on a pretend Mac.
  • TradingView may ask a question instead of closing (unsaved changes to a layout). Then it stays as it is, nothing is lost, and you get a line.
  • X after the restart. If X is signed out, or opens on another page, the X box will show that. The job says when the X window's name changed.
  • A floating picture-in-picture window is gone after the restart. Only a click on the Station's pop-out button can open one, so the job cannot bring it back.
  • Sound. Your sound settings send Brave to the Bluetooth output and keep the Hub quiet on the iMac's own speaker. They go by app, and a restart does not change which app is which; that they are applied again by themselves was not tried.
  • An update that was waiting for TradingView or Brave is applied when the app starts again. Usually that is all you would want; once in a while an update changes something.
  • The screen saver. At night it is Brave's own X capture that keeps the iMac's screen awake. While Brave is closed the job holds the screen awake itself; without that the screen saver would start and swallow the shortcut.
  • The Station's X health id and two X windows: as on 7 Oct. On a night the job cannot reconnect X afterwards, it does not close Brave at all.
  • A slow night. An app that takes longer than a minute and a half to close is left alone; if it closes later after all, the job opens it again before it ends and tells you.

What I did not do

  • I did not close, open or install anything on the iMac, and did not switch anything on. The files exist only in the Hub's code, on a branch.
  • I did not read which page X is showing. Copying Brave's saved-windows file was refused by this Mac's safety rules, and I left it there.
  • I did not change the Hub, the Station, the extension or the window helper (the file that presses the menu and the shortcut is the 7 Oct one, byte for byte). The small app has one change: its box shows the end of a long record instead of all of it.
  • I did not run the Hub's whole test suite, for the reason given on 7 Oct. I ran this job's own 55 checks and the 7 Oct job's 23.
  • I did not add YouTube to the restart, though it held 1.14 GB: it was playing when I looked.

Three calls for you

  1. The Hub on its first screen every morning. A restart cannot keep the room you were in. Recommendation: accept it; the Hub's page held 1.37 GB after one day. One setting keeps the Hub out of the restart if you would rather keep your place.
  2. YouTube. It held 1.14 GB and is not restarted. Recommendation: leave it out for a week, read the morning lines, then decide. A restart stops whatever it is playing.
  3. When do we test? Recommendation: today after the close, about ten minutes at the iMac: the permission switch, a look-only run, then the real restart once.

Where the numbers come from

  • Memory per app: macOS's own list of every process and its memory, read once on the iMac at about 09:08 on 8 Oct, added up per app. A gigabyte here is 1,024 megabytes, as in Activity Monitor. "Asking for" is the figure in Activity Monitor's Memory column. An app's pieces are its main process, the helpers it started, and for Brave its two web-app icons; for the Hub, the page, network and picture helpers macOS lists under its name.
  • 6.8 GB pushed out to the disk, of 8.0 GB set aside for that: macOS's own count at 09:05. "29% of memory free" is the coordinator's reading at 08:58.
  • How Brave runs: macOS lists it as a background app started by "SCINTILLA Station" on 6 Oct at 14:06; its settings file has no "continue where you left off"; its last ordinary window was saved on 5 Oct.
  • TradingView's options: read from the running app. Your "TV Launch" icon starts it with the first of the two.

Its neighbours, kept as they are

  • The 7 Oct reload and X reconnect: unchanged, and it runs after the restart.
  • The Hub's own night reload (03:00 to 05:00): untouched. On a night the Hub app is restarted it has nothing left to do.
  • The iMac's weekly restart (12:30, when due): another hour. Its rule - nothing of yours is discarded or force-quit - is this job's rule too.
  • Your "TV Launch" icon, the sound-per-app settings, the picture-in-picture helper, the window manager, the lights: none is changed. What each means for the restart is in the list of what could go wrong.
