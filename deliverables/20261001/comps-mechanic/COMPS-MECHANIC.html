What was built in one paragraph

The comparable set is now one stated rule, the July method restored: candidates are the union of FMP's algorithmic peers, Massive's related companies, every served company in the same FMP industry (or SIC code), and the served holdings of the industry funds that hold the company; a candidate stays if it is in the same industry (FMP industry, SIC, or a shared industry fund) and its market value lies between ÷10 and ×10 of the company's (a visible control: ×3, ×10, ×30, any); the survivors are ranked industry-first then by size distance and the nearest 10 are kept (a control: 6 to 15). Every peer row says which sources named it, why it is in and its market value; the operator's on/off sits on top; the dropped names are listed with their reason. The cohorts leave the rule. The tab is one clean screen: the set, the range drawn as a small football field with the price as the one overlay, the A · B · C switch with C's weights in one line, the summary field (one bar per valuation measure on the dollar axis, the blend under them), then the field going down: 16 measures in five groups, valuation as price ranges, growth, margins, balance sheet and capex as positions among the peers; the full table closed below. No status chips, no repeated price, no "log axis", no "median" on every mark, plain numbers with negatives in parentheses, one cyan line for the company. On the 453 served companies the rule yields a set of five or more for 405, and the ways' centres differ by more than 5% for 282 of 409, so C weighted stays and C equal goes.

Part 2 · the tab, one clean screen what changed and why

  • Out: the "decisions saved" chip, the peer-count status lines, the "all low / centre / high" repeats, "log axis", "today $" and the price on every row, "median" on every mark (it appears twice on the page now: once in the field's header and once in the C line).
  • The conclusion is drawn: a small football field, low – centre – high with the numbers on the marks, the price as the single cyan overlay, the upside to the centre as the one big number, low and high upsides small beside it.
  • Plain numbers: no plus signs anywhere; negatives in parentheses: (24%), (0.2x).
  • All 16 measures on the field, going down, grouped (valuation · growth · margins · balance sheet · capex). Going down rather than sub-tabs because the whole picture is one scroll and nothing is hidden behind a click; sub-tabs would hide ten of the sixteen on every visit. Valuation rows give price ranges with one upside each; the other ten show where the company sits among its peers (the line from lowest to highest, the box their middle half, the company's number and its quarter: top quarter, upper half, lower half, bottom quarter).
  • One mark for the company: the cyan line, with its number above on the valuation rows. The ring is gone.
  • The summary field: one bar per valuation measure on the dollar axis (its middle half, its median tick) and the chosen way's band under them, the price line through all of it — how the range is built, seen.
  • The look: the board's tokens (panel, hairlines, mono, cyan for the company, green and red only for direction), 12px padding, three sections and one closed disclosure, the same order on every visit. Set and band changes re-read the figures and redraw in place.

A and B always have the same centre, by construction: both are the middle of the six implied medians; only their edges differ (A's are the lowest and highest median, B's the middle 25th and 75th). That is why they "looked similar". C weighted differs from C equal by more than 5% for almost half the names, so it earns its place; C equal is dropped from the switch. The switch is A · B · C, C being the weighted blend, and its weights are said in one line on the tab: for LRCX today P/E 12% · P/E forward 20% · EV/EBITDA 22% · EV/sales 19% · P/S 14% · PEG 13%, from a sector prior × the share of peers with a value × how tight the peers sit × how well the measure prices its own peers (the Liu–Nissim–Thomas pricing error). Where each number comes from

  • The rule: peers.mjs (pure; tests by hand and on the four fixtures). The reads: read.mjs: company_profile (industry, market value), fmp_peers and peer_sources / ticker_industry when they exist, the tree's fund nodes, the dated probe file.
  • Migration and job: supabase/migrations/20261001_peer_sources.sql (+ rollback) adds peer_sources (FMP and Massive peers with their position) and ticker_industry (FMP industry and sector, Massive SIC); scripts/peer-sources-sync.mjs runs on Fly with the FMP and Massive keys, uses /stable/stock-peers and /stable/profile (the legacy /api/v3 paths return nothing) and Massive's /v1/related-companies and /v3/reference/tickers, and prints JSON lines for the coordinator to load, as was done for the rates.
  • The figures, the ways, the currency conversion: the 1 Oct template's modules, unchanged; the C3 reader takes the kept list by name. TSM's TWD now converts at FMP's rate from fx_rates (5,347 rows loaded today).
  • The measurement: measure.mjs → measure-2026-10-01.json. Tests: tests/comps-mechanic.test.mjs (5).

What could be wrong

  • "Same industry" leans on FMP's industry label and the funds. MSFT's set is mostly fund-mates from XLK because FMP files only 19 served names as Software-Infrastructure and the band keeps the giants; at ×3 the set tightens to five. The SIC test will add precision once ticker_industry is loaded.
  • Massive's related companies are on hand for the four test names only (the probe file); the job fills them for all.
  • Peers not served on the Hub (FMP's DOCN, GDDY, RPD, SPSC for MSFT, SAP for MU, ASX and DIOD for TSM; Massive's MKSI and NVMI for LRCX) have no figures and are listed as dropped; admitting them is a data decision.
  • The band default of ×10 is a stated choice, not a fact; it is a control and the counts show what it does.
  • Ten thin names on the Hub get no set at all; the tab says so for them.

What was not done

  • Not deployed, not pushed. Migration, rollback and the printing job for the coordinator.
  • The screener as a live source (FMP's search-company-screener) is stood in by the served names of the same industry; a screener job would add unserved names, which have no figures anyway.
  • No visible browser; every run headless and closed afterwards.

Decisions for Alan at most three

  2. Unserved peers: admit the names FMP and Massive put forward for served companies (nine across the four tested) so the sets are not limited by what the Hub serves. Recommendation: yes, through the admission process.
  3. Apply peer_sources and run the printing job so Massive's related companies and the SIC codes feed the rule for every name. Recommendation: yes.
