The board's READ as one icon

What this page is: a proposal, in plain words, for turning the three board columns TREND · MOM · READ into one small icon that can be scanned down the board, with the detail (the two Geigers and the sentence) one click away, and the freed width given to the Geiger bar. Nothing live has changed. No edit to the Hub, the board, the Station or any shipped page. Every drawing below is drawn on this page only.

Alan, 1 Oct: "We have trend, momentum, read. It takes up a ton of space. We need to translate the read into some kind of icon… There is a candidate that could be a small Geiger ring, but I'm not sure that I love that… clicking on that expands more detail. It shows me the trend and momentum Geigers and the sentence read… that space gets used for more Geiger width."

The recommendation in one line: use the two chevrons (trend above, momentum below, each pointing up, down or flat, drawn in green and red) as the board's READ icon, with the twin Geiger bars as the runner-up. The chevrons are told apart fastest in a 20-row scan, they are the only candidate where the shape alone names the state, they use the least width (14 px), and they are the up/down vocabulary Alan approved in June. The Geiger ring reads worst at row size: at 16 px a clockwise arc and a counter-clockwise arc look the same until you check the colour, which is the one thing the rules say the shape must not depend on.

  • ♥ ★ ◎ (and ⊙). Live, approved, used daily: LIKED ♥, FAVORITES ★, RADAR ◎/⊙, in the first cell of every board row, in the header ident bar, and as a column in USUAL DAY. They are list membership marks, not read marks. Nothing here touches them.
  • The Geiger bar (.sc-gmini, line 1583): a 13 px tall bipolar bar, zero in the middle, fill to the right in green for a positive composite and to the left in red for a negative one, with a soft glow. Every board row has one; the cohort Geiger above the board is the same bar (MEAN, and MEDIAN · MAX · MIN when opened). Alan ruled on this shape: R40, "no ring — flat geiger lines, same as every other one on the board, and the scale is the fixed ±1". That ruling matters for candidate A.

2 · Every read state, its rule, and how often it occurs today

Trend is the slow, daily-locked measure; momentum is the fast one (the equalizer weights 6h, 12h and 1D). Both run from −1 to +1. The rule is tested top to bottom; the first line that matches wins. The counts are from a headless read of the live Hub this morning (1 Oct, about 08:00 ET, market closed): the board as it opens, on LIKED (128 names), and the whole universe the Hub tracks (546 names). ◆ counts the rows where the divergence mark would also show.

ReadCondition (trend T, momentum M)Colour todayIn wordsLIKED 128ALL 546

"trend only" (momentum missing) and "—" (no trend) are the two non-reads; neither occurred on LIKED this morning. The ◆ is not a state: it rides on top of the word (pullback and turning up always carry it; mixed, constructive and stalling sometimes do).

What stands out: on LIKED this morning, three states cover 92 of 128 rows (mixed 38, aligned bear 35, broken 19). The icon's first job is therefore to separate aligned from constructive / broken (same direction, weaker agreement) from mixed, and to make the rare, most informative states (pullback, turning up, mom leads) jump out.

3 · Five candidates, drawn at the board's real row size

Every candidate is drawn for every state at the size it would have on a board row (the row is 33 px tall and the text is 11 px, so the glyph box is 16 px) and at three times that size so the shape can be read here. The ◆ divergence mark stays as it is, beside the icon, in every candidate. Rules applied: dark panel, green up and red down, no white, no grey lines, the shape carries the state and the colour repeats it. Representative trend and momentum values are real rows from this morning's board where a state occurred; where it did not occur on LIKED, a row from ALL is used and named; where it occurred nowhere, the value is made up and marked.

A · Geiger ring (Alan's candidate)

Two independent arcs, both starting at twelve o'clock: the outer ring is trend, the inner is momentum. A positive value sweeps clockwise (to the right half), a negative one counter-clockwise (to the left half); the length is the size of the number, so ±1 is a half ring. Near zero is a dot at the top. Divergence shows as the two arcs on opposite sides.

B · Twin Geiger bars

The board's own bipolar bar, twice, stacked: trend on top, momentum below, zero notch in the middle, fill right in green and left in red. It is the exact primitive of the board's Geiger bar and the Station chip, so it needs no learning. Divergence shows as bars on opposite sides of the notch.

C · Two chevrons (recommended)

Trend above, momentum below. Each is a chevron pointing up (green) or down (red), or a flat dash when it says nothing. Heavy stroke for the strong case (aligned), light stroke for the weaker one (constructive, broken). The shape is the state itself: the icon is scinRead()'s word, drawn. From Spec 02's approved momentum chevron.

D · The 29 Sep arrows (built, switched off)

What ?board=v2 already draws: one Unicode arrow per word (⇈ aligned bull, ↗ constructive, → stalling, ⤵ pullback, ⤴ turning up, ↑ mom leads, ↔ mixed, ↘ broken, ⇊ aligned bear). Zero drawing work, but the glyphs come from whatever font the device has.

E · Compass dot

A small square: trend runs left to right, momentum bottom to top, and one dot sits where the row is. Top-right is aligned bull, bottom-left aligned bear, the off-diagonal corners are the divergences. One drawing covers every possible pair of numbers; the dot's colour is the quadrant.

6 · Where the same glyph could serve elsewhere

  • Station chip. The chip shows the composite only. The two chevrons fit before the number at the chip's own capital height, so a wall of eight charts also says, per chart, whether trend and momentum agree. Drawn below. The twin bars would instead turn the chip into three stacked bars, which is heavier on an 8-up wall (Alan, 28 Sep: "don't go too crazy on the 8-chart layouts").
  • Cohort compare strip. It already prints the word under each cohort column, cut to "construc-tive" on two lines. The icon replaces the word at a quarter of the height; the word stays in the title and on hover.
  • Comps cohort table (the COMPS tab's cohort view: TICKER · TODAY · GEIGER · ALL · LOW · CENTRE · HIGH). A READ column of icons between TODAY and GEIGER would show, at a glance, which peers are in a pullback while the cohort is up, which is what the tranche decision wants.
  • Tree map (LAYERS and CLUSTER views). A node already carries a Geiger colour; the chevron pair as a small corner stamp on cohort nodes would show agreement or divergence without another colour. In the 3D cluster view it should stay off: labels there are already the hard part.
  • USUAL DAY and the Pine Geiger table. Any row that has trend and momentum can carry it. The Pine script Alan sends to TradingView would draw it as two characters (∧∨) since Pine has no SVG.

7 · Where each thing on this page comes from, what could be wrong, what was not done

Sources

  • Rows, prices, Geiger, RSI, market cap, F P/E, trend and momentum: a headless Chromium read of https://scintillahub.ai this morning, saved as board-20261001.json next to this page (the script is in tools/live-dump.mjs; every write request was blocked and none was attempted). Trend and momentum are the board's own store (window.SCIN_TM), which the Hub fills from the chart API's candidate Geiger for equities and composite_staged for the rest.
  • The read words and counts: scinRead() copied verbatim from index.html line 7616 and run over that file by this page's script, so the counts in section 2 are recomputed live from the JSON when the page opens.
  • Row and column sizes: measured in the same headless run (row 33 px on v1, 39 px on v2, 41 px on the phone; the 14 column widths at 1680).
  • The old documents: read and screenshot headless from /Users/alanharvey/SCINTILLA 0.5 (the crops in section 1).

What could be wrong

  • The counts are one morning's snapshot of a closed market; the mix of states changes daily, and the LIKED list is Alan's, so it skews to names he already likes.
  • The mock rows are drawn by this page, not by the Hub. Column widths are the Hub's fr values, but the font metrics of the device showing this page can differ by a pixel or two from the Hub's.
  • Candidate C's heavy / light stroke distinction is small at 16 px on a non-Retina screen; if it does not read on Alan's screen, the light form can be drawn with the same stroke and a shorter span instead.
  • I ranked by my own reading of the strips in headless screenshots. Alan's eye on his screen is the test that counts.

Not done, on purpose

  • No code on the Hub, the Station, the board or any shipped page was changed. The ?board=v2 switch stays as it was (off).
  • No new tests, since nothing in the product changed; node --test tests/ was not run for the same reason.
  • No push, no deploy.

8 · Decisions for Alan (three)

  1. Which icon. Recommendation: C, the two chevrons. If you want the numbers themselves visible in the row rather than the state, B, the twin bars, is the one to pick instead; nothing else comes close at row size, and the ring should be let go.
  2. Does the icon replace the words, or the words stay in full screen? Recommendation: icon everywhere beside the left panel and on the phone; in full screen (⛶, 14 columns) keep TREND and MOM as numbers and show the icon in the READ column instead of the word, so the two views agree.
  3. Does the Station chip take the icon too? Recommendation: yes, the chevrons before the number, on the big charts only (not the 8-up wall), as a separate Station lane after the board ships.
