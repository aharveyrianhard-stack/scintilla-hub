  1. What goes in. Every name any of four sources offers for LRCX: FMP's peer list, Massive's related companies, every served name in the same industry (Semiconductors, by the authority), and every served holding of an industry fund that holds LRCX. 76 candidates; 74 of them served.
  2. What each ring removes. A name rises one ring for each source that names it, so the top ring is where all four agree. Then the cuts: a name outside LRCX's industry by the authority is dropped whatever its votes (33 here; a fund vote alone does not make a peer), a name outside the size band — more than ten times bigger or smaller than LRCX — is dropped (20 here, the dashed ring), and of the 21 that pass, only the nearest 10 in size stay, ranked by votes then closeness (11 beyond the nearest 10).
  3. What is left. 10 names, the bright dots: AMAT (4), KLAC (4), MU (4), ARM (3), INTC (3), TXN (3), QCOM (3), AMD (3), ASML (2), MRVL (2) — the number is how many of the four sources name it.
  4. Why. A comp set should be names that several independent sources agree are alike, in the same business, of a comparable size — not one provider's list, and not every name in a sector. The rings show where the sources agree and where they do not, and what the cuts take away.
